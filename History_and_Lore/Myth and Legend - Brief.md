# Of Ancient Days — Brief

There was a world, once. Wide and warm. A human Empire of marble and spell-light, its Noble Houses bound to the Throne by chains of magic old enough to have forgotten why they were forged. Dwarven traders from across a nameless sea. Elves who moved between the mortal world and the Faewilds as easily as crossing a threshold. Orcs whose war-bands the Empire employed and whose roads wove through everything. Halflings in their quiet Shires. And others, more numerous than can be counted now.

Then came the Apostasy.

No one knows the cause — only the word, which means betrayal. Whether of the gods, by the gods, or by something that wore their name. What remains is what it left behind: a sky dimmed to a permanent haze, as though the sun had been wounded and would not heal. Seasons that forgot their proper order. Magic turned catastrophic, and the spell-wars that followed reshaped entire landscapes, carving scars into the earth too vast to have been made by anything merely natural.

And through every crack, the Umbra crept in.

A living darkness. A dark miasma that settled into the lowest places first — into valleys and riverbeds and the basements of ruined cities — patient, reaching, and hungry in a way that did not sleep. What it touched, it unmade. Trees became black stone. Beasts lost their shape. The dead rose — cold, purposeful, and monstrous in a way that made the living shudder. The Faewilds sealed themselves, completely and instantly, as if a door had been slammed from the other side, and the Elves who had walked between worlds for millennia found themselves terribly, suddenly alone.

Civilizations withered. The great fell first. The small followed. Some of the desperate made bargains with Devils and Demons to survive, and the mark of those pacts passed into their blood: the Tieflings — grey-skinned and dark-horned, or red-fleshed and burning-eyed — cursed or blessed depending on who you asked.

There was no safe place.

And then there was one figure — whose name is lost, though his deed is not — who did the thing no leader of a dying world is supposed to do. He gathered them: Human and Elf, Dwarf and Halfling, Orc and Tiefling, the desperate and the lost and the ones who had simply run out of road. He led them through passes no one had crossed in living memory, through gorge and freezing dark and the reaching of the Umbra at their heels — into a valley the shadow could not follow.

A plateau, broad and sheltered, cradled in impassable peaks.

There, at the valley's heart, he gave himself entirely. Blood, body, soul — offered to a ritual no one else has ever been able to name or repeat. In his place rose the Beacon: a pillar of eternal light, warm where the sun had grown cold, alive where the world had learned to die.

We named the valley Brightvale. We built Medsen around the place where he knelt. An Academy on the cliffs of Aurore's Peak, where Aetherweavers learned to focus the Beacon's reach and push back the dark. Halfling fields in the warm southern valley. Dwarven Hvergi carved from the eastern peaks. Elven settlements near the ancient forest stones, where something in those old circles whispers of pathways not entirely sealed — and where, quietly, investigators have begun to document the rare, strange accounts of those who claim to have brushed against the Faewilds again. And Emberfort at the mountain gate, where the Venturers make their base and descend, again and again, into the ruined world below.

We called ourselves the Enduring.

The Noble Houses of the old Empire reformed in miniature — proud, hierarchical, perpetually arguing about precedence in a world too small for all their pride. And as the Guilds grew — the Caretakers who tend the Beacon's holy fire, the Aetherweavers split between old and new magic, the Slayers who hunt the great abominations in the Wastes, the Emberguard who keep the peace, the Explorers' Guild who map what lies beyond the Vale — real power drifted toward those who controlled what survival required. The Monarchy became ceremonial across the generations. The current King has not been seen publicly in years. The Council governs now, in chambers where Guild and House wage a careful contest that has not yet become an actual war.

The Beacon flickers. The instruments in the Academy measure it: something has changed in that undying fire, and no one can explain why. An earthquake has opened a vast scar in the Umbra-choked lowlands below, reaching depths no map has ever touched. From the expedition that returned, two things were agreed upon: the scar descends further than their lights could reach, and from somewhere deep within, something vast and old has been disturbed.

The answers are not within these walls. They never were.
