# The Brightblood War (293–297 AA)

*"We fought each other because we had forgotten what we were supposed to be fighting."*
— Senna Iyengar, *Testimony Before the Amber Court, 298 AA*

---

The **Brightblood War** is the defining trauma of Brightvale's middle history — a four-year civil war that fractured the Vale, devastated its population, nearly destroyed [[Emberfort]], and ended only when the consequences of the fighting invited something worse through the gates.

---

## Causes

For 116 years, **King Cynred the Bitter** ruled [[Medsen]] through calculated neglect — closing borders to survivors, refusing audiences, allowing institutions to atrophy. When he died in **284 AA**, his grandson **King Aldhelm** inherited a realm structurally intact and spiritually hollowed. Aldhelm's sweeping reform agenda — redistributing Crown resources, auditing tax ledgers, reviewing all land claims — was necessary and catastrophically implemented. He moved too fast, consulted no one, and antagonized everyone.

By **293 AA**, the political fracture had become irreparable.

---

## The Factions

### The Crown's Coalition — *The Griffins*

Named for the heraldic beast of the Sun-Born line — half-eagle, half-lion, guardian of sacred places. Pride: noble protectors of the divine order. Insult: creatures of myth clinging to a world that no longer needs them.

- **King Aldhelm and the Sun-Born Court**
- **[[House Kaewdyn]]** — Warrior house, [[Emberguard]]-integrated. Order over chaos.
- **[[House Lugner]]** — Conservative institutionalists whose power rested on existing hierarchies.
- **[[House Aurelian]]** — Idealists who revered the Dawnfather's divine bloodline.
- **[[Caretakers of the Beacon]]** — The Beacon was the monarchy's divine mandate made visible.
- **The [[Emberguard]]** — The Crown's military arm.
- **The [[Slayers]]** — Refused on principle to wage war against the living; some individuals fought for the Crown regardless.

### The Reform Coalition — *The Ravens*

Named by their enemies for the carrion birds that circle the dying. Adopted with defiance: ravens are clever, adaptive, and unafraid of the dark.

- **[[House Von Zoltraak]]** — Progressive intellectuals who viewed the monarchy as an obstacle to innovation.
- **[[House Iyengar]]** — Diplomats seeking formal recognition for non-human communities.
- **[[House Thalum]]** — Infrastructure lords who wanted political power proportional to their importance.
- **[[House Drevorn]]** — Merchants whose ambitions were permanently blocked by the monarchical structure.
- **The [[Dwarvenkind|Dwarves]] of [[Hvergi]]** — Saw an opportunity to formalize their autonomy. Contributed sappers and fortification expertise.
- **Elements of the [[Aetherweaver Academy]]** — Progressive faculty chafing under [[House Lugner|Lugner]]-backed research restrictions.

### Neutral
- **The [[Explorers' Guild]]** — Refused both sides; continued Waste operations throughout.
- **[[House Ashwood]]** — Retreated to their groves. Supplied food to both sides' wounded.
- **[[House Aerincorvus]]** — Remained in the Overgrown Palazzo conducting Fae research.
- **[[House Ravenna]]** — Officially neutral. Rumoured to have sold intelligence to the Reform side.

Both sides employed **Orcish war-clans** and **Elven glade-companies** as mercenaries. Some clans fought on opposite sides of the same engagement.

---

## The War

The Reform Coalition held the initial advantage — [[House Thalum]] controlled [[Medsen]]'s infrastructure, [[House Drevorn]] its supply lines, and the progressive Aetherweavers could contest the wards defending the upper tiers. The Crown held the Temple-Castle and the Emberguard's loyalty, but was surrounded in a city whose foundations answered to someone else. Urban combat in [[Medsen]]'s tiers was brutal and indiscriminate.

In **294 AA**, **Senna Iyengar** convened emergency peace talks at the Amber Court. For eleven days, a ceasefire framework was negotiated. On the twelfth, a loyalist envoy was assassinated within the Court's walls — shattering the talks and the Court's neutrality. Responsibility was never established. The war resumed with renewed savagery.

By **295 AA**, the fighting had disrupted the [[Caretakers of the Beacon|Caretakers']] rituals. The Beacon **flickered**. The wards thinned. And in the Wastes, something that had been waiting began to move.

---

## The Fall of Emberfort

**Lord-Warden Theron** had been the [[Emberguard]]'s most celebrated commander — a tactical genius credited with innovations in wall-defense that [[Emberfort]] still used. He had been Hollowed during a Waste expedition fifty years prior.

When the Beacon flickered, Theron's instincts activated. The thing he had become did not remember his name or his oath, but it remembered **how to take a fortress**. Through pure tactical reflex, the Fallen General organized a massed Horde and led them at [[Emberfort]], whose garrison had been stripped by the civil war. A skeleton crew of reserves held for six days. On the seventh, the outer walls fell. By the ninth, [[Emberfort]] was burning.

---

## The Battle of Three Bridges

The war stopped — not through negotiation, but through the sudden understanding that if the Horde reached the plateau, there would be nothing left to fight over.

**Three Bridges** is a small settlement at the river fork where the Ashwater meets the Coldrun, roughly thirty kilometres southeast of [[Medsen]] — the first settlement into Brightvale when travelling from [[Emberfort]] along the Venturer's Trail. Suns and Ravens alike marched south and formed a combined line along the river. **King Aldhelm** rode at the head of the column.

The Sun-Born radiance channelled through him as he advanced into the Horde's front ranks — a searing golden light that burned the [[Umbra]]-touched on contact and disrupted the Fallen General's hold over the lesser creatures. The Horde broke. The combined force drove them back into the Wastes.

King Aldhelm collapsed on the field and died three days later in the Dawnfather Temple. The divine blood that had burned through the Horde had burned through him as well.

---

## The Pardoning & The Golden Reprieve (297–~308 AA)

**Queen Wendreda** — Aldhelm's consort — was crowned in the same temple where her husband's body had not yet cooled.

**Mercy:** Wendreda pardoned the Reform Coalition's lesser members — the foot soldiers, the junior scholars, the guild workers who had followed their leaders into a war they had not chosen. **Severity:** The most dangerous rebel leaders — those whose actions had contributed to the Beacon's flickering or committed the worst atrocities — were seized and **sacrificed to the Beacon**. Among them was **Drago Von Zoltraak**, the Reform Coalition's most brilliant strategist. The fire burned bright and steady for the first time in years.

The war and the Horde had devastated Brightvale's population. The bulk of the Venturing force was slain. But fewer mouths meant no food shortage, and the strengthened Beacon fueled a renaissance in aetheric research. Wendreda's reconstruction produced lasting innovations:

- **Ashlines:** Paint mixed with Beacon ash, applied to walls and fortifications. The treated surfaces generate a passive ward against the [[Umbra]]. [[Emberfort]]'s rebuilt walls were the first to receive them.
- **Lumen Censers:** Portable vessels of distilled Beacon light, carried by Venturers into the Wastes for limited but life-saving protection against [[Umbra]] corruption.
- **The Warding Expansion (300 AA):** High-Aetherweaver Brynstan doubled [[Medsen]]'s safe living zone.

## The Fallen and the Remembered

### King Aldhelm — *The Unyielding*
His reforms were correct in substance and disastrous in execution. He died proving the one thing no one had doubted — that the Sun-Born blood was real — in defense of a realm that had spent four years trying to depose him.

### Lord-Warden Theron — *The Fallen General*
Once the most respected commander in [[Emberguard]] history. Led a Horde against the fortress he had spent his life defending, using tactics he had invented against walls he had helped design. There is a memorial stone at the rebuilt [[Emberfort]]. It does not mention what he became.

### Drago Von Zoltraak — *The Architect*
The Reform Coalition's strategist. Called for ceasefire when the Beacon flickered, led Von Zoltraak forces south to Three Bridges. Wendreda sacrificed him anyway — not for war crimes, but because his capacity to organize made him too dangerous. The [[House Von Zoltraak|Von Zoltraak]] have not forgotten. A portrait of Drago hangs in the Crucible's central hall, facing the door.

### Senna Iyengar — *The Peacemaker Who Failed*
Spent eighteen months trying to prevent the war. When diplomacy failed, she fought for the Reform side. Pardoned by Wendreda. Spent the rest of her life recording testimony from survivors. Her journal's final entry: *"I have spoken to everyone who would speak. None of them believe they were wrong. I no longer believe I was right."*

### Ser Emeric Kaewdyn — *The Warden Who Heard It Coming*
A nineteen-year-old Ashen-Heir whose draconic sensitivity warned him of the Horde hours before any scout report. The loyalist command dismissed him. He organized an unauthorized civilian evacuation, then died at Three Bridges in the front rank beside his King. The [[House Kaewdyn|Kaewdyn]] training yard is named for him.

---

## Legacy

The monarchy survived, but the precedent that it could be challenged made the later formation of the [[Order in the Vale|Council of Seven]] (**357 AA**) inevitable. The Reform Coalition's demands were not granted by Wendreda. They were enacted, slowly and imperfectly, over the following century.

The Beacon's flickering established a truth: it responds to the state of the civilization it protects. Discord weakens it. Unity strengthens it. And in the rebuilt walls of [[Emberfort]], painted in Ashline wards, the Enduring wrote one rule:

**Never again.**

The names outlasted the war. A century later, families in [[Medsen]] still know which side their ancestors stood on. *"Your grandmother was a Raven. Mine was a Sun."* It is said casually — over drink, over cards, over disputes about nothing — but it is never said without weight. The war is over. The sides are not.
