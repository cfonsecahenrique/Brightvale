# [[Humankind]]

### [[Humankind|Humans]] (Heirs of the @Armisian Empire)

The most numerous and hierarchical. [[Humankind|Humans]] obsess over the **Noble Houses** and the lost glory of the **Armisian Empire**. Nobles maintain a feudal system of fiefs and palazzos, though their short lifespans drives the less blood-obsessed ones toward the "Mathematical Arcanum" to achieve in decades what [[Elves (Faefolk)|Elves]] do in centuries.

## Human Magic — Two Traditions

### Old Magic — Unstructured Tradition
Before Elven scholarship reached Brightvale's mages, human magic operated on no framework at all — only **belief**. A human who needed fire had no theory of combustion. They simply needed fire with enough conviction that reality provided it. Faith moved flame. Grief split stone. The desperate certainty that something *must* happen made it real.

This unstructured magic produced effects that the formulaic tradition still cannot fully explain or replicate. The Dawnfather's creation of the Beacon is the supreme example: a feat so far outside any known arcane framework that scholars who try to reverse-engineer it consistently reach dead ends. It may only have been possible precisely because he did not stop to consider whether it could be done.

Elven scholars refer to this approach, with a mixture of condescension and genuine awe, as *the wisdom of humans*. The **Old Magic** faction at the [[Aetherweaver Academy]] considers its recovery the most important arcane project in the Vale.

### Modern Human Magic — Derived Formulaic Tradition
The mathematical approach humans call the **Arcanum** is, at its root, borrowed from Elven principles. As Elven scholars shared their formulaic methods after the Apostasy, human mages adapted them — trading the raw power of pure instinct for the precision and teachability of structured theory. The short lifespans that drive humans to achieve in decades what Elves accomplish in centuries made the Arcanum's efficiency extremely appealing.

Modern human mages are formulaic practitioners, and the best of them rival Elven masters. But they inherit the tradition's central hazard: **Arcane Locks**, the self-imposed logical cages that perfect reasoning can build. Many of the Vale's most talented young mages plateau abruptly, not from lack of power, but from frameworks they constructed themselves.
