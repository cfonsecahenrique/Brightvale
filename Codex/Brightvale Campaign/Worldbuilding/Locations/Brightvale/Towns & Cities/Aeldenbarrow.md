# [[Aeldenbarrow]]

Most **[[Elves (Faefolk)|Elves]]** reside in secluded settlements, sequestered within hidden glades and stone druidic circles. Constructed from living timber and carved earth mounds, these communities center around megaliths arranged in perfect circles—sacred geometries believed to be potential gates to the lost **Faewilds**. Here, they seek to restore the connection severed by the [[Apostasy]] through communal meditation and natural alignment. Protected by shifting illusions and ancient glamours, these sites remain notoriously difficult for outsiders to find, appearing as nothing more than untouched forest to the untrained eye; one cannot hope to find their way into these sanctuaries without being guided by a resident. While primarily inhabited by [[Elves (Faefolk)|Elves]], these glades are also home to [[Halflings]] and occasional members of other races who are intrinsically connected to natural magic, the [[Fae]], or the primal rhythms of nature.

![[Pasted image 20260420135409.png]]
Aeldenbarrow is also the physical home of **[[The Concordance]]** — the vast corpora of folk magic, druidic primeval lore, and Fae-contact documentation maintained by [[House Aerincorvus]]. The settlement's age, its Elven residents' long memories, and its proximity to documented Primeval Aether sites make it uniquely suited as a repository. Most of the Vale does not know the Concordance is housed here; those who do tend to dismiss its significance.

## The Glade Itself

![[aeldenbarrow map.webp]]
> *Aeldenbarrow, pop. 650 — seen by moonlight, which is the only way anyone has ever mapped it.*

Aeldenbarrow sits in a **shallow forest hollow**, ringed so completely by old woodland that from any direction but directly above there is nothing to see. Within the hollow, the sacred geometry is not a monument standing apart from the village — **the settlement *is* the circle**. Two nested arcs of long, steep-roofed houses curve around an open bowl of ground, their gable-ends all turned inward, and the lanes between them run as arcs and radii rather than as streets. At the southern focus of that figure lies a **plain round green**, empty but for **a single standing stone at its exact centre**. Everything else in Aeldenbarrow is arranged with reference to that stone.

Four lanes leave the ring — north, east, southwest, and south — and each of them **thins, wanders, and simply stops** a few hundred paces into the trees. They are not roads. They are the paths the glamours permit, and they do not go anywhere for anyone who was not brought in along them.

There is **no field, no mill, no wall, and no gate**. Nothing here is cleared for crops; the hollow's floor is close-cropped turf and the forest begins where the last doorstep ends. The only light is the warm scatter of **lantern-glow at the lane junctions**, which in a settlement of six hundred and fifty is startlingly little — Aeldenbarrow is a dark place at night on purpose, and visitors are advised that the dark is the point.
