# Beyond the Sealed Door

**Quest ID:** q_beyond_the_sealed_door
**Type:** Dungeon delve / revelation (exploration, combat, mystery)
**Difficulty:** Hard (suggested levels 6–8; 4–5 sessions)
**Destination:** The Crypts of King Zothu — the sealed lower complex
**Posted by:** Explorers' Guild task board, Council-sanctioned; priority commission

> GM-facing brief. The first expedition mapped the upper complex and chose not to open the sealed door. This expedition opens it. Behind it is the truth of who Zothu actually was — not a king in the conventional sense, but a pre-Apostasy figure whose relationship to the Apostasy is the real prize. Do not resolve the question completely; leave the deepest layer for higher-tier play. What the player takes home is a fact that recolors everything prior.

## Premise

The sealed door at the bottom of Zothu's crypt has been on the Guild's list since the first expedition returned. A follow-up has been delayed by resource constraints, competing priorities, and — if the player reads the administrative record carefully — a notable reluctance from the senior Caretakers, who filed an objection to the priority commission on grounds they described as "unspecified spiritual risk." The objection was overruled. The commission stands.

The player's party is the follow-up expedition. They have the first expedition's maps, the Explorers' Guild's backing, and whatever they learned from *The Noble House's Claim* if that arc was completed.

## What Is Behind the Door

A second complex, older than the crypt above it, built by hands that were not building a tomb. Its original purpose was a archive: a pre-Apostasy repository of records, preserved in a medium that has survived the Apostasy in fragmentary form. Zothu was not a king. He was a Keeper — a scholar-priest of the pre-Apostasy period who understood, to some degree, what was coming and used this complex to preserve what he could.

The records are damaged. What survives is partial, contradictory, and in some cases physically dangerous to read directly. What they collectively describe is an account of the Apostasy's early phase — told from inside it, by someone who watched the Gods depart and knew what would follow.

**The deepest available truth at this tier:** the Apostasy was not an accident or a natural cycle. Someone opened a door that should have stayed shut. Zothu knew who. His records do not name them in full.

## The Crew

- **Kaze "Kita" Deep-Delver** — if the player established a relationship in the first expedition, Kita returns as an indispensable specialist. She knows this complex as well as anyone alive.
- **Ysa Vael** — the Aetherweaver from the first expedition; requested specifically because the lower complex's arcana require her particular skills. She was also affected by the first visit in a way she has been processing for months.
- **A Caretaker of the Beacon** — assigned by the Order over the Guild's preference. She has a copy of the objection her superiors filed and will not explain its contents.

## Arc Structure

**Commission → The Descent → The Archive → The Revelation → Return.**

### Session 1 — Commission & The Descent

The Guild briefing includes the first expedition's maps. The descent through the upper complex is mapped and known; the player moves through it efficiently, noting what has changed since the first visit (some things have changed — things that should not move have moved).

The sealed door: a meter of stone, Apostasy-era warding, a locking mechanism the first expedition assessed and didn't trust. Kita and Ysa assess it together. Opening it requires time and will cost something — a ward, a Weave expenditure, something the player has to give.

What the Caretaker does when the door opens is her most honest moment in the arc. Watch her face.

### Session 2 — The Archive's Outer Chambers

The lower complex is older and stranger. The architecture belongs to a tradition the Aetherweaver Academy does not have in any catalog. The air is preserved — not stale, but still, as if time here has moved at a different rate.

The outer chambers hold lesser records: logistics, names, inventories of what was brought here. They confirm that Zothu was operating this complex as a deliberate preservation project with a known deadline — he was storing things before a specific date, and that date was the Apostasy. He knew it was coming.

Combat: the lower complex has its own guardians — constructs, not undead, built to test intent rather than kill intruders. They respond to aggression and to specific approaches to the records. Kita knows how to read them; Ysa can help disable them if necessary.

### Session 3 — The Core Records

The deepest accessible chamber: a circular room with preserved record-vessels arranged in a sequence that Ysa reads as chronological. The first vessels are historical — accounts of the pre-Apostasy world, the Gods' active involvement in the death-cycle, the state of the Beacon's predecessor. The later vessels are first-person. Zothu, recording what he is seeing as the Apostasy unfolds.

**The Revelation:** the Apostasy did not begin with divine departure. The divine departure was a response to something. Zothu describes a ritual performed — not by him, not by any single faction — that breached the boundary the Gods maintained. The Gods did not abandon the world; they were driven from it. Zothu names the faction that performed the ritual with three words in a language the Caretaker recognizes and immediately attempts to confiscate the record.

The player must decide what happens next.

### Session 4 — The Confrontation & Return

The Caretaker's response reveals the Order's awareness of this information. Whether the Order has known all along, or whether the senior Caretakers filed the spiritual-risk objection because they suspected this was here, becomes clear based on how the player reads her. She is not a villain; she is someone whose institution has been built on a story that may not be the full truth, and she has just watched that story become complicated.

The player takes what they take and returns to Medsen. The Guild receives their report. The Caretakers receive theirs, separately.

What the player knows now: the Apostasy was done. The Gods were expelled. The faction responsible is named in three words that someone in the Order already knew. The records' deepest layer — the sealed vessel at the room's center, which Ysa identified but the player did not have time to open — contains something Zothu kept back even from this archive.

That is a thread for *The Dimming Beacon*.

## Combat

Moderate and thematic: the constructs in the outer chambers (level-appropriate dungeon encounters), one significant confrontation in the core chamber with a guardian whose purpose is specifically to prevent the records from leaving, and potentially a confrontation with the Caretaker's escort if the player attempts to withhold the records.

## Complications

- Ysa Vael's prior exposure to the upper complex's arcana has left her more sensitive to the lower complex than anyone anticipated. She begins reading the records directly — not through the vessels — and what she sees is affecting her.
- The sealed vessel at the core chamber's center responds to the player's presence in a way the other records do not. It is the one Zothu sealed last.
- A Noble House agent (Lugner, or Drevorn, or someone new) has been tracking the expedition and is waiting at the crypt's entrance when the player returns.

## Outcomes & Rewards

- **Full record recovered:** the three-word faction name, Zothu's account of the early Apostasy, and the knowledge that the sealed vessel exists. Political and religious crisis potential.
- **Records destroyed or withheld:** the Caretakers' institutional position holds; the player carries the knowledge alone; the Guild's commission is not fully satisfied.
- **Partial recovery:** some records safely extracted; the Caretaker has copies; the player has copies; the political contest over the information begins.
- Significant Guild pay; access to the records as worldbuilding artifacts; Ysa Vael as a changed contact.
