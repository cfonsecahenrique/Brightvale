# The Undercity

**Quest ID:** q_undercity
**Type:** Dungeon delve / expedition (exploration, mystery, limited combat)
**Difficulty:** Hard (suggested levels 7–10; 5–6 sessions)
**Destination:** The Undercity, accessed via a new entrance discovered in the northern Wastes
**Posted by:** Explorers' Guild priority commission, Council-sanctioned

> GM-facing brief. The Undercity is a massive underground complex that pre-dates the Apostasy — possibly pre-dates the Armisian Empire entirely. The question the Guild is asking is archaeological; the question the players will end up asking is something else. The Undercity was not abandoned. It was sealed from the inside. Pace the revelation slowly and let the player's imagination work before you confirm anything.

## Premise

Explorers have located a new entrance to a massive underground complex beneath the northern plateau — larger, by all estimates, than anything previously mapped below the Vale. The Guild's brief is straightforward: descend, map, assess, and return. The political context is more complicated: the senior Caretakers have assigned an observer to the expedition without explanation, and the Aetherweaver Academy's archaeology division has submitted competing bid documents that the Council has not yet adjudicated. Someone knows, or suspects, that this is significant.

What is down there is not a ruin in the conventional sense.

## What the Undercity Is

A pre-Apostasy city — not small, not peripheral, not a waystation. A population center, built underground deliberately, by people who had reasons to be underground that were architectural rather than circumstantial. The construction is older than the Armisian Empire by at least two centuries and may be older than that.

**The deepest truth (GM-only):** the Undercity was built by survivors of a prior collapse — the cycle referenced in the Apostasy archives, the civilization before this one that also ended in cataclysm. They built underground because they had learned what happens to surface civilizations. They sealed themselves in to wait out the cycle. Most of them did not survive the wait. What remains is their record, their infrastructure, and in the deepest chambers, something they built to survive the next cycle — something they intended the next civilization to find.

## The Expedition

- **Senior Explorer Marten Voss** — the Guild's lead, focused on mapping and recovery of portable artifacts. Practical, respectful of the player's judgment. He has led six major expeditions; this is the first that has made him nervous.
- **Academy Archaeologist Deva** — the Academy's observer; brings scholarly context that proves indispensable and a political agenda she is open about: the Academy wants priority research access. She is easier to work with once the agenda is acknowledged.
- **The Caretaker Observer** — sent without explanation by the senior Order. He does not explain why he is here. He will not examine any record that predates the Beacon's construction. He will explain this as "procedural." It is not procedural.

## Arc Structure

**Commission → Descent → The Outer City → The Inhabited Depths → The Deep Chambers → Return.**

### Session 1 — Commission & Descent

The Guild briefing is thorough: maps of the entrance, estimates of scale, existing surveys of analogous underground formations. The entrance itself is a worked passage — carved by tools, not formed by geology — that descends at a controlled grade for nearly a kilometer before opening into the first level.

The outer city: intact architecture, preserved by conditions the player cannot immediately explain (the air is dry and still in a way that is not natural). Voss and Deva begin cataloguing. The Caretaker walks slowly and says nothing.

### Session 2 — The Outer City

Three to four days of survey. The outer city is visually stunning and analytically confusing: its design solves engineering problems that the surface world does not have, suggesting the builders understood something about the surface that made them prefer to be underground. The inscriptions are in a language Deva partially recognizes — a proto-form of the script the Apostasy-era records used, which means these people existed before the Armisian Empire and after the previous cycle.

**The question that emerges:** if this city was built deliberately, with this level of engineering, by people who knew what they were doing — why was it sealed from the inside?

Evidence begins to accumulate: the outer gates were closed by the inhabitants, not by outside force. The food stores were consumed, not abandoned. The people who lived here waited for something and eventually stopped waiting.

### Session 3 — The Inhabited Depths

The middle levels were not abandoned in the same way. Evidence of habitation continues further down than any surface-city equivalent — generational habitation, not a temporary refuge. Children were born and grew old here. Something sustained them.

**The something is still running.** An infrastructure system — water, air circulation, a form of preserved light — operates in the middle levels without any visible power source. Deva cannot explain it. The Caretaker can explain it and does not.

**The first inhabitants the player meets:** not undead, not hostile. A small community of Undercity descendants, three or four generations removed from the builders, who have lived in the middle levels their entire lives. They speak a language Deva can partially translate. They are not surprised to see the surface expedition. They were told someone would come eventually.

### Session 4 — The Deep Chambers

The Undercity descendants lead the player's expedition to the deep chambers — not all the way, but to the boundary they themselves do not cross. Below the boundary: the builders' most deliberate construction, sealed by methods the descendants cannot break and will not try.

The Caretaker attempts to end the expedition here. His reasons, delivered under pressure, are revealing: the senior Order has maps of the Undercity — maps made before the Apostasy — and what is in the deep chambers is connected to the Beacon's predecessor. He was sent to ensure the expedition did not reach it. He was not told what to say if that attempt failed.

The player must decide whether to descend.

### Session 5 — Below the Boundary (if the player descends)

The deep chambers: a single large room containing a mechanism the builders constructed to survive a cycle's end. It is not a weapon. It is not a shelter. It is a record — a compressed account of what the previous civilization learned about why cycles end, in a format designed to be understood by whoever came next. It is addressed, in the oldest written language Deva has seen, to *the people who find this after the next darkness*.

Reading it requires time the player's Weave-reserve may not support. What it contains is a partial answer to the question *why do the Ages end* — not a complete answer, but enough to know the current Age's end is not the Apostasy. The Apostasy was survivable. What the deep chambers describe is not.

The Caretaker, if he is still present, reads it silently and does not speak again on the return journey.

### Return

Back to the surface. The descendants stay in the middle levels. Voss has a full outer-city map. Deva has two weeks of notes. The player has whatever they carried out of the deep chambers, and whatever they decided to leave behind.

## Combat

Not the primary mode: the outer city is cleared; the middle levels are the Undercity community's home, and combat there is a political disaster. The deep chambers have one guardian — not hostile, but testing — whose function is to verify that the visitors have earned what they are about to read. Calibrate to levels 7–10.

## Complications

- The Undercity community's leaders ask the player for something before they will lead them to the deep chambers: formal recognition from Medsen's Council as a people, not a curiosity. The Caretaker will oppose this.
- Deva's notes are the Academy's property. She will share them with the player if the player shares with her; she will not protect them from the Academy if the Academy asks.
- Something from the deep chambers follows the player out. Not aggressively — it is curious. It has not seen a surface-dweller before.

## Outcomes & Rewards

- **Full descent:** the deep chambers' account in hand; the Caretaker's complicity in the concealment documented; the question of what the current Age is actually ending into newly open.
- **Stopped at the boundary:** partial information; the Undercity community's trust; the deep chambers unread.
- **Undercity community recognized:** a named ally beneath the plateau; political credit with the Council's progressive faction; the senior Caretakers' displeasure.
- Significant Guild pay; Academy research access if Deva's cooperation was maintained; the deep chambers' record as a worldbuilding artifact feeding *The Dimming Beacon*.
