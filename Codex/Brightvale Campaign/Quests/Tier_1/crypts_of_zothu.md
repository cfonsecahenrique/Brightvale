# The Crypts of King Zothu

**Quest ID:** q_crypts_of_zothu
**Type:** Dungeon delve (explore / map / clear)
**Difficulty:** Hard (suggested level 3–5; a multi-session delve)
**Destination:** The Crypts of King Zothu, deep in the Umbra-choked lowlands
**Posted by:** Explorers' Guild task board, the Venturer's Hall, Emberfort — Council-sanctioned

> GM-facing brief. Read when the player signs on. This is the marquee expedition: a real dungeon to be paced over several sessions. Reward boldness, punish recklessness, and guard the deep mysteries — hint, never explain. The crypt should feel older and stranger than anything the player has met.

## Premise

A major pre-Apostasy crypt complex, long sighted in the lowlands but never entered, was finally **reached and named by the previous expedition** — who came back with partial maps, a few relics, and the conviction that it was worth a proper effort. (Older Guild records note a "King Zothu" crypt first sighted generations ago; whether the previous expedition rediscovered that same site, or merely borrowed a half-remembered name, is unresolved — and may matter.) The task: **explore it, map it accurately, and clear what can be cleared.** If it can be secured, it may anchor a new Checkpoint deeper into the Wastes than any before — which is why the Council signed off and the Guild is willing to spend Venturers on it.

## The Crew

The largest, most professional expedition of the three. The player is a junior member.

- **A senior Explorer officer** — expedition commander; cautious, by-the-book, carries the previous expedition's maps.
- **Kaze "Kita" Deep-Delver** — Explorer specialist in below-grade ruins; the one who actually reads the crypt. Calm underground in a way that unnerves the others.
- **Slayer muscle** — one or two, for the things that don't stay dead.
- **Ysa Vael** — Aetherweaver, tapped off her Emberfort flux-work to read the crypt's arcana (and privately fascinated by it).
- *(Optional)* a Caretaker for Rites and ward-craft.

## Structure

A long, dangerous descent, then a keyed dungeon built on the **5-room dungeon** skeleton (see `srd_search('five-room dungeon')`). Hold to **Explore → Map → Complete Task → Return → Report** — accurate mapping is half the contract and feeds all future expeditions. The five rooms are a skeleton, not a script: scale the levels to how hard the table is pushing, reorder or expand the middle, and let player choice reshape the path. Never railroad.

**Getting there — the long descent (frame, not a room):** days deeper than Ember 7, beyond any Checkpoint. Attrition: rations, ward-reagents, nerves. The crew establishes a forward camp at the crypt mouth, then finds the previous expedition's markers, a cached note or a name carved in haste, and their last mapped point. Past it, the map is blank.

### Room 1 — Entrance & Guardian
The **entrance hall**: Armisian-era grandeur gone to ruin, defaced iconography, and the crypt's first defenses. A sealed or trapped threshold (collapsed mechanism, ward-glyph, or a bound guardian-construct) that announces the easy world has ended. Reading the previous expedition's markers helps the crew get past without paying full price.

### Room 2 — Puzzle / Roleplay Challenge
**The King's gallery**: imagery of the entombed "King Zothu" laid out as a sequence to be read, a mechanism to align, or wards to satisfy in the right order — a problem you cannot fight through. Crucially, something here **doesn't match the Guild's assumptions** about who Zothu was; a player who studies it instead of rushing earns a real clue (and a seed for the long mystery). Kita Deep-Delver and Ysa Vael shine here.

### Room 3 — Trick or Setback
**The warded vaults**: what looks like the obvious route or the obvious treasure turns on the crew. A false floor or looping passage that costs them their mapping; a vault whose opening wakes its wardens; Revenants *older* and stranger than any surface-dweller, bound to the crypt and not behaving by the rules the Slayers know. The plan gets worse before it gets better.

### Room 4 — Climax
**The deep pool**: Umbra has gathered in the lowest level — the site's high point and hardest fight. The central confrontation lives here (a crypt-lord, a warden-construct, or an Umbra-thick horror), and pressing in trades safety for the best discoveries. This is the point of no easy return.

### Room 5 — Reward & Revelation
**The sealed door** the previous expedition chose not to open — foreshadow it from Room 1 on. Beyond it (or won by surviving Room 4): the real payoff — relics and Guild-grade treasure, plus **information** that recolors the whole site (about Zothu, the Apostasy-era builders, or why the Umbra pools here), and a hook that points onward. Reveal something; leave the deepest truth dangling.

**The call:** partial map and withdraw, or push for a full clear? Let the player and crew weigh it at any point. **Return & Report** either way.

## Combat

Hard. Ancient undead, crypt guardians, Umbra-touched horrors (DM pulls suitable SRD undead/aberrations and scales for a level 3–5 party with NPC support). Resource attrition this far out is itself a threat.

## Complications

- The previous expedition left something — or someone — behind, and it's still here.
- A relic the crew recovers is wanted quietly by a Noble House; word may have gone ahead of them.
- The crypt is not as dead as it looks; disturbing the wrong vault wakes what should have slept.

## Outcomes & Rewards

- **Partial success:** maps and relics back to the Guild — valuable even without a full clear. Strong reputation; the best pay and loot of the three.
- **Full clear / viable site:** a candidate for the deepest Checkpoint yet — a genuine expansion of Brightvale's reach.
- **A thread left dangling:** whatever was behind the sealed door, or wrong about the King's gallery, becomes a long-term campaign hook — consonant with the setting's promise that *something is waking up.* Do not resolve it here.

## DM Notes

- This is where the campaign's deep mysteries can begin to stir. Keep the Apostasy, the Beacon's true nature, and anything pre-Beacon as **secrets** — surface texture and dread, not answers.
- The "King Zothu" naming is a deliberate soft thread: the previous expedition may have gotten it wrong, and the truth can pay off much later.
