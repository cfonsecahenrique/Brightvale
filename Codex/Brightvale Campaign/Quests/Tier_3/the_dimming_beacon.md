# The Dimming Beacon

**Quest ID:** q_dimming_beacon
**Type:** Political crisis / investigation / revelation (with combat)
**Difficulty:** Very Hard (suggested levels 11–14; 6–8 sessions)
**Destination:** Medsen, the Beacon, and the depths below the Pale
**Posted by:** The Council of Seven — emergency commission, restricted distribution

> GM-facing brief. The Beacon's flicker has become undeniable and politically impossible to contain. This arc is where the institutional crisis of Brightvale converges with the player's accumulated knowledge about what the Beacon actually is. The GM secret of the Beacon's true mechanism — that it burns souls, that Beacon Dust is ground bone, that the Caretakers have known — surfaces here at a level the player can now act on. Do not resolve the question of whether the Beacon can be saved; that is Tier 4 territory. End with a decision made and a truth the player carries into whatever comes next.

## Premise

The Beacon has dimmed measurably, visibly, and publicly, for three consecutive nights. Food stocks are at two seasons' supply. Refugees from outer settlements are overwhelming Medsen's capacity. The Caretakers' public position — divine fluctuation, prayer is the response — is collapsing. The Council of Seven has commissioned a restricted investigation, and the player is its lead operative.

What they will find is not a mechanical failure. The Beacon's mechanism is working exactly as designed. Something about its fuel supply has changed.

## The Crisis

Three simultaneous pressures:

- **Political:** the three religious factions are in open contest for the Council's response. The Orthodoxy is calling for mass prayer and a suppression of the Pragmatist research program. The Pragmatists are pushing for a study access the Caretakers will never grant voluntarily. The fourth position — the veiled goddess's adherents — is surfacing in the lower wards in ways that cannot be ignored.
- **Logistical:** the Halfling Hearthlands' yield failure is real and worsening. The player's investigation will intersect this: the Beacon Dust used as fertilizer is at the center of it in a way no one has publicly connected.
- **The Dimming itself:** the Beacon is not failing randomly. It is responding to something. The senior Caretakers know what; they are not saying.

## The Crew

- **Councilor Brenn Iyengar** — the Council's commission holder; House Iyengar's neutral-arbiter tradition means she was the only Council member all factions would accept. She is calm, politically precise, and operating without the information the player will accumulate. She will act on what the player brings her if the player brings it clearly.
- **Aetherweaver Ysa Vael** — if she survived *Beyond the Sealed Door*, now a changed scholar whose direct reading ability is the most reliable way to examine the Beacon's mechanism from outside. She knows she is doing something the Order considers criminal.
- **A Senior Caretaker** — assigned to the investigation by the Order as a "liaison." The player has interacted with Caretakers before. This one is different: older, quieter, and in possession of something she intends to give the player at the right moment. She is not an obstacle. She is a confession waiting for the right listener.

## Arc Structure

**Commission → The Political Crisis → The Investigation → The Mechanism → The Truth → The Decision.**

### Session 1 — Commission & The Political Crisis

The Council commission is explicit in what it authorizes and careful about what it does not: the player may investigate the Beacon's dimming, access Guild and Academy records, and interview Caretaker personnel. The player may not access the Beacon's inner structure without Order consent. This restriction will be the first thing the player has to work around.

The lower wards: the fourth position's adherents are gathering nightly. The player may choose to engage with them — they know things about the veiled goddess's shrines in the Wastes that are directly relevant to the investigation — or the Council may assign a suppression operation that the player can deflect.

### Session 2 — The Investigation Begins

The investigation runs on three tracks:

- **The Beacon Dust track:** agricultural records show yield failures correlating with a reduction in Dust output. The Dust comes from the Caretakers' processing. The processing has slowed. The Caretakers explain this as a necessary conservation measure. Ysa Vael's analysis of the Dust's composition is the thread that pulls this apart.
- **The Dimming pattern track:** the Beacon dims on a cycle the player can chart. The cycle does not correspond to prayer schedules, to Umbra activity, or to weather. It corresponds to the rate of a specific natural process the player has to identify. The senior Caretaker will give the player the key to reading it if they ask the right question.
- **The records track:** the material from *Beyond the Sealed Door* or *The Undercity* (if completed) contains references to the Beacon's predecessor — what it was before the Caretakers formalized it — that clarify what the Beacon is consuming and why the supply has changed.

### Session 3 — The Mechanism Surfaces

Ysa Vael's analysis of the Beacon Dust confirms: the Dust contains bone. Specifically, the calcified remains of individuals with a strong Beacon resonance — the Marked, as the Caretakers internally call them. The Beacon burns souls and the Caretakers grind the bones.

The player now knows the GM secret. What they do with it — who they tell, in what form — is the arc's central political question.

The senior Caretaker's confession, when it comes, is not apologetic. She explains the mechanism plainly: it was designed this way, it has always worked this way, and the people who designed it understood that calling it something holy was the only way the Enduring would accept it. She does not defend this. She asks what the player intends to do, and she means it as a real question.

### Session 4 — Why the Beacon Is Dimming

The specific cause of the current dimming: the pool of Marked individuals has narrowed. Centuries of consuming the most Beacon-resonant people in each generation has depleted the bloodline. The Sun-Born line — the Kings of Brightvale — was the most Marked of all, and King Alfwyn's line is the last of it. The Beacon is dimming because its most efficient fuel source is nearly gone.

The Caretakers' proposed solution, which they have not shared with the Council, is to identify and cultivate the next generation of Marked individuals — a breeding program for the Beacon's fuel. The documents for this program exist. The player can acquire them.

### Session 5 — The Political Crisis Peaks

The lower wards' fourth-position community has obtained — from somewhere — a partial version of what the player is investigating. Not the Beacon Dust's composition, but the fact that the Caretakers have withheld information from the Council. They are circulating it. The political crisis becomes a public crisis.

Councilor Iyengar needs an answer from the player. She will hold the line for one more session. After that, the factions move without the player.

### Sessions 6–7 — The Decision

The player must decide what to do with what they know, and it must be a decision, not a deferral:

- **Full disclosure to the Council:** the Beacon's mechanism, the Caretakers' program, the breeding proposal. The institutional crisis this causes may be worse than the dimming itself. The Orthodoxy collapses. The Caretakers lose their authority. Something has to replace them before the Beacon goes dark.
- **Selective disclosure:** tell the Council enough to stop the breeding program without revealing the Beacon Dust mechanism. Buy time. The Orthodoxy is shaken but survives in modified form. The Dust secret remains.
- **Work within the system:** bring the Caretakers to the Council table under terms the player negotiates. The breeding program is suspended; the mechanism is disclosed to the Council alone, not publicly; a study access for the Academy is the price. The truth is managed rather than released.
- **Find an alternative fuel:** the fourth position's shrines in the Wastes — particularly the one from *The Veiled Goddess* — have a Umbra-suppression property no one has explained. The player has enough information to ask whether the veiled goddess's mechanism could supplement or replace the Beacon's current one. This is not a solution in this arc; it is a direction that opens Tier 4.

The arc closes with a political fact: one of these paths is taken, one faction gains authority, one truth is either in the world or not. The Beacon is slightly less dim by the arc's end — not because the mechanism is fixed, but because the player's action bought time. How much time is the question the next tier answers.

## Combat

Escalating and political: a Slayer unit deployed by the Caretakers to suppress the lower-wards gathering, a confrontation at the Beacon's outer structure when the player attempts access, and one encounter in the deep structure beneath the Pale where the breeding program's infrastructure is maintained. Calibrate to levels 11–14.

## Complications

- King Alfwyn knows part of the truth — he has known since his coronation — and has chosen silence. His decision to maintain or break that silence in response to the player's investigation is his character's defining moment.
- The Aetherweaver Academy's progressive wing has been running parallel research and has reached a different conclusion about the Beacon's mechanism — one that is wrong in a specific, dangerous way, and that they intend to publish.
- The sealed vessel from *Beyond the Sealed Door*, if the player has it, contains a partial account of the original Beacon's construction. It answers a question the player has been carrying. It also raises one they have not asked yet.

## Outcomes & Rewards

- **Full disclosure:** political upheaval; the Beacon's mechanism in public knowledge; a new institutional arrangement to be built; the player the most important political actor in Brightvale.
- **Selective disclosure or managed truth:** stability, fragile and purchased; the player holding the most dangerous secret in the Vale; allies in all three factions for different reasons.
- **Alternative fuel pursued:** the fourth position's shrines as a genuine investigation target; Tier 4 opened; the institutional crisis paused but not resolved.
- The player's standing with every major Brightvale faction shifts permanently. This is the arc that makes the Vale a different place than it was.
