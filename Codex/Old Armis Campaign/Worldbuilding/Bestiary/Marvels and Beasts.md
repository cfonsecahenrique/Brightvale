# Marvels & Beasts of the Known World

A Pliny-style natural history: the creatures, monsters, and wonders of the age, as a venturer (or Hannor the Far-Sailed) might catalogue them. Fact and tall tale sit side by side here — exactly as they would in any honest chronicle of a world still mostly unmapped. The age's deepest dread is no longer the **dragon** — charted and assimilated since contact with Ziramat — but the **unknown** that has fled to the unmapped edges, where the old Homeric monsters wait.

> **Note on the age:** the **Umbra does not exist yet.** Revenants, Rotlords, and Umbra-touched horrors belong to the *Brightvale* era after the [[Apostasy]]. The undeath of *this* age is **necromantic** ([[An'Hiseret]]) or **residuum-born** ([[Adamantis]]) — related horrors with wholly different sources. Keep them distinct.

---

## Beasts of Sea and Sky

- **The Azurian Serpent** — the great sea-drake of the open ocean, terror of the Western Voyages; ration-discipline and Weave-calm are the only defences. Real, and worse than the stories.
- **Reef-scavengers and channel-hunters** — the lesser predators of the shallows and reefs, a green crew's first taste of teeth.
- **Hannor's tall tales** — the ship-swallowing *kraken* of the deep Azurian, the singing fish of the Hesperine shoals, the bird that is never seen to land. He reports them faithfully and believes perhaps half.

## Beasts of the Frontier

- **The horses of the Steppes** — the Orcenfolk's peerless mounts, finer than any the heartland breeds; half the steppe's power is in them.
- **[[Glacier Giants]]** — the ice-borne ancient enemy of [[Athregal]], calving from the high glaciers in raiding-bands.
- **The exotic fauna of the new world** — beyond the [[The Titan's Sword|Titan's Sword]], strange animals the Empire has no names for, brought home (live or stuffed) to astonish the Concord and fill the menageries of the great houses.

## Dragons and the Dragonkin

- **The dragon-folk of [[Ziramat]]** — scaled people, kin to the primordial dragons that shaped them; not beasts, but no Armisian is quite sure of that.
- **The [[Primordial Dragons|primordial dragons]]** — the world-making powers of Ziramati myth: Tiamat the World-Mother and her five brood. Foreign superstition, the Empire says. The Empire is wrong.

## The Deathless and the Husk

- **The Mindless of [[An'Hiseret]]** — corpses raised by necromancy into tireless labour and war; the undead the south is built on. Soulless tools, controlled by living (or souled) masters.
- **The Isle-Touched** — not undead at all, but the *living* unmade: those who channel the raw residuum of the [[Adamantis|Adamantis]] and come apart by degrees into the Aether that saturates that ground. A wholly different horror, and a warning the Empire is not heeding.

## The Fae — the Good Neighbours

The folk of the **[[_Elven Faith and the Fae|Faewilds]]**, communed with by the Elves at the thin places: court-fae and small folk, the Wild Hunt, the river-mothers and the Hag of Winter. Bound by bargain and glamour; banished by cold iron. To Hannor they are a charming northern superstition. They are nothing of the kind.

## Wonders of the Made World

- **[[Pattis]], the moving city** — an island-metropolis that sails the [[The Central Sea|Central Sea]] above and below the waves; the supreme marvel of the age.
- **The Moving Necropolis** — the corpse-hauled wandering city of the An'Hisereti desert, dragged on the backs of the chained dead. The grimmest marvel a traveller can claim to have seen.
- **The [[Armis-Caen#The Harbour Quarter|cothon of Armis-Caen and Pattis]]**, the cliff-cities of Ziramat, the standing stones and barrows of the Elves, the Aether-Helm — the made wonders of the peoples.

## Wonders of the Wild World

- **The residuum of Adamantis** — raw Aether bleeding from the ground, found nowhere else; the prize of the age. (GM: a dead primordial's blood.)
- **The [[The Eye of the Richat|Eye of the Richat]]** — the vast ringed structure deep in the southern desert, which Hannor reports as a natural marvel of stone. (GM: a reclusive god's seat; see [[Age of Heroes and the Withdrawal of the Gods]].)
- **Tyrian purple, woad, glass, Aether-worked craft** — the precious substances and arts the chronicle lingers over.

## How Hannor Tells It

Hannor reports the husk and the serpent as plainly as the kraken and the singing fish, drawing no firm line between what he saw, what he was sworn to, and what he suspects. The reader sorts truth from marvel — and the GM knows that some of his "superstitions" (the Fae, the dragons, the gods at the world's edges) are the truest things in the book.
