# The Gorgon Ysmene

The serpent-haired woman of the **[[The Garden of Stone|Garden of Stone]]**, in the high hills behind [[Lissipos]]. She was a mother whose child was killed. She prayed to [[Kythera]] for vengeance, and the goddess gave it to her and never took it back. *(The tale: **[[_Heroes and Legends#VI. The Mother Who Prayed for Vengeance|The Mother Who Prayed for Vengeance]]**.)*

> **Kind:** *The Cursed* — though Ysmene would say *the answered*. Of all the monsters of the Elder Days she is the most human, and the most dangerous to meet with open eyes.

## What She Is
- **A woman, mostly.** She is tall and gaunt, robed in grey wool she weaves herself, with hands roughened by a thousand years of shepherding. Her hair is a mass of thin green-black serpents that move with her moods: still when she is calm, a hissing crown when she grieves. Her face is beautiful and terrible, and almost no one living has seen it.
- **The Gaze of Kythera.** Whoever meets her eyes **sees what they have done**: every cruelty, every harm, everything they let happen, all at once. They turn to stone under the weight of it. Mirrors and reflections blunt the effect but don't remove it. Phaedra Moon-Shield proved that the reflection still shows the truth.
- **Not a hunter.** She keeps to her valley and her flock. She has never come down to the coast. She doesn't need to. They come to her.

## The Singing
At night, when the wind is right, Ysmene sings a lullaby in a dialect so old the scholars of [[Himilkos]] can only half-read it. Everyone who hears it understands it anyway. It is the song she sang to her child.

The grieving come to it. The guilty come to it too, and they are the ones who never come back. Most of the statues in the Garden are not heroes. They are people who walked up the mountain at night because they could not bear what they had done, and needed someone to see it.

## How She Is Feared
- **Eyes to the ground.** In the hill-villages below the Garden, children are taught to walk the high pastures looking at their feet before they are taught their letters. Shepherds wear a fold of cloth that drops over the eyes when the serpents hiss.
- **Nobody hums the lullaby.** Humming it is the worst luck in Lissipos. A mother who catches her child humming it takes the child to Kythera's temple that day.
- **The Widows' Registry.** The oldest entry in the Lissiote temple's accounting of wrongs done to mothers is Ysmene's, and it is still marked **unsettled**. *(See below.)*

## The Truth (GM)
- **Her vengeance is not finished.** Ysmene prayed for the lord *and his line* to see what they had done. She turned the household she found, but one son was away from home that day. **The lord's line survives.** It runs, thinned and ennobled, through a family of rank in [[Armis-Caen]], a house with no idea where its first fortune came from or what it owes.
- **When the line ends, the debt is paid.** Kythera's gift may then lift, and Ysmene could die as a woman, at peace. Or she might simply walk down the mountain to see the last of them for herself. Kythera's priesthood at Lissipos knows the family's name. It has never said it aloud.
- **She can be spoken to.** Anyone who comes to her blindfolded, with no blade drawn, and asks her about the child will be answered. She has not had a conversation in a very long time.
- **Her head is a weapon of terrible power,** even severed. The Empire would want it. That alone is reason enough to keep it on her shoulders.

## Hooks (GM)
- **A glory-hunter from the capital** arrives in the hill-villages with mirrors, hired swords, and a patron's purse. The villagers want him stopped before he provokes her. His patron, it turns out, is a member of *that* family.
- **Someone the party knows** walked up the mountain after the singing. Their statue isn't in the Garden yet.
- **The Registry entry.** Lissipos's priestesses quietly hire the party to trace a bloodline, and don't tell them why.
- *Use the `generate-daggerheart-adversary` skill for a Solo stat block. The gaze should be a reaction to eye contact, not an attack, and it should hit harder the more the target has to answer for.*
