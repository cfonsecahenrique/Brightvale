# The Bull-King of the Horned Court

**Asteros**, once king of **[[Ambrakis and the Horned Court|Ambrakis]]**, now a bull-headed giant deathless in the dark of his own palace. He kept the white bull he had vowed to [[Thalassos]], and the sea-god made him into the thing he wouldn't give up. *(The tale: **[[_Heroes and Legends#II. The Bull-King's Oath|The Bull-King's Oath]]**.)*

> **Kind:** *The Cursed* — a mortal remade by a god's wrath. He is not an animal, though it has been a very long time since he was a man.

## What It Is
- **Size and shape.** He is half again the height of a tall man, with a bull's head, neck and shoulders on a body that is still recognisably a king's. His hands are a man's hands, too large and too strong, with nails gone to horn. The bronze crown of Ambrakis is grown into the bone between his horns, green with a thousand years of damp.
- **Deathless, not unharmed.** He bears every wound anyone has ever given him. Spear-heads are lodged in his hide and old axe-scars run across his back. A dozen generations of heroes and fools have hurt him. None has ended him, because the curse won't allow him to die.
- **He remembers.** In the long dark of the maze he is mostly beast: territorial, savage, starving. But he knows his own halls perfectly, and fragments of the king surface without warning. He speaks the vow. He weeps at the frescoes of his queen. Now and then he spares someone, and no one knows why.

## The Maze
Daedrion the Maker built the Horned Court around the old palace at the queen's request: a prison with no answer, its walls set so that no path leads out the way it led in. *(See [[Ambrakis and the Horned Court]].)*
- **It is growing.** Since the [[Aetherweave Mastery|Awakening]], new passages have opened that no one cut, and older ones have found their way under the town. Some say the curse is spreading as the king's mind fails. Others say Daedrion's work was always meant to tighten.
- **He hunts by sound and smell.** Lamps don't draw him, but voices do. Survivors' advice, passed down in Ambrakis, is the same every time: *walk soft, say nothing, and if you hear him say the vow, kneel.*

## How He Is Feared
- **The bellowing.** On still nights the whole town hears him through the rock. Children are taught the words in it (*"I swore — I swore — I swore"*) as a warning against perjury before they can read.
- **No cellars.** No house in Ambrakis is built with one. Nobody digs a well within the old wall. A family that hears hoof-scrapes under the floor moves out the same night and doesn't sell the house.
- **Oath-breakers fear him across the whole Sword.** Colonial magistrates on the [[The Titan's Sword|Titan's Sword]] sentence convicted perjurers *"to the King."* The phrase alone has ended more lawsuits than any court.

## The Truth (GM)
- **He can be released, and killing him won't do it.** The curse ends when the vow is kept: a white bull from the sea, sacrificed to Thalassos *by Asteros's own hand*, at the Opening of the Sea. That means getting a bull worthy of the god (a thing of legend in itself), getting it into the maze alive, and getting a cursed king to remember himself long enough to raise the knife.
- **If he is freed, he dies.** He will die at once, a thousand years overdue, and a king's ghost might have a great deal to say about the Age of Heroes before it goes.
- **The Thalassos cult of [[Pattis]] knows this** and has never acted. A god's curse made public, visible and permanent is a better sermon on vows than any priest could preach.

## Hooks (GM)
- **A perjurer the party needs** (a witness, a cartographer, a defector) has just been sentenced to the King. They have one night.
- **The maze has broken through** under the harbour-master's house. The town wants it sealed, a scholar wants it mapped, and something is coming up the new tunnel.
- **Ending the curse.** This is a full mythic quest: find the white bull, reach the king, and wake the man inside the monster. Done properly, it will be sung for a thousand years.
- *Use the `generate-daggerheart-adversary` skill for a Solo stat block. Give it a "Vow-Fragment" reaction that ends a fight when the right words are spoken.*
