# Imperial Governance — How the Empire Is Ruled

The Armisian Empire is a **hegemonic confederation**, not a centralised state: a web of city-states, colonies, and great houses bound to the twin capital of **[[Armis-Caen]]** by trade, treaty, prestige, and a fraying thread of sacred kingship. Power here does not sit in one hand. It is contested between four estates, and the contest is the constant background of imperial life.

---

## The Anax — the Sacral High King

At the apex, in title, sits **the Anax of Armis-Caen** — the sacral high king, last thin inheritor of the **demi-god royal blood of the [[Age of Heroes and the Withdrawal of the Gods|Age of Heroes]]**. Where [[An'Hiseret]]'s god-kings never stopped ruling (their divine line kept deathless by necromancy), in Armis the god-blood **thinned and faded** across the long centuries since the gods withdrew, until the Anax became more priest-king and symbol than autocrat.

The Anax sanctifies the great undertakings, presides over the high rites, embodies the Empire's continuity, and — crucially — **breaks deadlocks** the other estates cannot resolve. But the day-to-day machinery of empire has leached away from the throne into the institutions below. A strong Anax can still move the world by will and prestige; a weak one reigns over men who no longer truly obey him.

*(The faded-divine-king of Armis is the deliberate mirror of An'Hiseret's deathless god-king: two ends of the same Age-of-Heroes inheritance.)*

## The Concord — the Council of Cities and Houses

The real administrative power. The **Concord** is the standing council where the free city-states and the great **merchant-noble houses** sit — debating tariffs, law, war, colonial charters, and the endless question of how much the centre may ask of the cities. It is loud, slow, faction-ridden, and genuinely powerful: no imperial undertaking proceeds far against the Concord's will. [[Pattis]], [[Himilkos]], and the heartland cities each send their voices, and each guards its own interest first.

## The Imperial Admiralty — the Sword and the Sea

The Empire is a thalassocracy, and the **Admiralty** holds the instrument of its power: the navy, the licensing of the Western Voyages, the colonial charters, the contract-boards that send venturers into the world. In practice the Admiralty is the most far-reaching arm of the state — wherever the Empire *acts* abroad, it acts through the Admiralty. **Navarch Kassia Thorne** keeps her contract-office at [[Pattis]]. The Admiralty's reach is also a standing source of friction with the Concord and the cities, who finance what the Admiralty commands.

## The Merchant Councils — the Purse

The Empire runs on coin, and the coin runs through the city **Merchant Councils**, chief among them the **Merchant Council of Pattis** — which finances the voyages the Admiralty charters and resents the distant capital's hand on its docks. The harbor-temple treasuries lend and underwrite (see [[Trade and Daily Life]]). Whoever holds the purse holds a veto that needs no army.

## The Fault Lines

Imperial politics is the friction between these estates:
- **Centre vs. free cities.** Armis-Caen and the Concord pull toward unity; [[Pattis]] and the great free cities guard their independence — and Pattis, which can literally *sail away*, makes that threat real. The **secession current** runs beneath everything.
- **Admiralty vs. Concord.** The arm that acts vs. the body that pays and debates.
- **Throne vs. institutions.** A sacral king with fading real power, and estates that increasingly rule in his name.
- **The houses, always.** The great merchant-noble dynasties play every side, and some of them carry the last thin traces of god-blood — a prestige they never let anyone forget.

## What a Venturer Deals With

In practice, a venturer almost never meets the Anax. They meet **the Admiralty** (who hires them), **a Merchant Council or house** (who funds or owns the venture), and **the Concord's politics** (which decide whether their findings matter). The throne is a distant, sacred rumor; the contract-board is the face of the Empire they actually serve.

## Hooks (GM)

- A weak or dying Anax is a succession crisis waiting to happen — and the god-blood question (who truly carries it) can turn a political squabble sacred.
- Centre, cities, Admiralty, and houses each want different things from any major discovery; a venturer's report is a stone dropped into all four ponds.
- The secession current (Pattis especially) is a slow fracture a clever player can widen or mend.
