# Peoples & Faith of the Empire

The Armisian Empire is human-ruled but far from human-only. Its golden age is a meeting of peoples, old and new.

---

## Humankind — the Ruling People

The young, numerous, restless people who rose to overtake the elder Elves and build the Empire. Hellenic-Phoenician in culture, drunk on the memory and the making of empire. They keep the **[[_Armisian Pantheon|Armisian Pantheon]]** — a Bronze Age Mediterranean family of gods of sea, sky, craft, and war, worshipped in harbor-temples and ship's shrines, propitiated before every voyage.

## Elves — the Elder People

The elder people of the world: the great civilisation that flourished *before* humankind, the first to raise cities and walk the threshold of the Fae. Ancient Celtic, specifically Brythonic — tribal, druidic, oral, bound to the seasons and to standing stones. By the golden age they are a conquered people, subjugated and assimilated into a younger empire, most numerous in its greener north-eastern reaches where the old ways are still kept.

To Armisian eyes they are **godless** — no pantheon, no idols, no temples, only the worship of primordial nature and communion with the Fae. They keep **Powers, not gods**: the living land (grove, spring, and stone), the ancestors in the barrows, and the **[[_Elven Faith and the Fae|Faewilds]]** — the Otherworld of spirits and seasons, which touches ours at thresholds and is openly walked by elven druids. Theirs is a magic of **bargain and glamour**, older than the Empire's planar Aetherweave and answerable to neither it nor the death-cult. They mark their skin in sacred **blue woad** and refuse iron, which is bane to the Fae — and so feel, as no one else does, the Empire's spreading iron closing the Otherworld's doors.

## Dwarves of Athregal — the Newest Arrivals

Met only within living memory, at [[Zergas-by-Azure]] on the [[The Titan's Sword|Titan's Sword]]. Norse-tongued, clan-fractured, and bellicose, the Dwarves of the far north war endlessly among themselves — and, when they unite, against the [[Glacier Giants]] who press down out of the ice. First contact was war; an uneasy trade now follows, and what began as Dwarven trading-posts in [[Pattis]] and [[Armis-Caen|Armis]] have grown into **sanctioned colonies** — Dwarves are an ever more common sight in Armisian life, and they call the Armisians the **Miðfolk**, the "Middle Folk," in their own tongue. They are also the world's **iron-masters**: the secrets of smelting and steel are a Dwarven inheritance, and it was through them that the bronze-working Empire first met iron. They guard both the craft and the rich northern ore, keeping the Empire dependent on what they choose to sell.

## Orcenfolk — the People of the Steppes

The free horse-lords of the **[[The Eastern Steppes|Eastern Steppes]]**, beyond the settled heartland — mounted, clan-organised, and never ruled by the Empire. Peerless mounted archers who, in most seasons, feud among themselves and are easily played one clan against another — but who, when a **Great Kha** unites the grass into a single horse-host, can force the proud heartland to **buy peace** with caravans of "gifts" that are tribute in all but name. They keep an ancestor-honouring **faith of the Endless Sky**, an oral law, and a code of hospitality and feud outsiders underestimate at their peril.

## Dragonkin of Ziramat — the Strangers Across the Ocean

The dragon-folk of the crimson deserts south of the Azurian, only just contacted and barely known. Scaled kin who carved cliff-cities into red rock, ruled by a matriarchy in the image of their dragon-queen, and practising a form of the Weave unlike the Empire's own. They remain a **Bronze Age society**, behind both the Empire and [[Athregal]] in metallurgy — though their Weave and their dragons make that a more dangerous gap than it sounds.

## Beneath the Pantheons

Older than any of these faiths are powers the cultures only half-name — dragons, the deep, the slumbering things in the unexplored dark. The Elves keep communion with the Fae; the dragon-folk speak of a World-Mother and her brood; Armisian sailors mutter their own charms against what lives past the last chart. They are glimpsing, each in their own tongue, something true and very old. What it is, none of them yet knows.

And older than all of it is the truth the peoples have forgotten: that they are **one shattered family**, divergent branches of a single ancestral stock from a fallen Age, and that the gods who shaped them once *walked the earth* in the Age of Heroes before they withdrew from the world's open places. See [[Origins of the Peoples]] and [[Age of Heroes and the Withdrawal of the Gods]].
