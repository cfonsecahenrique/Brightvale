# Trade & Daily Life

The texture of the golden age, in the things people carry, wear, eat, and owe. The Empire is a **Bronze Age Mediterranean** civilisation in its bones — and a sea-empire, so its life is the life of cargo and harbor — with the Weave laid over the top as the ultimate luxury and the ultimate dependency.

---

## Bronze, Tin, and the Long Routes

The age is named for its metal, and the metal has a secret weakness: **bronze is copper and tin, and tin is rare.** Copper the Empire has; tin must be carried from a handful of distant sources, and the trade-routes that bring it are lifelines a civilisation of bronze cannot live without. Much of the Empire's restless reach — and much of what it will not say aloud about *why* it must keep trading no matter the cost — comes down to securing tin. The coming of Dwarven **iron** is so coveted partly because it promises escape from the tin-leash; see [[Iron and Bronze]].

Metal moves in standardised **oxhide ingots** — flat slabs cast with handle-like corners for carrying and lashing — stacked in every ship's hold. To see a hull's ballast of oxhide ingots is to see wealth in its rawest form.

## Money Before Coin

There are **no coins** in the Bronze Age sense — value is **weighed**, not counted. Trade runs on **hacksilver** (cut and weighed pieces of silver and gold), on bullion, and on sealed, standardised weights checked against a merchant's own balance. The great **harbor-temples hold the treasuries** and so serve as the age's banks: they store wealth, lend at interest, guarantee contracts, and underwrite voyages. A merchant's word is only as good as the temple that stands behind it.

Contracts and ownership are sealed — literally — with carved **cylinder and stamp seals**, rolled or pressed into clay to mark a deal, a jar, a door, a letter. A person's seal is their signature and their identity; to lose it is a small catastrophe, and to forge one is a serious crime.

## The Prestige Goods

Status is worn and displayed in a handful of precious things:

- **Tyrian purple** — the royal dye, wrung in tiny quantities from sea-snails at ruinous labour and stench. A purple-bordered garment is a statement of rank no one can mistake; deep, full purple is for the highest houses alone.
- **Glass and faience** — Armisian glasswork is famous, glittering, and dear: beads, vessels, inlays, the glazed blue-green faience of temple offerings.
- **Cedar and worked timber** — the Empire's ships and its great halls are built of prized timber hauled from the few coasts that still grow it; good ship-cedar is a strategic good.
- **Ivory, wine, olive oil, fine textiles, incense** — the everyday luxuries of the wealthy, and the cargoes that fill the contract-boards.

## Dress, Table, and Household

- **Dress:** linen for the heat, wool for the cold and the sea, draped and pinned rather than tailored; dyed bright by those who can afford it, with the purple border as the mark of standing. Sailors go plainer and barefoot on deck.
- **Table:** bread, olives, salt fish, figs and dates, cheese; **wine cut with water** (to drink it neat is a barbarian's habit); the rich add roast meat and imported delicacies. Fresh water is itself precious at sea.
- **Household gods:** every home keeps a shrine to [[Maeris]] and the ancestors of the house; the dead of one's own line are honoured at the hearth.

## The Unfree

Like every power of the age, the Empire runs on **slavery** — war-captives, debt-slaves, the sold and the seized — labouring in households, mines, fields, and at the oar. It is unremarkable to the Armisians and woven through everything, the quiet ugliness beneath the golden age's beauty. Its victims have a way of ending up at [[Borkum Reef]]. For the most monstrous version of bound labour, look south to [[An'Hiseret]], where even death is no release.

## Writing and Memory

The Empire keeps two hands: an older **temple syllabary** for sacred and formal record, and a quick **merchant alphabet** — the Empire's own great export to the world — for accounts, contracts, and letters. Records are kept on clay tablets, wax boards, and imported reed-paper; the deep archives of old [[Himilkos]] are the closest thing the age has to a memory of itself.

## The Weave, Laid Over Everything

What sets the Empire apart from any mere Bronze Age is the **Aether laid across ordinary life** — as luxury, as infrastructure, and as dependency:

- **At sea:** fresh water and preserved food held sound on long crossings by the Weave; winds summoned and swells calmed. A planar-worked voyage outruns and outlasts anything oar and sail alone could manage.
- **In the great cities:** the undersea districts of [[Pattis]] kept dry and breathing, its very streets carried across the sea — comforts that exist *only* while the Weave holds.
- **In craft:** the finest workshops sell Weave-worked goods — vessels, ornaments, and tools touched by the planes — that no ordinary smith can equal, the Aetherwright's art bleeding into the luxury market.

Every one of these is a wonder, and every one is a thread that can be cut. The Empire has woven the Weave so deeply into daily comfort that it has half-forgotten the comfort is borrowed.

## Hooks (GM)

- A tin-route cut, a seal forged, a temple-treasury's loan called in — the economy is full of pressure-points a venturer can be hired to protect or exploit.
- Tyrian purple, Dwarven steel, Weave-worked treasure: the rewards that actually signal status in this world, worth more as marks of standing than as coin.
- Let the small Weave-comforts flicker now and then — preserved food gone bad, a lamp dimming in the Pattis undercity — as quiet, deniable foreshadowing.
