# Iron and Bronze

The Armisian Empire is, for all its magic, a **Bronze Age civilisation**. Its soldiers carry bronze; its smiths cast and hammer bronze; its temples, tools, and treasures are the work of a bronze-working people. Strip away the Weave and the Empire would look like what it is — a brilliant Mediterranean bronze culture at its zenith. The Weave is the one thing that makes it more, and for two and a half centuries it has been enough.

Then the Empire met the Dwarves, and the Empire met **iron**.

---

## The Metal the North Brought

The Armisians did not discover ironworking. The **[[Dwarven Clans|Dwarves of Athregal]]** did, long before contact — the smelting, the forging, the secrets of steel are a northern inheritance, jealously kept. When Armisian and Dwarven colonists first clashed at [[Zergas-by-Azure]] in the **900s AF**, the Empire's bronze met Dwarven iron, and the difference was not subtle. Iron and steel hold an edge bronze cannot, take an impact bronze will not, and turn a bronze blade aside almost contemptuously.

In the roughly **hundred years since first contact**, iron weapons and armour have begun to flow into the Empire — through trade where the Dwarves permit it, through plunder where they do not, and through the slow, imperfect theft of technique. The Empire, which is nothing if not adaptive, has been **quick to adopt it**: iron is spreading through the legions, the fleets, and the great houses as fast as supply allows. A century from now there may be no bronze left in an Armisian armoury. For now, the Empire is a civilisation mid-transition — bronze in its bones, iron in its ambitions.

## The Supply Problem — Why the Empire Wants the Sword

Iron is transformative, and the Empire cannot get enough of it. The heartland sits on little workable ore, and the two great sources lie at the ends of the world:

- **[[The Titan's Sword]]** holds the most accessible iron deposits within the Empire's reach. This is a — perhaps *the* — material reason the Empire is so determined to colonise the western landmass. The voyages of discovery are romance and gold-fever; the grip on the Titan's Sword is iron, literally. [[Zergas-by-Azure]] and the younger colony of Asimo are not only gateways to the new world — they are mining claims.
- **The deep North** holds the richest deposits of all, and they are **monopolised by [[Athregal]]**. The Dwarves control the ore and the craft both, and have every reason to keep the Empire dependent on what they choose to sell. As long as the [[Glacier Giants]] press down on the clans, Athregal cannot fully exploit this advantage — which is one more reason the Empire is content to see that northern war continue.

## The Three Powers and the Metal

The metallurgical gap is now a strategic fault line in the three-way scramble:

- **Armis** — bronze transitioning to iron, hungry, adaptive, and dependent on others' ore. Strong in the Weave, racing to close the metal gap before it matters in a war.
- **[[Athregal]]** — the iron-masters. Outmatched in planar Aetherweave, but holding the metal advantage and the knowledge behind it. Fractured by clan feud and the Giant war, or they would be far more dangerous than they are.
- **[[Ziramat]]** — still very much a Bronze Age society, like the Empire was a century ago. The dragon-folk have their own Weave and their own dragons, but in raw metallurgy they are behind both rivals. Whether they know it, and what they will trade or risk to catch up, is an open and dangerous question.

## The Cost No One Counts

Iron is a military and economic revolution — and, to the Elves, a spiritual catastrophe. **Cold iron is bane to the Fae:** it breaks glamour, repels the Good Neighbours, and seals the thresholds to the **[[_Elven Faith and the Fae|Faewilds]]**. The Elves work bronze, gold, and stone for exactly this reason. So the spread of Dwarven iron is, beneath the trade and the warfare, a quiet closing of the doors to the Otherworld — the veil thickening as the iron advances, the Fae withdrawing, an ancient part of the world being severed by a people who do not believe it exists. The Empire calls it progress. The druids of Eldion call it the end of something. See [[The Closing Doors]].

## What a Venturer Sees

- **Mixed arsenals.** A wealthy Armisian carries iron; a common levy still carries bronze. The metal a person bears is a sign of status, era, and access.
- **Iron as treasure.** A Dwarven-forged steel blade is a genuinely prized reward — better than its bronze equivalent and a mark of standing.
- **The colonial stakes laid bare.** Behind every contract on the Titan's Sword is ore. Behind every dealing with Athregal is the metal monopoly. Behind Ziramat's hostility is a power that knows it is falling behind.

## Hooks (GM)

- Control of an iron deposit, a Dwarven smith's defection, or a stolen cache of forging technique is a prize whole expeditions — and houses — will kill for.
- The Empire's iron dependence on Athregal is a leash the Dwarves can pull. A venturer caught in a trade dispute is caught in something larger than they know.
- Ziramat's metallurgical weakness is a tempting target and a dangerous one: a power that fears falling behind is a power that takes risks.
