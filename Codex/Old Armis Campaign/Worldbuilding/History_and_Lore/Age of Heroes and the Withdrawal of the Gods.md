# The Age of Heroes & the Withdrawal of the Gods

The age when **the gods walked the earth** — and the long step back that followed it. Remembered by humans only in myth and half-garbled epic, and by the deathless Elves as something closer to history, this is the lost golden world that shaped the present one. The crucial thing to understand, and the thing humanity has forgotten: **the gods did not leave. They withdrew.** They are in the world still.

> Much of this survives as **oral myth and druidic memory**, not verified record. What is presented here is what is *remembered and believed*; the deepest truths are GM-only.

---

## When the Gods Walked

In the Age of Heroes the gods were not distant powers prayed to from harbor-temples. They were **present** — walking the world, shaping it, intervening in it directly. Chief among their works was their care for **humanity**: the youngest, weakest, least magical of the peoples, whom the gods took deliberately under their protection and guidance.

Over the long ages of that guidance, gods and mortals **intermarried**, and their children — the demi-gods, half-divine and mighty — became the **rulers of humanity**, the god-blooded kings and queens whose deeds are the stuff of every epic the Armisians still half-remember.

## The Withdrawal — the Gift of Agency

The gods did not stay to rule forever. Judging their work done — humanity grown, thriving, and ready — they **deliberately stepped back from open life**, so that mortals and the younger races could carve their own destinies unshadowed by their makers. They instructed the **first-generation demi-gods** to do the same. This was not abandonment. It was a gift, and a terrible one: the gift of agency, freely given.

They did not leave the world. They **withdrew into it** — into its loneliest and most extreme places — and there they remain, while their demi-god children still roam among mortals, thinned and scattered through the bloodlines of the age.

## Where the Gods Dwell Now

The withdrawn gods keep to the places no empire reaches:
- the **lonely peaks** above the cloud
- the deep **arctic** beyond the northern ice
- the **[[The Eye of the Richat]]**, the great ringed structure deep in the southern desert
- **beneath the waves** of the Central Sea
- and other thresholds of the world's edge

To find one is the stuff of legend — and not always survivable. A venturer who reaches such a place may meet a power that has not spoken to mortals in an age.

## The One Who Did Not Withdraw

There is an exception, and it is the wound of the south. **Amun-Atir, the Death God,** did not step back. When the other gods withdrew, she had already *seized* the human cradle by the [[The Seretis|Seretis]], turning it to the death-cult that became [[An'Hiseret]] — and it was her seizure that drove the other gods to withdraw northward and nurture the peoples who founded Armis, Caen, and Himilkos. Through her demi-god sons, the **Undead God-Kings**, she rules openly still, the only god who never let her people go.

## Her — the Nurturer of Humanity

Among the gods who withdrew, one is remembered above all by the Elves: a **Goddess who nurtured and protected humanity** — Her, the mother-power who sheltered the young people most tenderly. The Elves call humans, to this day, **"Her sons and daughters,"** a phrase worn so smooth by time that few even among the Elves recall what it means.

The humans themselves have **forgotten Her.** Her name does not stand among the [[_Armisian Pantheon|Armisian Pantheon]]; at most She survives there diminished, an echo in a lesser hearth-goddess. She is not dead and not gone — only withdrawn, like the rest, and *erased from memory* in a way the others are not. Why a goddess who was once humanity's own protector should be so thoroughly scrubbed from human memory is a question with a dangerous answer.

## The Pantheon as Faded Memory

The gods the Armisians worship now are best understood as humanity's **mythologised memory of the gods who really walked**, and who are *still out there*, withdrawn. The centuries since the Withdrawal have worn living powers down, in human telling, into a civic pantheon of names and rites — propitiated for wind and safe passage by a people who no longer remember these were beings that *stood among them*, and could be found yet by anyone mad enough to seek the world's edges.

## The Tragedy Coiled Inside the Gift

The Withdrawal is the setting's great Shakespearean turn. The gods gave humanity freedom — and that freedom is the instrument of their undoing. As human societies grow and their dominion over the Weave deepens, mortals will one day reach for divinity itself and make war on their absent makers. The gift of agency becomes the murder weapon. The gods stepped back to let humanity flourish; humanity will flourish into the thing that ends them.

## Hooks (GM)

- An epic "myth" a venturer dismisses turns out to be deep history the Elves can confirm — and a withdrawn god may still be sitting where the story says.
- A god-blooded bloodline or a relic of the walking gods is a prize and a peril.
- The forgotten Goddess is a slow-burn mystery across ages; surface it as fragments — the Elven phrase, a defaced shrine, a name no priest will say.
