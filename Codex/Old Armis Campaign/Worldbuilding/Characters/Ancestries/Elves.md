# The Elves — the Elder People

The first civilisation: the people who raised cities and walked the threshold of the Otherworld before humankind was anything at all. By the golden age the Elves are a conquered people, subjugated and assimilated into a younger empire — but they are old in a way the Empire can barely comprehend, and they tend monuments older than their own memory. Their defining mood is **twilight**: a people who know their dawn is behind them, keeping faith with a world the bright young Empire is paving over.

> **Cultural model:** the **Brythonic Celts** — Iron Age Britain by way of the Picts, the druids, and the Mabinogion's Otherworld — set as the *elder* race rather than a contemporary one.

---

## Deathless, and Remembering

The Elves are the **elder people** in the most literal sense: their long communion with the Otherworld reached into them and rewrote them into **deathless, ageless beings**. They do not die of years. This is why "they remember" is not metaphor — an Elf alive in the golden age may have *witnessed* the **[[Age of Heroes and the Withdrawal of the Gods|Age of Heroes]]** with their own eyes, watched the gods walk, and seen what humanity has since forgotten. They call humans, still, **"Her sons and daughters,"** a phrase worn so smooth by time that few even among the Elves recall what it once meant.

But their immortality is **Otherworld-fed, not innate** — and the spreading iron that seals the thresholds is, slowly, taking it back. The Elves are beginning to die of time again, by degrees, as the doors close: a diminishment they feel in their own bones and that no one else even perceives. See [[The Closing Doors]].

## Tribes, Not a Nation

The Elves never built the Empire's kind of order, and despise it a little. Their world is **tribal**: petty kings and chieftains ruling clan and *tuath*, cattle counted as wealth, bonds made by fosterage and marriage-alliance, justice kept by blood-feud and **honour-price**. They raise **hillforts and ringed duns**, not walled cities; roundhouses, not colonnades.

Above all they keep **everything by memory** and write nothing by choice. Law, genealogy, history, and rite live in trained minds across generations. To the literate Armisians this proves the Elves primitive. To the Elves, writing is how you let knowledge *die* — a thing you surrender to clay because you no longer trust it to mind. A people who refuse to write are a people the Empire finds it very hard to truly conquer, because their soul is not in any building it can burn.

## The Learned Orders

The spine of Elven society — and the thing the Empire most feared and most tried to destroy:

- **Druids** — judges, lawkeepers, star-readers of the seasons, and the ones who walk the threshold into the Otherworld. The highest authority among a free people who have no single king.
- **Bards** — keepers of memory and praise, and wielders of **satire as a weapon**: an Elven bard's curse-poem is a true magical attack, able to raise blemishes on an unjust lord, blight his name, and unmake his standing. A king fears his bard more than his enemies.
- **Ovates** — seers, healers, and readers of omen in birdflight, in water, and in the entrails of the offering.

## The Faith That Looks Like No Faith

To Armisian eyes the Elves are **godless** — no pantheon, no idols, no temples, no priest-banks, no civic gods. This is precisely how the Empire sees them, and precisely wrong. The Elves keep **Powers, not gods**:

- **The living land.** Every river has its mother, every spring its maiden, every grove its presence. You do not build a temple to the river; the river *is* the holy thing. Worship is the **sacred grove** (*nemeton*), the offering thrown into spring and bog, the reverence of oak and yew and ash, the fires lit on the turning-nights.
- **The ancestors** dwell in the **barrows**, present and honoured at the grass-mounds.
- **The Fae**, communed with and bargained with — never worshipped. See [[The Faewilds]].

An Armisian needs a face and a name on the holy. The Elves would say that is exactly why the Empire cannot see it.

## Woad — the Written Skin

A people who refuse ink for knowledge nonetheless write on **themselves**. The Elves mark their bodies in **blue woad tattoos** — spiral-and-knot patterns that are not mere war-paint but a sacred record: tribe and lineage, druidic rank, **fae-pacts sworn**, and protective glamour worked into the skin. The woad is grown in grove-gardens and laid in by rite; a druid can *read* a stranger's life and bargains in their markings. A fully-marked elder is a walking text in a culture that will not commit a word to clay — the one book the Elves permit themselves, and one the Empire cannot burn.

## Things Made and Worn

- **Bronze, gold, and stone** — never iron, which is taboo and worse (see below).
- **Torcs** of twisted gold at the throat, marks of rank; **spiral and knotwork** ornament on everything; checked and striped cloaks pinned at the shoulder; warriors who **lime-wash their hair** into pale spikes and go to battle behind the screaming **carnyx** war-horn and the light war-chariot.
- And everywhere in the old country, the **standing stones and barrows** — raised by hands older than living memory, aligned to the turnings of the year, and thinnest of all the places where the world touches the Otherworld.

## The Iron Dread

The Elves work no iron, and it is not only craft-conservatism. **Cold iron is anathema to the Fae** — it breaks glamour, repels the Good Neighbours, and seals the thresholds shut. To live alongside the Otherworld is to keep iron out. The deeper consequence of the Empire's spreading iron is the Elves' quiet catastrophe; see [[The Faewilds]] and [[The Closing Doors]].

## The Conquest — How the Elder People Fell

The Elf–Armis history follows Rome's breaking of the Celts, and the Elves have forgotten none of it:

- **Ynys Nemeth, the Martyred Isle.** To shatter druidic power, the Empire stormed the Elves' greatest sanctuary — a sacred grove-isle — and **massacred the druids** gathered there amid their burning oaks. It is the unhealed wound of the Elven soul, remembered in every recitation, named in every grievance.
- **Caradwen's Rising.** A great revolt under the war-queen **Caradwen** came within a hair of throwing the Empire out of the old country — and was crushed. She is legend and warning both: proof that the Empire *can* be beaten, and that the price of failing is annihilation.
- **The Long Assimilation.** Then the slow erosion. Most Elves became the imperial subjects of [[Venagillium]], trading the old ways for an imperial seat; a stubborn remnant kept faith at **Eldion**. Druidry was outlawed and driven to the margins — where, like everything the Elves keep, it lives on in memory and in the thin places the Empire never learned to find.

## What a Venturer Finds

- **A conquered elder people** — proud, oral, marked in blue, keeping a faith the Empire cannot even recognise as one.
- **Druids and bards** whose old powers (the satire-curse, the threshold-walk) are real, suppressed, and underestimated.
- **A grudge older than the Empire**, and a knowledge older than that.

## Hooks (GM)

- An Elven bard's satire, an honour-price feud, or a druid's threshold-rite can pull a venturer into a logic of obligation that runs nothing like imperial law.
- The memory of Ynys Nemeth and Caradwen is dry tinder; the wrong spark in the old country could light a second rising.
- The Elves know things — about the Fae, about the standing stones, about the turning of Ages — that no imperial scholar will credit until it is far too late.
