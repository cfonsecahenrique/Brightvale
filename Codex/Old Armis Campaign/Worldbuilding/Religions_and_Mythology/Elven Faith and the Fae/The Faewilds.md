# The Faewilds — the Otherworld

The Otherworld of the Elves: a realm of spirits and seasons that lies alongside our own and touches it at **thresholds**. The Elves call it by names an outsider is wiser not to repeat — *Annwn*, the Hidden Country, the world under the hill — and they do not worship its powers. They **bargain** with them, host them, fear them, and very occasionally walk in among them and come back changed, if they come back at all.

This is a magic of **bargain and glamour**, wholly unlike the Empire's planar [[Aetherweave Mastery|Aetherweave]] or [[An'Hiseret|An'Hiseret's]] necromancy — older than both, and answerable to neither.

---

## The Two Courts — the Turning Year

The Otherworld runs on the Celtic seasonal duality: the year is halved between two powers, and passes from one to the other on the great fire-festivals.

- **The Court of Summer** — growth, glamour, fae revels, the green and golden half of the year. Ruled by a **Summer Lord and May Queen**, beautiful and no safer for it.
- **The Court of Winter** — frost, the Hunt, the hungry dark. Ruled by the **Hag of Winter**, the old crone-power who holds the world in cold until her reign breaks.

The year turns between them at the cross-quarter nights, when the veil is thinnest:
- **Beltane** (the May fires) — Summer takes the year; the Fae come abroad; cattle are driven between bonfires for blessing.
- **Samhain** (the year's dark threshold) — Winter takes the year; the **dead and the Fae walk together**, and the doors stand widest of all.

(*Imbolc* and *Lughnasadh* mark the quarters between, lesser but observed.)

## The Powers and the Hunt

Not gods — **things you fear and bargain with**:
- The **Summer Lord and May Queen**; the **Hag of Winter**.
- **The Lord of the Hunt** — who rides the **Wild Hunt** across the night sky on the storm, his hounds baying, gathering up the souls of those caught out under it. To hear the Hunt is terror; to be ridden down by it is to be taken into the hill forever.
- Countless lesser folk: the river-mothers and spring-maidens, the lord of the green wood, the **Good Neighbours** of mound and hollow, the small folk of hearth and field.

## The Rules — Folklore Is Law Here

Every one of these is a genuine folk-belief and a trap at the table:

- **Bargains are binding and literal.** The Fae never lie — and deceive by truth. Read the exact words; they will be enforced exactly.
- **Names have power.** Give a use-name, never your true one. To know a thing's true name is to hold it.
- **Never thank them.** Thanks denies the debt and gives offence. Leave instead offerings of milk, bread, and honey.
- **Glamour rules the eye.** Fae gold is dead leaves by morning; the shining hall is a damp mound; the beautiful stranger is something else.
- **Time runs wrong.** A night of dancing in the hill can be a hundred years in the world. Many who returned came back to a world with no one left who knew them.
- **Hospitality is sacred — both ways.** Eat the food of the Otherworld and you may never wholly leave it.
- **Geasa.** A druid or hero may carry a *geas* — a sacred personal prohibition. Keep it and prosper; break it and doom comes, without appeal.

## The Thresholds

The Otherworld touches ours at **places**, not everywhere:
- **The barrows** — the grass-mounds of the dead are the fairy-mounds, the *sídhe*; the door under the hill.
- **The grove, the spring, the bog, the standing-stone ring** — every sacred Elven place is a thin place.
- **Eldion** above all, ringed in barrows and stones, where the veil is thinnest in all the old country.

The thresholds open widest on the festival nights — and a venturer who shelters in the wrong ring of stones on the wrong night may find the door has opened around them.

## Cold Iron — the Bane, and the Slow Closing

The one sure defence, and the seed of catastrophe: **cold iron breaks glamour, repels the Fae, and seals the thresholds.** A nail of iron over the door, a blade of iron in the hand, and the Good Neighbours cannot come — which is why the Elves who live alongside the Otherworld have always kept iron out, working bronze and gold and stone instead.

Which makes the Empire's **Iron Tide** a quiet apocalypse. As Dwarven iron spreads through the land — every blade forged, every nail in every new building — the veil thickens and the Fae withdraw. **The Faewilds are receding as iron advances**, the thresholds closing one by one, and the Empire is severing the world from the Otherworld without the faintest idea it is doing so. The druids of Eldion know. They cannot stop it, and no one with power will believe them. See [[The Closing Doors]].

## Hooks (GM)

- A fae bargain is the perfect double-edged reward: exactly what was asked for, enforced exactly, at a price read too late.
- A threshold opening on a festival night is a doorway into an adventure with its own rules — and its own clock, since time runs wrong on the far side.
- The receding of the Faewilds is dread you can *show*: a thin place gone dead, a Good Neighbour's hollow empty, an old druid grieving a door that won't open.
