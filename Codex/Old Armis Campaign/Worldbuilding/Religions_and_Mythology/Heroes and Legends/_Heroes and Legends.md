# Heroes & Legends of the Elder Days

The great tales of the **[[Age of Heroes and the Withdrawal of the Gods|Age of Heroes]]**: the god-blooded champions, the curses the gods laid and never lifted, and the monsters that outlived the heroes sent to kill them. Every Armisian child knows these stories. Every Armisian adult is fairly sure they are exaggerated.

They are not exaggerated enough.

> **The key thing to understand:** the heroes are dead or withdrawn. **The monsters are not.** When the gods stepped back from the world, they took their protection with them, but they left their curses, their servants, and their mistakes exactly where they lay. No one remains to lift the curse on the Bull-King, to call the Cyclopes home, or to forgive the Gorgon. These things are still out there, mostly in the far places the Empire's fleets sail past without stopping.

---

## How the Empire Tells Them

- **On the stage.** [[Thespia]] performs the great cycles every year at the Festival of Twin Faces. *The Bull-King* is the most-staged tragedy in the Empire; *The Smiling Thief* is the most-staged comedy. The masks in the Mask-Vaults are the faces most Armisians picture when they think of a hero, which is why most of those faces are wrong.
- **In oaths.** Soldiers swear *"by Alkaon's spear"*, merchants *"by the empty peg"*, sailors *"by Nerissa's horn"*. Nobody swears by the Gorgon.
- **In every city's own version.** Each patron city tells the tale of its own god's child as the central story and pushes the others into the margins. [[Venagillium]] says Daedrion was the greatest hero who ever lived. [[Triverium]] says it was Ereskes, and that Daedrion owed him money.
- **With educated contempt.** The Concord's scholars and the Aetherwrights of [[Pattis]] regard the monster-tales as provincial superstition, or at best as ordinary beasts grown large in the telling. This is why no imperial fleet has ever been sent against any of them. A civilisation that commands wind and sea does not trouble itself with a bull in a cellar. *(GM: this is hubris in miniature. Let it be wrong.)*

## The Great Tales

### I. The Laying-Down of Arms

When the gods withdrew, they asked the **first-generation demi-gods**, their own children, to step back as well. Mortals would never learn to stand while heroes stood over them.

The demi-gods obeyed. They sailed together to a bare island in the middle of the [[The Central Sea|Central Sea]], hung their weapons in a roofless sanctuary, and swore on [[Anaru]], lord of oaths, that they would never take them up again. Then they scattered into the bloodlines of the world and were not heroes any more.

The island is **[[Hoplonesos]]**, the Isle of Laid-Down Arms, and the weapons are still there, under guard. Some of the pegs are empty, and each empty peg has its own story.

### II. The Bull-King's Oath

**Asteros**, king of the cliff-town of **[[Ambrakis and the Horned Court|Ambrakis]]** on the [[The Titan's Sword|Titan's Sword]], prayed to [[Thalassos]] for a sign of favour. The sea-god sent him a white bull, walking up out of the surf, and Asteros vowed to sacrifice it at the next Opening of the Sea.

He kept the bull instead. It was the most beautiful thing he had ever owned.

Thalassos does not forgive broken vows. Asteros woke with horns. Within a year he was more bull than man. He was **deathless**, too, because a curse that ends is only a punishment. His queen begged **Daedrion the Maker** to build a prison around the palace so the king could not reach the town. Daedrion built it the only way he knew: as a puzzle with no answer.

Asteros is still in there. He still wears his crown, grown into the bone between his horns. On still nights the people of Ambrakis can hear him bellowing, and they swear the bellowing has words in it, the words of a vow. *(See **[[The Bull-King of the Horned Court]]**.)*

### III. The Labours and Death of Alkaon

**Alkaon the Unwearied**, son of [[Aetheon]], was the hero of common soldiers: a man with no throne, no fleet, and no patience for kings. His labours were done on foot and for hire. He strangled the **Lion of the Salt Flats** for a village that paid him in bread. He dragged the **Grey Boar of Thornfield** out of the Aldari forests alive, because the forest folk asked him not to kill it. He broke the **Bronze Watchman's** knee so that the fishermen of the southern cape could beach their boats, and the Watchman still limps.

His last labour was **[[The Hydra of Enneara|the Hydra]]**. Its island sat on the western sea-lane, and it ate everything that passed. Alkaon cut its heads for a day and a night. Each head he cut grew back as two, and its blood turned the shallows green. At the end of the second day he drove his spear into the rock of the landing-beach and wrapped himself around the shaft so that he would die standing.

He did not lay down his arms with the other demi-gods. He was already dead, and his spear, the **Ninefold Bane**, is still standing where he left it, on **[[Enneara]]**, with the Hydra coiled around the island behind it. Soldiers swear by that spear. None of them have gone to fetch it.

### IV. The Smiling Thief and the Blinded One

**Ereskes**, son of [[Kalvris]], was a liar, a thief, a charmer of widows, and the most beloved rogue in Armisian myth. In the oldest days, the story goes, only the gods and their servants had **the forge-fire**, the fire hot enough to work bronze. It burned in the volcanic isles of the **[[The Pyrrhai|Pyrrhai]]**, where the **[[The Cyclopes of the Pyrrhai|Cyclopes]]** worked it for [[Hevrek]].

Ereskes went there with a herd of stolen sheep and a jar of wine. He made friends with **Ourkon**, eldest of the Cyclopes, got him drunk, and when the giant slept he carried off a live ember in a hollow fennel-stalk. Ourkon woke as the thief was escaping. Ereskes put out his single eye with a burning stake, and he went laughing all the way to the boat.

The ember became the first forge-fire of mortals. It is said to burn still, unbroken, in the Forgehall of [[Venagillium]].

Ourkon is still alive, and still blind. He sits on the black beach of the Pyrrhai and listens for oars. Every sailor is a thief to him, and the Cyclopes have never again let a mortal leave their islands with anything. When the demi-gods laid down their arms at Hoplonesos, Ereskes hung up an **empty peg** and swore on it with a straight face. Merchants still swear "by the empty peg" when they mean they're lying.

### V. Nerissa and the Serpent-Mother

**Nerissa Wave-Born**, daughter of [[Thalassos]], was the first mortal to sail out of the Central Sea, past the Titan's Sword, and onto the open ocean. In the Azurian she met the **Serpent-Mother**, dam of every sea-drake that has hunted the western waters since. Nerissa did not fight her. She blew a horn made from a great spiral shell, and the sea went still, and the serpent was calm enough to bargain.

What Nerissa promised the Serpent-Mother in exchange for safe passage is not in any version of the story. The Pattisians say it was a song. The Elves, who were alive at the time, will not say.

**The Conch of Nerissa** is the one heroic relic the Empire openly holds. It rests in the undersea Temple of Thalassos at [[Pattis]] and is sounded once a year at the Opening of the Sea. The keepers of Hoplonesos insist it was *taken* from the Isle, not given, three centuries ago. Pattis insists it was "returned to its father's house". The argument has never been settled.

### VI. The Mother Who Prayed for Vengeance

**Ysmene** was a shepherd's wife in the hills behind [[Lissipos]]. A lord's hunting party rode through her pasture, and a horse trampled her child. The lord didn't stop. Ysmene climbed to the shrine of [[Kythera]], goddess of mothers and of vengeance, and prayed for one thing: *let them see what they have done.*

Kythera granted it. Everyone who met Ysmene's eyes saw exactly what they had done, every cruelty they had ever committed, all at once and forever, and turned to stone under the weight of it. She went down the mountain and petrified the lord and his entire household. Then she found she could not stop. Her hair writhed into serpents as her grief would not lie down, and anyone who met her eyes was turned.

She has lived alone in her valley ever since, in the **[[The Garden of Stone|Garden of Stone]]**, among the statues of everyone who came after her. Most of them came looking for her head. *(See **[[The Gorgon Ysmene]]**.)*

### VII. Phaedra's Pity

**Phaedra Moon-Shield**, daughter of [[Aelun]], went to kill the Gorgon with a polished bronze shield, meaning to look only at the reflection. It worked. She walked into the Garden backwards, raised the shield, and saw Ysmene clearly in it.

But a mirror shows a face truly, and what Phaedra saw was a mother weeping. She lowered the shield to speak to her.

She stands in the Garden still, one hand raised, her face turned toward the Gorgon, kinder than any statue ought to be. Her shield lies at her feet. Her peg at Hoplonesos is empty. Aelunos's priests call her story the truest one in the cycle, and its lesson is not the one heroes want to hear.

### VIII. Daedrion the Maker

**Daedrion**, son of [[Hevrek]], learned his craft at the anvils of the Cyclopes before Ereskes's theft soured the islands against mortals. He built the maze of the Horned Court, the **Bronze Watchmen** that guarded the coasts, and the wings he wore when he fled the Pyrrhai after the theft, because the Cyclopes blamed the god's own son. One wing broke in the crossing. He hung the other at Hoplonesos.

Venagillium's Smiths' Guild claims descent from his apprentices and keeps a "Daedrion's tool" in every master's workshop. Most of them are fakes. One of them isn't.

## Lesser Legends & Rumours

Seeds for the GM. Each one is a tale someone in a harbour tavern will swear to, and each has a place where it might still be true.

- **The Bronze Watchman** limps along the beaches of the Titan's Sword's southern cape, a giant of hollow bronze that throws boulders at any ship that does not dip its sail to him. Alkaon broke his knee. Nobody has finished the job.
- **The Sphinx of the High Pass.** **Anthea**, daughter of [[Setharis]], once answered its riddle. It has been waiting a very long time for a better one. Smugglers on the [[The Eight Forts|Eight Forts]]' highest crossing pass a riddle down between generations, and it still gets them through. Mostly.
- **The Grey Boar of Thornfield.** Alkaon spared it at the forest folk's request. The [[Aldari]] of the march say it is still alive, still enormous, and that you can bring down a kingdom's luck by killing it.
- **The Chained Rock off [[Lyx]]**, where a princess of the Age of Heroes was bound as an offering to a sea-beast. The legend says she was rescued. The chains are still there, bright and unrusted, and they are warm.
- **The singing shoals of the [[The Hesperine Isles|Hesperine Isles]].** In Hannor's chronicle they are singing fish. Older sailors' tales describe women's voices and no women, and crews who row toward the song without stopping.
- **The Snatchers of the Aelunos cliffs.** These are winged things with women's faces that take the sleepless from the cliff-paths. The priests of [[Aelunos]] call them a visitation and decline to say anything more.
- **The gold-guarding griffins** of the [[The Eastern Steppes|Eastern Steppes]], which the Orcenfolk say are real, are ill-tempered, and taste of hawk.
- **The tomb-Lamia of [[An'Hiseret]]**, older than the death-cult, a thing the God-Kings walled in rather than raised.

## The Truth Beneath the Tales (GM)

The monsters of the Elder Days come in **three kinds**, and the in-world tellings blur them together:

1. **The Cursed.** These are mortals remade by a god's wrath: Asteros, Ysmene. Divine curses do not lapse, and when the gods withdrew nobody was left with the authority to lift them. These monsters are *people*. They remember, they suffer, and a few of them can be reasoned with. Ending their curses (not their lives) is the most heroic thing a venturer of this age could do, and it may require reaching a **withdrawn god** at [[The Edges of the World|the edges of the world]] to ask.
2. **The Abandoned Servants.** These are beings made to serve the gods in their works: the Cyclopes at Hevrek's forge, the Bronze Watchmen, the Sphinx at its pass. They were never dismissed. They are still at their posts, guarding a god who doesn't come home, and they have become strange and bitter over the long wait.
3. **The Spawn of the Deep.** After bearing her five dragons, [[Tiamat]] brought forth **lesser brood**, monsters of sea and serpent without the dragons' world-shaping minds. The Hydra and the Serpent-Mother are among them. The Ziramati know this and call them *"the second birth."* To the Armisians they are simply the things the sea makes. These are not people. They are pieces of a primordial, and they feed on **Aether**.

**Why it matters now:**
- **The Awakening fed the deep spawn.** Since 744 AF, Aether-driven hulls have been crossing waters the spawn have hunted since before the gods walked. The Hydra has grown. The serpents have grown bolder. The Empire blames the sea.
- **The god-blood still runs.** The demi-gods "scattered into the bloodlines." Some relics answer only to a descendant, and some curses can be broken only by one. A player character may well be the thinned great-grandchild of Alkaon or Ereskes, and the Anax of [[Armis-Caen]] is *certainly* a descendant of someone.
- **The oath at Hoplonesos is a hinge.** The demi-gods swore never to take up arms again. Whoever takes those weapons down breaks the first promise humanity's champions made to the withdrawn gods, and so takes the first step on a road that ends with mortals making war on their makers. *(See [[Apostasy]].)*
