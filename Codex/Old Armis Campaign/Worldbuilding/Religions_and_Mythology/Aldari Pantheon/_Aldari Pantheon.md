# Aldari Pantheon

The faith of the **[[Aldari]]** — a **Continental Germanic** tradition of earth-goddess, sacred grove, and ancestor. Older in memory than the Empire, and largely incomprehensible to Armisian eyes: no marble temples, no haruspex, no civic cult. A faith practiced in **grove sanctuaries**, spoken in verse, and kept by **clan-mothers** who hold the old ways.

> **Flavour:** matrilineal sacred authority, sacred groves not temples, the dead in the earth rather than judged in an underworld, seasonal rites that blur into the Elven tradition at their edges.

> **Cultural inspiration (GM):** Continental Germanic paganism — Tacitus's *Germania*, the Matronae cults of the Rhine, sacred grove traditions. Distinct from the Norse flavour of [[_Athregali Pantheon|Athregal's faith]].

---

## The Goddess

The divine centre — the one the **[[The Armisian Empire|Empire]]** calls simply *"the Goddess"* because she stands alone above the rest.

Among the Aldari, she is **Elda** — the Elder, the Ancient, the land herself given presence. Not a creator-goddess in the sense that Armisian [[Anaru]] created the order of things, but older than creation: she *is* the earth, the hill, the deep grove, the roots that predate any planting. Where she walks, things grow; where she rests, the land holds the dead in memory.

Her attributes:

- **Earth and sovereignty.** The land belongs to Elda; every Aldari clan holds it on loan from her, never in ownership. This is law as much as theology — a chief who squanders the land transgresses against the Goddess, and may be deposed on those grounds.
- **The turning of the year.** She governs the seasonal cycle, and the four great turning-feasts of the Aldari year mark her passage through the seasons — planting, midsummer, harvest, and the long dark.
- **The dead and the memory.** The dead go *into* the earth, into Elda. Aldari burial is always in the ground; they refuse the Empire's practice of pyre-burning, which would scatter the dead out of her keeping. The honored dead are not gone — they rest with Elda, and may be spoken to at the barrows on the feast nights.
- **Clan-oath and bond.** Oaths sworn in Elda's name are sworn before the earth itself. Where an Armisian oath-taker invokes [[Anaru]] with fire and wine, an Aldari swears kneeling with one hand pressed flat to the ground. Breaking such an oath is, in Aldari understanding, a thing the earth *remembers*.

**Sacred places:** No temples. Elda is present in **grove sanctuaries** — clearings in old forest, maintained by the Eldamothren, marked with standing stones or woven-branch constructions that are remade each year. **[[Eichenholt]]** is her most sacred seat in the known world, tended jointly by Aldari elders and their elven kin.

**Priests:** The **Eldamothren** (*"Elda-mothers"*) — the clan-mothers and elder-women who tend the groves, speak the seasonal rites, and hold religious authority. They are not a separate caste; the eldest woman of each clan *is* its Eldamothren. The Armisian eye finds it disorienting to encounter no male priests, no civic temple hierarchy, no oracle-at-a-distance who can be consulted and taxed.

## The Lesser Powers

Where Elda is the sovereign and the earth, the lesser powers of the Aldari faith are her children, her aspects, or the forces she sets loose on the world. They are venerated but never placed above her.

| Power | Domains | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Waldher** *(the Green Lord)* | Hunt, forest, the wild, the beast | Elda's consort in the warm season; the untamed world's personification. He is present but not sovereign — and disappears into the dark half of the year, which the Aldari mark as a mourning. |
| **Grasberin** *(the Bone-Keeper)* | The honored dead, barrows, memory | Tends the dead in Elda's earth; summoned at grave-rites and the winter ancestor-feast. He does not judge the dead — the Armisian notion of soul-weighing at [[Morvath]]'s threshold is alien to the Aldari. Grasberin only *keeps*. |
| **Donnershand** *(the Storm-Son)* | War, thunder, lightning | Elda's most rebellious child — storms and battle are his. Aldari war-chiefs invoke Donnershand before a raid; the clan-mother still has the final word on whether the war is righteous. Thunder without her blessing is just noise. |
| **The Spinners** | Fate, the thread of a life, death-in-its-time | Three ancient women who sit at Elda's feet and weave — or cut — the threads of lives. They are observed rather than worshipped; the Aldari do not pray to the Spinners for fate-change, only to Elda to be granted a worthy thread to run. |

## Character of the Faith

**The earth is sacred, not a resource.** Elda's ownership of the land is not poetry — it is obligation. Aldari culture does not sell land. The Empire's annexation of [[Orierba]] and extension of Armisian deed-law over Aldari territory is, in Aldari theology, not just conquest but *blasphemy* — treating as chattel what belongs to the Goddess.

**The dead are present.** Ancestor-communion is regular practice: speaking to the barrow-dead on feast nights, leaving offerings, consulting the honored dead on clan decisions of weight. The Bone-Keeper tends them; the clan-mother speaks to them. The Armisian practice of burying the dead in city necropoli — sorted, managed, walled off — strikes the Aldari as a deliberate effort to silence the ancestors.

**Women hold the sacred.** The Eldamothren are the faith's authorities; male war-chiefs hold secular power, but the clan-mother's word on sacred matters is final. An Aldari man who dismisses an Eldamothren's ruling is declaring himself impious — a serious social wound in a clan-and-oath society.

**Elf-faith and Aldari faith blur at the edges.** After centuries of coexistence and intermarriage, the traditions have bled into each other — the grove at [[Eichenholt]] is tended jointly, and the boundary between Elda's dead-in-the-earth and the Fae of the Otherworld is not always clear. Both traditions find meaning in the threshold, the sacred season-turn, the living land. Neither claims the overlap is wrong. The Elves quietly note that the Aldari are the only humans who have *never* closed the old paths.

## Why the Empire Does Not Understand It

The Armisian eye looks for a temple, a civic cult, a pantheon with clear hierarchy and a presiding deity-statue. The Aldari offer none of these. Their faith looks to a conqueror like **no faith at all**: no market, no oracle, no formal sacrifice they can tax or co-opt into civic religion. The Empire tends to describe the Aldari as **superstitious** or **nature-worshippers** — and in doing so, consistently misunderstands the sacred weight of their land-tenure, their ancestor-culture, and their oath-systems.

This is a political failure as much as a cultural one. Every Armisian deed-title to a grove is a heresy. The Aldari remember every one.

## Hooks (GM)

- **Blasphemy by deed-law.** Every Armisian land-sale in Aldari territory is, to the Aldari, a sacrilege that accumulates. This is fuel for the eventual uprising, and the Eldamothren are tracking the count.
- **The Eldamothren as intelligence network.** Grove-keepers travel between clans freely, pass information, and are not obviously combatants — they are the Aldari's covert infrastructure. An [[The Armisian Empire|Empire]] that doesn't recognise female religious authority won't notice them as a threat until it's too late.
- **Eichenholt as a crisis point.** The joint Aldari-elf grove is sacred to both peoples; threatening or desecrating it will unite them in a way no military campaign has managed.
- **The Bone-Keeper and displaced ancestors.** A ruined Aldari barrow in Armisian territory means displaced honored dead — a wrong that demands redress in Aldari law. Families do not forget.
- **Waldher's disappearance.** The Green Lord is absent in winter, which the Aldari mark as a time of mourning. A prolonged absence — an endless winter, a dying forest — would be read as Elda's grief made manifest. Something has gone wrong.

## The Erde Synthesis — After the Sacking of Armis-Caen (1144 AF onward)

When the Aldari sack [[Armis-Caen]] and found the Realm of [[Ezaro]] in 1145 AF, their faith enters a new phase — transforming from the religion of a highland tribe into the dominant spiritual force of half the former Empire.

### The Terra Merger

The key is [[Ezaro]], the Aldari's chosen capital. Ezaro was already the seat of the Armisian goddess **[[Terra]]** — harvest, earth, seasons, grief, endurance. Her Ezarian devotees and the Aldari Eldamothren find each other remarkably legible: both venerate an earth-mother who governs the seasons and holds the dead; both locate the sacred in the ground rather than in heaven.

The merger is not imposed or decreed. It is organic — Eldamothren walk into Terra's stone temples and find the iconography halfway familiar; Ezarian priestesses encounter the grove-practices of the conquerors and recognize something older than their own tradition, not alien to it. Within a generation, joint rites are common. Within two, separate identities are blurring.

The linguistic trace is the faith's new name: **Erde** — the Aldari *Elda* and the Imperial *Terra* folding into a single word that is neither and both. It appears first in common speech, then in hymns, and eventually in formal Eldamothren usage.

### Demotion of the Armisian Pantheon

Over the 250 years of the Cold Centuries, the eleven remaining gods of the [[_Armisian Pantheon|Armisian Pantheon]] are gradually reinterpreted — not by edict, but through a hundred local accommodations between temple-priests and Eldamothren, between Ezarian laypeople and Aldari settlers.

Three schools of interpretation emerge, and all three coexist:

- **The Servant school** — the other gods are real, but they serve Erde as Waldher and Donnershand serve Elda. [[Thalassos]] is Erde's voice on the water; [[Aetheon]] is her arm in battle; [[Kalvris]] her foot in the marketplace. Their temples stand and their names are spoken, but every rite ends with acknowledgment of the one from whom all flows.
- **The Envoy school** — the gods are divine messengers sent by Erde into the world to tend her domains. They are her hands, not independent powers. Popular among former Armisian civic priests: it preserves the structure of temple life while repositioning authority upward.
- **The Facets school** — the gods are expressions of Erde's singular divine nature, not separate beings but the goddess seen through different lenses. The most theologically radical reading, trending toward outright monotheism; most common in the Aldari heartland, least welcome among former Armisian elites who liked their patron gods distinct and personal.

The result across the Cold Centuries is a functional **henotheism** — one supreme deity acknowledged above all others — trending toward monotheism in its most devout expressions.

The hard exception: **[[Morvath]]**, god of death and soul-judgment. His cult conflicts directly with Elda's foundational claim — the dead go *into the earth*, not to judgment at a threshold. Morvath's temples are the last to be absorbed into the Erdean framework, the least successfully, and his cult quietly persists at the margins of the Realm in forms the Eldamothren prefer not to examine too closely.

### The Pattisian Contrast

Across the peace line, **[[Pattis]]** retains the old Armisian pantheon and wears its identity as the Empire's legitimate heir. But its relationship with its gods grows increasingly hollow as its mastery of the [[Aetherweave Mastery|Aetherweave]] deepens.

A people who can *command* the wind no longer needs to pray for it. A navy powered by planar Weave does not propitiate [[Thalassos]] before departure — or does so as a formality, a civic tradition emptied of urgency. By the mid-Cold Centuries, Pattisian religion is largely civic ritual: the priests are functionaries, the temples are monuments to what the faith once was, and the educated quietly believe that gods, if real, are simply very powerful entities — no different in kind from what the Amun-Orb's immortals are becoming.

This is not atheism. It is something more dangerous: the sincere conviction that divinity is an *achievement*, not a gift. That the gods are simply mortals who got there first. That the gap, with sufficient mastery, is closable.

This belief is the road to the **Pattisian Apotheosis**.

### The Waking of the Gods — The Race (~1395 AF)

The **slaying of [[Azhdashar]]** is the trigger.

When Pattisian Aetherweavers kill the living god-queen of Ziramat and harvest her primal core to fuel the Apotheosis, the Aldari Realm feels it before any political intelligence confirms it. Across three nights, the barrow-dead go quiet — no voice at the feast, no warmth at the stones. The grove-fires dim without wind. The Eldamothren who tend the deepest sanctuaries report that something in the Aetherweave has changed: a note that was there is gone, as if a string on a great instrument has been cut.

The theological read is immediate: if Pattis can slay a god and eat her power, no god is safe. The dormant gods that old Armisian expedition charts mapped at sites across the known world are the next harvest targets.

The **Great Grove-Council at [[Eichenholt]]** convenes — the largest gathering of Eldamothren in living memory, joined by their elven kin who have their own reading of the disturbance in the Otherworld. The decision is unanimous: *send the most devout to find the dormant gods, wake them, and warn them before Pattis gets there first.*

Pattis, meanwhile, has access to the same Armisian expedition archives. Their Aetherweavers are reading the same charts — not to wake what they find, but to reach it and take it apart.

The race begins.

## Hooks (GM) — The Waking of the Gods

*A campaign frame for the final era before the [[Apostasy]]. Player characters may be placed on either side of the race — or may switch.*

**Playing as Pattisian agents:**
- Reach dormant god-sites from the expedition charts and extract divine essence — or prevent a waking entirely.
- The moral weight is real: these sites hold something *present*, capable of speech or response. Players are sent to harvest or destroy it.
- Not all dormant gods are benevolent. Some may be willing to deal with Pattis on their own terms — which may not be what the Aetherweavers expected.
- Success feeds the Apotheosis; failure gives the Aldari one more divine ally.

**Playing as Aldari/Ezarian agents:**
- Reach the same sites and wake the gods — then recruit them to the coming war, or at minimum get them out of Pattis's reach.
- Waking a sleeping god is its own danger: not every god will be grateful, lucid, or on the right side. Some are maddened by centuries of dormancy; some will need convincing that the world they remember still exists in any form worth defending.
- The Eldamothren send not just warriors but priests — the persuasion matters as much as the arrival.
- Pattisian operatives race them to every site.

**Cross-cutting complications:**
- Some gods may side with Pattis, or refuse all sides, or have interests incompatible with either.
- [[Ziramat]] is broken, its surviving Dragonkin leaderless after Azhdashar's slaying. Both sides want their allegiance. The Dragonkin have reasons to despise both.
- **Erde herself** is the theological engine of the Aldari side — but does she act directly? Does she speak through the Eldamothren, or withhold? Does she have her own agenda beyond what the Eldamothren are told? *This is a dial the GM controls.*
- Sites on the expedition charts may have been reached already by tomb-raiders, independent cults, or remnant [[An'Hiseret|An'Hiseretian]] deathless who have their own uses for dormant divinity.
