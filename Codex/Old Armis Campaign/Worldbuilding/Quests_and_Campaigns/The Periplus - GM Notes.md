# The Periplus of Hannor the Far-Sailed — GM Notes

The in-world chronicle of the known world — and the voice we write it in. A *periplus* is a sailor's account of a voyage around the coasts, and this one is the life's work of **Hannor the Far-Sailed**, a merchant-traveller of [[Pattis]] who claims to have set foot on every shore an Armisian ship can reach and a few it cannot.

> This file establishes the **narrator and frame**. The chronicle proper is written *in Hannor's voice*; the rest of the lore vault is the GM's true reference behind it.

---

## The Author

**Hannor the Far-Sailed** — a Pattisian of the Salt Quarter, merchant by trade and wanderer by temperament. Not a scholar of the archives like the dry men of [[Himilkos]]; a man who *went*. He has sailed the Western Voyages, drunk in the taverns of Zergas, haggled with Dwarves and (he swears) with dragon-folk, walked the barrow-roads of the Elven north, and stood — he says — within sight of the [[The Hesperine Isles|Hesperine Isles]]. He writes for other merchants, for venturers, and for the glory of having seen it.

## His Voice

Pure Herodotus-at-sea. Hannor is:
- **Curious and generous** — he likes the peoples he describes, even the Empire's enemies, and grants them their dignity more readily than the Admiralty would.
- **A lover of marvels** — he never met a wonder he wouldn't write down, and he reports the tall tale alongside the true one, often unable to tell which is which. *"I did not see this myself, but a man of Zergas swore to me…"* is his favourite sentence.
- **Proudly, blithely Armisian-Pattisian** — and so wrong, with total confidence, about exactly the things the Empire is wrong about.

## Why This Voice Is the Design

Hannor's confident errors are the chronicle's secret engine. He "knows" things the GM knows are false, and states them as plain fact:
- that the **Elves are a godless, declining people** (he cannot see that they remember the gods better than anyone);
- that the **[[Adamantis|Adamantis]]** is a rich rock of strange ore (he has no idea it is a grave);
- that the **gods are old stories** the temples keep for luck (he does not know they are alive and watching from the world's edges);
- that the **golden age is the dawn of an endless ascent** (it is the evening of one).

Every GM-secret in this vault can hide, in plain sight, inside something Hannor says with a smile. The reader who knows more than Hannor does is exactly the reader we are writing for. **Let him be charming, and let him be wrong.**

## The Chronicle's Structure

A periplus follows the coasts. Hannor's runs, roughly:
1. **The Making of the World** — cosmography and the gods, as Hannor understands them.
2. **The Elder Times and the First Cities** — the Elves, An'Hiseret, the northern founding.
3. **The Rise of Armis and the Golden Age** — the Awakening, the conquest of the south, Armis-Caen and Pattis at their height.
4. **The Peoples of the World** — Elves, Orcenfolk, Dwarves, Ziramati, and others.
5. **The Western Reaches** — the Titan's Sword, the colonies, Athregal, Ziramat, Adamantis.
6. **The Edges & the Marvels** — the Hesperine Isles, the blank south, beasts and wonders.
