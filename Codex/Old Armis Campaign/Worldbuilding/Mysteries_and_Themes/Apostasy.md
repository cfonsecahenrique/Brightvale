# The Apostasy

**The doom at the end of the age** — the cataclysm that ends the **[[The Armisian Empire|Armisian Empire]]**, breaks the cycle of death, looses the **Umbra**, and leaves the shattered, dark-drowned world of the *Brightvale* setting as its inheritance.

> **GM-only.** This is the gravity well the whole prequel falls toward. The players are not meant to know it; the GM is. Foreshadow it with **hubris and dependence**, never with prophecy. Never name it at the table.

## What This Campaign Is the Prologue To
This golden age is the Empire **at its apex** — which makes it the story of how the fall *begins*. The proud cities the venturers sail between become the tombs the Venturers of Brightvale will one day loot; the Armisian coin a player earns becomes a curio in a dying valley. (See **[[The Vale to Come]]** for the one place that survives.)

## The True Shape of the Doom — the Century of Blood
The Apostasy is **not** an accident, a decay, or an unexplained abandonment. It is a **war, and mortals start it.** And it does not begin in 994 AF — it begins with the Empire's own ruin, four centuries later. (See **[[Timeline (Armis)]]** for the full dated arc; this note gives the shape, not the schedule.)

The gods did not leave the world; they **withdrew**, granting mortals the freedom to make their own fate. That gift becomes the murder weapon, in three stages:

1. **The Dark Reign (1044–1144 AF).** Armisian elites first turn the **[[The Amun-Orb|Amun-Orb]]** toward *personal* immortality, not godhood — a century of deathless lords feeding on **[[The Contested Isle|Adamantis]]** residuum while common soldiers still age and die. The strain it puts on the Empire is what actually breaks it: civil war splits **[[Pattis]]** from **[[Armis]]**–**[[Caen]]**, and the conquered **[[Aldari]]** sack Armis-Caen outright, founding their own realm at **[[Ezaro]]**.
2. **The Bid for Divinity (~1395 AF).** Two successor empires — Pattis and the Aldari Realm of Ezaro — grow for 250 wary years, kept in check by the Orb's own dual edge: it can *unmake* immortality as readily as grant it. Then Pattis's Aetherweavers, having fed the Orb the slain **[[Azhdashar]]**'s primal core and **[[The Eye of the Desert]]** alongside Adamantis residuum, stop wanting mere immortality and start wanting **apotheosis** — to seize divinity and fill the empty thrones of the withdrawn gods.
3. **The Sinking of Pattis.** Warned by the Aldari, who use old Armisian expedition charts to wake the gods mapped dormant across the world, the gods strike first: they **sink Pattis**, the moving city they raised. The gift, drowned by its givers, is the spark. Heaven strikes; mortals answer.
4. **The Century of Blood (1395–1495 AF).** A hundred years of war between mortals and immortals. **Pattis** (humanity, the Bid) against the **Aldari Realm of [[Ezaro]]** (the gods' chosen warners, now the gods' army) — and the free cities and subjugated nations fall in on both sides. Aether-empowered mortals fight gods directly; the demi-gods choose sides, **some against their own kind**; and **Amun-Atir, the Death God of [[An'Hiseret]]**, is *erased from existence* with the loosed soul-unmaking knowledge (see **[[The Return of Xol-amun]]**). Into the wounds of a world at war with its gods, the **Umbra** — the entropy the death-cycle always held in check — begins to seep.
5. **The Apostasy (1495 AF / 0 AA).** The end-state: the gods are **killed or driven from the world**, the death-cycle breaks for good, both successor empires are shattered, and the **Age of Umbra** begins. Refugee nobility from *both* Pattis and Ezaro converge on the surviving Vale — which is why the Brightvale era's Old Houses carry a mix of Mediterranean-Pattisian and Germanic-Aldari blood and naming alike.

> This is the true answer to the *Brightvale* mystery — *"the Gods departed, for reasons no record explains."* They did not depart. **They were made war upon, and they fell.** No record survives because the age that knew it did not.

## The Fault Lines Already in the Foundations
Foreshadow the doom through the cracks, not a calendar:
- **The Weave is a crutch the whole civilisation leans on** — [[Pattis]] floats on it, the fleets fly on it. Let small failures flicker; let no one in power take them seriously.
- **The residuum is a dead god's blood** — and the likeliest road to godhood. Every voyage to **[[The Contested Isle]]** is a step toward the Bid. (See **[[Primordial Dragons]]**.)
- **The god-killing weapon already exists** — the An'Hiseretian art of truly unmaking a bound divine soul, loosed and scattered. (See **[[The Return of Xol-amun]]**.)
- **An'Hiseret already taught the world to hold death open** — a literal early tremor of the cycle's collapse. (See **[[An'Hiseret]]**.)
- **The doors to the Otherworld are closing** — the spreading iron seals the thresholds to the Fae, severing an old protection before it is needed. (See **[[The Closing Doors]]**.)

## Hooks (GM)
- The Apostasy is a **gravity well, not a plot point.** Let the wonders be genuine and the appetites thrilling, and let the player feel, underneath, that this is a height from which the only way is down.
- Reward players for **glimpsing** the danger; never reward them with the whole truth.
- **Dials:** how the Bid actually works; why the gods sink [[Pattis]] specifically; which gods are killed, which driven out, and which (a veiled goddess?) endure to stir again in the Brightvale age.
