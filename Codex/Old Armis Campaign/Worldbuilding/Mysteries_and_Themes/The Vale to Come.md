# The Vale to Come

The geographic and dramatic link between this campaign and the post-Apostasy world of *Brightvale*: an overlooked mountain valley, and the unremarkable village in it that becomes the **last refuge of the world to come**.

> **GM-only.** Dramatic irony, never exposition. Plant the geography, not the prophecy. Never name it at the table.

## The Secret
The nested mountain valley **west of [[Venagillium]]**, and the small village of **[[Medessos]]** that sits in it, are the future **Brightvale** — the last lit refuge of the **[[Apostasy|Umbra-drowned age]]** that follows.

**Medessos becomes Medsen.** The name erodes across the centuries the way the world does: *Medessos → Medsen*. The terraced mountain village of a few score households becomes the great walled refuge-city, seat of its Council and its dimming Beacon — the last place the light still holds.

## Why It Survives
When the **[[Apostasy]]** comes and the Umbra drowns the world, the proud cities die first — **[[Pattis]]** sinks, the heartland burns, the sea-lanes go dark. The places that endure are the ones **too high, too small, and too far** from the great powers to be worth the world's attention: a ringed, spring-fed, defensible valley holding a stubborn, self-sufficient people who never depended on the Weave the way the cities did. Everything that makes Medessos forgettable in 994 AF makes it the **seed of the next age.** The vale was never important. That is precisely why it lasts.

## Hooks (GM)
- If the players pass through **[[Medessos]]** — rest there, help its people, learn its name — let them feel it as a quiet human interlude. A player who has stood in the village and later realises what it *becomes* has received one of the best gifts this setting can give.
- **Dial:** does anyone in the golden age suspect the vale's significance — a druid who reads the thresholds, an archivist who knows how ages end — or is its survival pure, blind luck?
