# Hoplonesos

*"The Isle of Laid-Down Arms."* A small, sheer-sided island in the open middle of the [[The Central Sea|Central Sea]], claimed by no city and taxed by no empire. When the gods withdrew, the first-generation **demi-gods** came here, hung their weapons in a roofless sanctuary, and swore on [[Anaru]] never to take them up again. The weapons are still here. So are their keepers. *(The tale: **[[_Heroes and Legends#I. The Laying-Down of Arms|The Laying-Down of Arms]]**.)*

## The Island
- **A single landing.** Cliffs ring Hoplonesos on every side but one, a narrow stair cut into the rock above a stone quay just large enough for two ships.
- **The Hall Without a Roof.** On the island's crown stands a long colonnade of weathered marble open to the sky, with no walls and no roof, so that the gods (wherever they've gone) can see the oath is kept. Between the columns hang the **arms of the heroes** on bronze pegs, each peg inscribed with a name in a script older than any living alphabet.
- **Untouched by weather.** In two ages of salt wind the hanging arms have not rusted, tarnished or rotted. Bowstrings are still taut. Shields still gleam. The scholars of [[Himilkos]] write papers on why. The keepers consider the question impolite.

## What Hangs There
There are perhaps forty pegs. A few of the most famous:
- **Anaktoros**, the bronze spear of the first Anax of Armis, son of Anaru. Every new Anax of [[Armis-Caen]] must sail here and lay a hand on its shaft before being crowned, which is the only reason the throne pays the keepers any attention at all. The spear carried in Armisian state processions is a copy, and everyone in the capital pretends not to know it.
- **The Grieving Bow** of Kallira, daughter of [[Kythera]], strung with her own hair.
- **One bronze wing** of **Daedrion the Maker**. Its pair broke in the crossing from the Pyrrhai.
- **Ereskes's peg**, empty from the day of the oath. He swore on it with a straight face. Merchants swear "by the empty peg" when they mean they are lying.
- **Phaedra's peg**, empty because she never came back from the [[The Garden of Stone|Garden of Stone]]. Her shield is still there.
- **Nerissa's peg**, empty for three centuries, since the Conch "returned to its father's house" at [[Pattis]]. The keepers call it theft. Pattis calls it piety.
- **No peg for Alkaon.** He was dead before the oath. His spear still stands at [[Enneara]].
- **A handful of empty pegs no one can explain.** The keepers have their suspicions. *(GM: some were reclaimed by the [[The Cyclopes of the Pyrrhai|Cyclopes]] who forged them. One or two were taken by heroes of later ages, and may yet turn up in a tomb, a treasury, or a pirate's hold on [[Borkum Reef]].)*

## The Keepers
A small hereditary order, the **Oath-Keepers**, has guarded the arms since the day of the oath. They claim descent from the demi-gods' own squires. They are few, ascetic, and stubborn. They are armed with plain iron and bronze and are formidable with both. They will feed and shelter any pilgrim and let them walk the colonnade. They will let **no one** take anything down. In their view anyone who takes up a laid-down weapon breaks the demi-gods' oath and inherits the wrath that falls on oath-breakers, as Asteros of [[Ambrakis and the Horned Court|Ambrakis]] did.

## Hooks (GM)
- **A pilgrimage of state.** The party escorts an Anax-designate, or a pretender, to touch the Anaktoros. Not everyone wants the coronation to happen.
- **A theft.** A peg was emptied last night. The keepers are certain it was not the Cyclopes. Someone in the Empire wants a hero's weapon, and has decided the oath doesn't apply to them.
- **A request.** A dying keeper asks the party to return something taken centuries ago, beginning with the Conch, and Pattis will not give it up gladly.
- **The question no one asks (GM).** The demi-gods laid down their arms so that mortals could stand on their own. When the Empire comes to want godhood for itself *(see [[Apostasy]])*, the arms of Hoplonesos will be among the first things it reaches for. The oath was the first promise humanity's champions made to the withdrawn gods, and breaking it will be the first step of the Century of Blood.
