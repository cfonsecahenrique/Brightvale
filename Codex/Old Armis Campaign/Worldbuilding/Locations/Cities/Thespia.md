# Thespia

A city of the **[[The Armisian Empire|Armisian Empire]]**, on the **southeastern coast** of the **[[The Central Sea]]** near **[[Aelunos]]** and **[[Lissipos]]** — the youngest patron city in the Empire, and proud of exactly that.

## Overview
- **Patron of [[Thespis]].** Thespia's one temple belongs to Thespis, the newest god of the [[_Armisian Pantheon|Armisian Pantheon]] — a cult barely two centuries old, and Thespia's elevation to patron city is almost as recent. Older cities consider this faintly presumptuous. Thespia's answer, delivered from a stage, is usually funnier than the complaint deserved, which tends to end the argument.
- **A city built around its amphitheatre.** Where other patron cities organise themselves around a temple in the conventional sense, Thespia's sacred centre *is* its great open amphitheatre, tiered into a natural hillside facing the sea — the temple proper is a modest structure backstage, more workshop and mask-vault than sanctuary. The performance is the rite. Everything else is preparation for it.
- **The Mask-Vaults.** Beneath the amphitheatre, a warren of cool stone chambers holds the city's collection of ceremonial masks — some centuries old, each one used for a specific role in a specific cycle of plays, and each treated with the reverence other cities reserve for a cult idol. A mask is never destroyed, only retired to the deepest vault when its wood finally fails.
- **The Festival of Twin Faces.** Thespia's great annual rite, timed to coincide with the turn from the sailing season to the storm season, stages the year's tragedies and comedies back to back, from dusk until dawn, for the whole city and any pilgrim who has made the journey. To fall asleep during the tragedies is considered merely human. To fall asleep during the comedies is considered a small, forgivable insult to the god.
- **Playwrights as clergy.** Thespis's priesthood inducts its members through apprenticeship to a working playwright rather than any formal seminary — a structure the older cults find bewildering and the god, by all accounts, finds exactly right.

- **The great cycles.** The Festival's fixed repertoire is the **[[_Heroes and Legends|Elder Days cycle]]**. *The Bull-King* is the most-staged tragedy in the Empire and *The Smiling Thief* the most-staged comedy. *Phaedra's Pity* is performed only once a generation, because the priests say it is too true to be safe. No play about the Gorgon herself has ever been staged in Thespia.

## Hooks (GM)
- **A new play, written for this year's Festival, contains something its playwright swears they did not intend to put in it** — a detail, a name, a prophecy that turns out to be true. Thespis's priests take this more seriously than outsiders expect.
- **The Mask-Vaults** hold centuries of the Empire's dramatic history, including plays about events other cities would rather forget were ever staged. A banned or suppressed mask, if it still exists, is here.
- **Thespia's youth as a patron city** makes it a natural rival for older cults jealous of a jumped-up newcomer getting a temple of its own — petty theological politics that can escalate further than anyone intends.
