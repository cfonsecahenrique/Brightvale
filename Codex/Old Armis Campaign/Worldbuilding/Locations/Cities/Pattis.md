# Pattis

The **westernmost city** of **[[The Armisian Empire]]** and the great gateway to the new world — and, uniquely, a **moving city**. Pattis is an **island-metropolis** that drifts under its own power across the **[[The Central Sea]]**, most often holding to the sea's **western edges**, near the passage around **[[The Titan's Sword]]** into the open **[[The Azurian Ocean]]**. It guards its independence jealously: a free city-state that pays the Empire trade and prestige but not obedience.

> **Inspiration:** **Carthage** (a Punic, sea-mastering rival-within-the-empire) by way of **Númenor and Atlantis** — a proud, brilliant, sea-crowned city at the apex of its golden age, with all the foreboding that comparison implies.

## A Moving City Above and Below the Waves
Pattis is the Empire's supreme work of **[[Aetherweave Mastery]]**. It is not anchored to any coast: ceaseless command of the **Water** and **Air** planes carries the whole island-city through the [[The Central Sea]] at will. Half of it rises in tiers above the surface; the other half is built **beneath the waves**, its districts, ways, and gardens held dry and breathable by that same planar mastery. To live in Pattis is to depend, every moment, on the Weave holding — and to wake, some mornings, to a different horizon.

## Overview
- **Where the expeditions begin.** Most great new-world voyages are launched, crewed, and financed from Pattis's harbours.
- **Independent.** One of the city-states that retains real sovereignty within the imperial confederation.
- A landing-point for the new **[[Dwarven Clans|Dwarven]]** communities entering Empire society.

## Secession (GM / [[Plot Hooks]])
The halls of Pattis and **[[Armis]]–[[Caen]]** seethe with intrigue. Some hold that the **Pattisians are a separate people** entirely, who would **secede** from the greatest empire of any age — a fracture made all the more dangerous by the fact that Pattis can simply *sail away*. *(See hook #6 in [[Plot Hooks]].)*

## Looking Forward (GM)
Pattis outlives Armis-Caen — but not by enough. When the deathless elites' century of rule (see **[[Timeline (Armis)|the Dark Reign]]**) breaks the Empire in civil war, it is Pattis that survives the sack of the old capital and declares itself the Empire's sole legitimate heir, growing for 250 wary years against the Aldari realm at **[[Ezaro]]**. It is Pattis's own Master Aetherweavers who, centuries later, push the Amun-Orb past immortality and toward true godhood — the act that finally ends the age. The glorious-overreach theme below is not just flavour; it is the city's actual fate.

## Hooks (GM)
- Numenor/Atlantis framing invites a theme of glorious overreach — exploration shading into hubris.
- The undersea districts — and the city's very motion — are only ever one failure of the Weave away from catastrophe.
- A city that can *move* is a city that can flee, blockade, or vanish: enormous strategic and political leverage over the capital ([[Armis]]) and the western trade.

## Layout

Like all Armisian cities, Pattis is **circular** — though where the mainland cities grew outward from a fixed hill or harbour over centuries, Pattis was *designed* from the outset as a disc, its rings planned rather than accreted. The city's upper half rises in concentric tiers above the waterline; the lower half descends below it, each ring mirroring the one above but quieter, older, and stranger. The great **Cothon** — the military harbour — sits at the city's heart, a circular enclosed basin with a single narrow channel-gate. Everything else spirals out from it.

## Points of Interest

### The Cothon
The military heart of Pattis. A circular enclosed harbour, its single entrance sealed at night by a **great bronze chain**. The inner rim is lined with the **Neoria** — long covered ship-sheds, vaulted arch-mouths gaping at the water, each housing a war-galley on timber rollers. Arriving visitors see the arch-mouths before the temples. This is deliberate.

### The Emporion
The commercial port district, ringing the Cothon on its seaward side. Counting-houses, bonded warehouses, factor-offices, foreign enclaves. The **Salt Quarter** here has become the heart of Pattis's growing Dwarven community — forge-smoke and guttural speech in a city of sea-wind and Armisian dialect. The two cultures trade, argue, and occasionally brawl with the particular warmth of people who need each other.

### The Sacred Ring
A full circuit of twelve temples — one for each god of the [[Armisian Pantheon]], arranged in a ring around the city's upper tier. Each temple was built by a different generation and follows no shared plan; the ring looks less like a sacred district and more like an argument in stone that has been going on for centuries. Thalassos's temple is conspicuously absent from this ring. He has somewhere better.

### The Temple of Thalassos
The greatest building in Pattis — and one of the great wonders of the age — lies **below the waterline**, in the city's deep undersea quarter. To descend to it is to pass through a series of Aetherweave-sealed airlocks and emerge into a vast vaulted space that feels less like a temple and more like standing inside the ocean itself.

The walls and ceiling are set with **gargantuan panes of glass** — each one a feat of craft that took a generation to produce — through which the open sea is visible in every direction: fish, kelp-forests, the slow drift of deep-water creatures, and on clear days the green-lit abyss fading into black. Light moves constantly across the floors and the faces of the worshippers. There is no separation between the sacred space and the sea. That is the point.

Running through the temple's lower chambers are **salt-water pools**, open to the ocean through grated channels, in which the rites of Thalassos are conducted: immersion, offering, the reading of currents. Initiates are held beneath the surface for as long as they can bear. The deepest pool is never fully lit and its floor has not been measured. Priests claim the god moves in it.

No other temple in the Empire approaches this one. Even sailors who have spent their whole lives praying to Thalassos describe their first visit as the moment they understood what they had been praying to.

### The High Terraces
Pattis's wealthiest residential tier — a series of broad stepped terraces on the city's upper ring, their colonnaded facades facing the open sea. The harbor-temples of fulfilled vows stand here, their walls lined with bronze plaques recording the bargains kept: *I promised Thalassos a silver anchor if I survived the Sword. Here is the anchor.* The temples are also haunted, in a subtler sense, by the plaques that were never installed — the debts of crews who did not come back to pay.

### The Aether-Helm
The great working that moves the city — and the closest thing Pattis has to a seat of government. Located at the city's centre, directly above the Cothon, the Aether-Helm is a vast circular chamber open to the sky above and the sea below, where the city's master Aetherweavers maintain the continuous planar workings that keep Pattis afloat, dry, and in motion. It is part temple, part engine room, and part throne hall.

The chamber is always occupied. Shifts of Aetherweavers — called **Helmsmen**, regardless of rank or gender — rotate in and out in strict cycles, maintaining the flows of Water and Air that sustain the city. The head of their order, the **Archhelm**, holds more practical power over Pattis than the city's elected suffetes: a Helmsman who slows his working can slow the city; an Archhelm who withdraws cooperation can stop it entirely. This is not a theoretical threat. It has been made twice in living memory.

Visitors are permitted in the outer galleries, where they can watch the Helmsmen work — a circle of robed figures standing at evenly spaced stations around the chamber's edge, hands raised, eyes closed, the air around them faintly luminous with drawn Weave. The sound is a low, resonant hum that visitors feel in their chests before they hear it with their ears.

### The Weave-Yards
Pattis builds ships, but not in the way other cities do. The **Weave-Yards** occupy the city's eastern arc — dry-docks where hulls are laid in the traditional manner, then handed to teams of Aetherwrights who lace the structure through with planar workings before it ever touches water. The result is a vessel that responds to Aetherweave commands rather than — or in addition to — wind and oar.

The Yards are also where experimental craft are tested. The current obsession is a hull design intended to sail *beneath* the surface entirely, maintained like the undersea districts by an Air-plane pocket. Three prototypes have been lost. The project continues.

### The Scholastikon
The Empire's premier academy of Aetherweave theory, housed in a tiered tower on the upper city that leans — deliberately — outward over the sea at a vertiginous angle, held in place by a continuous working of the Air plane. Students are told this is to remind them that mastery of the Weave begins with trusting it. The building has never fallen. Students find this less reassuring than the faculty intends.

The Scholastikon trains both theorists and practitioners, and has a rivalry with the more conservative academies of Armis-Caen that occasionally produces actual duels. Its archives hold the original notes of the **Awakening** — the 744 AF breakthrough that gave the Empire command of the deep Weave — and access to them requires a petition, three faculty endorsements, and, in practice, knowing someone.

### The Undersea Districts
The lower half of Pattis is older, stranger, and quieter than the surface city. Residential streets, workshops, markets, and gardens are held dry and breathable by unceasing Aetherweave mastery — a fact residents are so accustomed to that they rarely think about it. Visitors sometimes cannot sleep for thinking about it. The streets here are dimly lit by phosphorescent coral cultivated in wall-niches, and the faint sound of water under pressure is constant. The Temple of Thalassos is the jewel of this district, but older structures surround it: some of the founding-era architecture, before the upper city was fully built, before anyone knew if the city would hold.
