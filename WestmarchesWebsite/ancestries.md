# Ancestries & Cultures of Brightvale

*The Enduring are not one people. They were many peoples before the Apostasy, and they remain many now — crowded onto a single plateau, sharing a single Beacon, finding reasons to cooperate and reasons to argue with equal consistency.*

---

## Humankind
### *Heirs of the Armisian Empire*

The most numerous ancestry in Brightvale. Humans carry the weight of the Armisian Empire in everything: their architecture, their legal systems, their social hierarchies, and their tendency to organize everything into Noble Houses whether the situation requires it or not.

Their short lifespans — compared to Elves and Dwarves — produce a distinctive urgency. Humans who want to achieve things achieve them in decades or not at all, which drives both remarkable ingenuity and remarkable impatience. The Aetherweaver Academy's structured magic was adopted so quickly by human mages largely because it was *efficient* — teachable in years rather than centuries.

Human culture in Brightvale is preoccupied with lineage and status. Who your ancestors were, which Houses they served or founded, what their name meant in the courts of Medsen — these things matter intensely to humans of the upper tiers and considerably less to those below, who have more immediate concerns.

### Magic
Before Elven scholarship reached Brightvale, human magic operated on no framework at all — only **belief**. A human who needed fire had no theory of combustion. They simply needed fire with enough conviction that reality provided it. Faith moved flame. Grief split stone. This unstructured tradition produced effects that modern formulaic magic still cannot fully replicate. The Dawnfather's creation of the Beacon is the supreme example: a feat so far outside any known arcane framework that scholars who reverse-engineer it consistently reach dead ends.

Modern human mages use the **Arcanum** — a formulaic approach borrowed and adapted from Elven principles. Efficient, precise, teachable, and subject to the same hazard as the Elven tradition: **Arcane Locks**, self-imposed logical frameworks that can cage a mage's ceiling rather than raise it.

### Naming Traditions
*Noble and scholarly names (Central European in feel):*
Kaleb, Florian, Lucien, Petyr, Matthias, Reinhardt, Harald, Narcissa, Liana, Lilianne, Aelfred, Florenz, Essek, Eldris, Wyll.

*Common names:*
Azi, Nayar, Thyazi, Thalia, Shadia, Kari, Neera.

## Elves (Faefolk)
### *Children of the Severed Threshold*

Before the Apostasy, Elves maintained a connection to the **Faewilds** — a parallel realm of ancient natural power. The Apostasy severed that connection. The Faewilds went quiet. The Fae stopped coming. What exactly happened is a wound the Elves have not finished processing four centuries later.

Elven philosophy in the Vale has fractured along three broad paths:

**The Seekers** believe the connection can be restored. They study Aetherweaving at Aurore's Peak, maintain the old megaliths and standing stones, and work with researchers like House Aerincorvus to document Fae-contact anomalies. The Faewilds were *sealed*, they insist. Not destroyed.

**The Scholars** believe the answers lie in the ruins of the old world. The Apostasy is the mystery worth solving, and solving it requires archaeology, archivism, and patience measured in centuries.

**The Urban Elves** have detached from the past and built their lives in Medsen or within the Noble Houses. Forward-looking, integrated, and occasionally regarded with quiet judgment by the other two groups.

Elves live considerably longer than humans, and this shows in how they approach everything. Their magical tradition is built on thorough understanding — the **Formulaic Tradition**, where a spell is an inevitable conclusion of correct reasoning rather than an act of will. The precision this produces is remarkable. The hazard is the **Arcane Lock**: a mage who has built a perfectly consistent internal framework may find themselves unable to advance past it, not from lack of power, but because their own reasoning has ruled out the next step. Breaking a Lock typically requires recognizing it is self-imposed — and then breaking the logic entirely, at least momentarily.

**Home settlement:** Many Elves live in **Aeldenbarrow**, a druidic enclave half-concealed by glamours near the Vale's western edge. Others are dispersed throughout Medsen, Aurore, and beyond.

### Naming Traditions
*Celtic in sound:*
Cillian, Ciara, Eira, Maeve, Aodhán, Faolan, Fíadh, Elowen, Enriel, Cirdan, Hwynfer, Taliesin.

## Dwarvenkind
### *The Rune-Keepers*

Dwarven culture in Brightvale is built around a single conviction: this is not home. The Dwarves arrived on the Armisian coastline **less than a century before the Apostasy** — recent enough that the first generation to settle had made the crossing themselves. They knew where they had come from. They had names for the headlands. They remembered the smell of the water.

Then the Apostasy fell, the world ended, and the march north severed them from every coast entirely. Brightvale is landlocked. The Great Sea they remember is not myth. It is a place. It simply cannot be reached from here.

The Dwarves hold the Vale as a shore to defend until the Umbra recedes enough for the passage to become possible again — and they hold it with the same stubborn, sustained competence they brought to every shore before it.

### Hvergi

The Dwarven enclave of **Hvergi** is carved into the eastern mountain-roots of Brightvale — mine, fortress, and cultural heartland in equal measure, founded in the first decades after the Apostasy. The Dwarves mine the deep crystal seams beneath the mountains: crystals used by Aetherweavers to focus the Beacon's light, some carrying latent magical energy of their own. This gives Hvergi a quiet but significant hold on Brightvale's arcane supply chain. They are aware of this. They manage it carefully.

Three ruling families govern Hvergi and have done so since its founding:

**Clan Brumdain** are the eldest family and the deepest miners — scholars of the mountain's grain, keepers of the oldest rune-stones. Their authority is geological as much as political.

**Clan Hjalrim** are Hvergi's warriors and gatekeepers, and the finest weaponsmiths in all of Brightvale. They decide who enters the enclave and, by extension, who matters.

**Clan Sigr** manage Hvergi's trade relationships with the outside world — the family most fluent in Medsen's political language, and the most controversial within the enclave for it.

### The Hjalrim Blade

A Hjalrim blade is not a weapon. It is an inheritance.

Clan Hjalrim works **Hvergi crystal shards** directly into the steel during the smelt, producing blades that carry the crystal's natural resonance. In the darkness of the Wastes, a Hjalrim blade glows faintly — a pale blue-white light that unsettles Umbra-touched creatures. When the blade is further treated with **ashgeld**, the glow becomes something more: the blade burns in the dark, and Umbra-touched flesh does not tolerate it. Venturers who carry one describe the experience as fighting with a torch that also cuts.

There are perhaps one hundred such blades in existence across all of Brightvale. Each has a name. Clan Hjalrim does not sell them — they are gifted, rarely, to those the Clan considers worthy, and each one is tracked. If you meet a Dwarf who wants to know how a particular blade ended up in a particular hand, that is why.

### Heimsfar

Every Dwarf in Hvergi knows the word *Heimsfar* — the Homeward Voyage. The prophecy holds that one day the Dwarven people will reach open water, build great ships, and sail back to the homeland from which the Apostasy cut them off. The earliest carvings of it were specific — coastal landmarks, sea-routes, distances. Four centuries of re-carving from slightly worn originals have reduced those specifics to symbol and impression: *ships on dark water*, *fire on the horizon*, *a shore that is not this shore*.

Whether the prophecy is a literal sailing plan that has been slowly corrupted into poetry, or always was poetry, is a question Dwarves argue about and never resolve. What is not argued: Brightvale is a shore to be held, not a home to be kept. The sea is still out there. The Dwarves will reach it.

A Dwarven character who cares about *Heimsfar* is not naive or backward-looking. They are carrying the longest patience in Brightvale — the conviction that survival now is in service of something larger, and that the work matters even if the destination is still beyond the horizon.

### Naming Traditions
*Norse in sound:*
Storvald, Hjalrim, Merlos, Brumdain, Dain, Thormor, Tyr, Gyrid, Brigitte, Dyanna, Freyr, Eivor, Sigr.

## Halflings
### *Guardians of Joy*

Halflings hold the **Halfling Hearthlands** — the most important piece of real estate in Brightvale after Medsen itself. The agricultural heart of the valley. Their fields produce the majority of the Vale's food through communal agrarian ritual that blends practical farming with storytelling they believe keeps the lingering presence of the Fae woven into the soil. Whether this is mythology or mechanics, the Hearthlands produce harvests that Beacon-ash fertilizer alone does not explain.

Like Elves, Halflings retain a connection to the natural world that echoes their Fae-adjacent ancestry. They are not political in the way humans and Dwarves are political. They are concerned with different things: community, memory, the stories that bind people together, and the practice of joy as maintenance rather than luxury — something that requires active tending, especially in dark times.

**Note:** Some scholars consider Halflings a distinct branch of Faefolk ancestry. Halflings find this debate largely uninteresting.

### Naming Traditions
Halflings tend toward warm, comfortable-sounding names — often with a rustic or Old English feel.
*Examples: Rosie, Bramble, Tobias, Merric, Calla, Fen, Aldric, Petal, Oswin, Brix.*

## Orcenfolk
### *The Nomadic Senshi*

Orcish culture blends the traditions of nomadic steppe peoples with the disciplined philosophy of a warrior class that treats combat as craft and craft as identity. In Brightvale, horses are extraordinarily rare — only a small number survived the trek up the Venturer's Trail, and the Brightblood War further devastated the population. The Orcish community are their stewards, handling the surviving bloodlines with the care reserved for things that cannot be replaced.

Through **House Kaze**, they operate the Vale's vital horse messenger relay. In battle, they station near Emberfort. The **Verse of the Blade** — the warrior philosophy that now dominates Slayer culture — originates in Orcish tradition: a combat philosophy that treats martial skill as inseparable from artistic expression, and holds that a warrior who fights without soul is no better than the things in the Wastes.

Orcish clans maintain significant autonomy outside Medsen's direct governance. House Iyengar has been pushing for formal legal recognition of non-Medsen Orcish communities for decades. Progress has been slow, but it has been made.

### Naming Traditions
*Japanese and Korean in sound:*
Musha, Ryuujin, Orochi, Kasai, Kenshi, Haru, Rin, Hana, Hama, Goro, Goryo, Sakae, Nari, Jin, Nantai.

## Infernis (Tieflings)
### *The Shard-Kin*

The Infernis did not exist before the Apostasy. They are its children — the consequence of desperate bargains struck in the cataclysm's darkest years.

When the Umbra consumed civilization and survival looked like a matter of hours, various noble lineages and desperate individuals made pacts with extraplanar entities: Devils, Demons, beings from beyond the fraying fabric of a collapsing world. They survived. Their descendants bear the mark of those bargains in their blood, passed down through every generation since, permanent and indelible.

The physical form of an Infernis reveals the nature of the original pact:

**Red-skinned Infernis** descend from diabolical pacts — lineages associated with themes of order, contracts, and rigid hierarchy.

**Gray or blue-skinned Infernis** descend from demonic pacts — lineages associated with raw power, primal resonance, and the chaotic survivalism of the Apostasy's worst hours. House Lugner carries this phenotype most prominently, which they regard as a feature.

Despite — or because of — their unusual origin, the Infernis are not outcasts in Brightvale. They are visible reminders that the people of the Vale did whatever they had to do to survive. Many find that admirable in retrospect. High-ranking Caretakers who devote their lives to the Beacon sometimes adopt Latinate names to mark their dedication.

### Magic
The extraplanar entities of the original pacts are gone — vanished along with the gods. What remains is the bloodline and the **Warlock tradition** it carries: magic through obsessive specialization. Where Elven magic builds universal frameworks and human magic reaches for raw faith, Warlock magic picks one domain and pursues it until the distinction between knowing something and being able to do it collapses entirely. A Warlock who has spent decades mastering movement eventually stops asking *how* to move through air and begins asking why they *couldn't*. Flight becomes not a feat but an absence of assumption.

Infernis practitioners do not universally follow this path — but those who do often find it comes more naturally to them than to practitioners of any other ancestry.

### Naming Traditions
Infernis generally follow Human naming conventions. Those with deep Caretaker devotion sometimes adopt Latinate names:
*Dante, Maximus, Beatrice, Gabriel, Vergil, Lucia, Augustus, Lucian, Cassius, Sylvia, Orpheus.*
