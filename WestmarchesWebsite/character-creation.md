# Character Creation
## Building a Venturer for Brightvale

*Before you choose what your character can do, decide who they are.*

---

## Step 1: Heritage

Your character's **Heritage** is the combination of two things: your **Ancestry** and your **Community**.

### Ancestry

Your ancestry is your biological lineage — the people you were born from. Brightvale is home to six ancestries, each with its own history, culture, and relationship to the world that was lost:

- **Humankind** — Heirs of the Armisian Empire. Short-lived, ambitious, obsessed with lineage.
- **Elves (Faefolk)** — Children of the Severed Threshold. Long-lived, precise, still mourning the Faewilds.
- **Dwarvenkind** — The Rune-Keepers. Stubborn, skilled, remembering a Great Sea they can no longer reach.
- **Halflings** — Guardians of Joy. Agrarian, communal, tending the Hearthlands and the stories that keep them fertile.
- **Orcenfolk** — The Nomadic Senshi. Martial artists and horse-stewards, carriers of the Verse of the Blade.
- **Infernis (Tieflings)** — The Shard-Kin. Children of desperate Apostasy-era pacts, marked in blood and skin.

See **Ancestries & Cultures** for the full details on each.

### Community

Your community is where and how you grew up — the environment that shaped you before you became a Venturer. In Daggerheart terms, this is your **Community card** (Highborne, Loreborne, Orderborne, Ridgeborne, Seaborne, Slyborne, Underborne, Wanderborne, Wildborne).

When choosing your community, think about where in Brightvale your character was raised:

- A **Highborne** character likely grew up in Noble House circles in Medsen — ballrooms, inheritance politics, and the weight of a family name.
- A **Loreborne** character may have been raised at Aurore's Peak among Academy scholars, or in a family of archivists and chroniclers.
- An **Orderborne** character probably grew up within the structure of the Emberguard, the Caretakers, or another institutional framework.
- A **Ridgeborne** character hails from the mountain edges — Hvergi, the mining camps, or the remote plateau settlements.
- A **Seaborne** character carries the cultural memory of the Dwarven Great Sea or the lost Armisian coastlines — a heritage of water in a world that has none.
- A **Slyborne** character may have grown up in a group that operates outside of the law, including all manner of criminals, grifters and con artists. Members of slyborne communities are brought together by their disparate goals and their clever means of achieving them. Many people in these communities have an array of unscrupulous skills: forging, thievery, smuggling, and violence. People of any social class can be slyborne, from those who have garnered vast wealth and influence to those without a coin to their name. To the outside eye, slyborne might appear to be ruffians with no loyalty, but these communities possess some of the strictest codes of honor which, when broken, can result in a terrifying end for the transgressor.
- An **Underborne** character knows Medsen's lower districts, its tunnels, its underside — or the places people don't talk about.
- A **Wanderborne** character moved between settlements — Orcish relay riders, traveling merchants, displaced families who never quite settled.
- A **Wildborne** character grew up outside the walls: Aeldenbarrow's druidic enclaves, the Hearthlands' outer edges, or somewhere the Beacon's light barely reaches.

## Step 2: Nobility & Social Standing

Not every Venturer comes from the gutter, and not every Venturer comes from a Great House. Consider where your character falls:

### No Noble Affiliation
Most Venturers. Common-born, guild-raised, or simply from families that never had titles to lose. You owe nothing to anyone except the guild that trained you. This is, in some ways, the most dangerous position — no patron, no safety net — and in other ways, the most free.

### Minor or Unknown Nobility
Perhaps your family holds a small estate, a rural title, or a name that once meant something three generations ago. You are noble in the legal sense but not in any way that opens doors in Medsen. Your claim to status is real but quiet, and you may be the only person who cares about it.

### A Recent House
Your family was elevated *after* the Apostasy — for services rendered, debts called in, or strategic marriages. Recent Houses include **Ashwood**, **Drevorn**, **Kaze**, **Ravenna**, and **Thalum**. They are respected, but the Old Houses never let them forget the difference. You have resources and connections, but also something to prove.

### An Old House
Your lineage predates the end of the world. The Old Houses — **Aerincorvus**, **Aurelian**, **Iyengar**, **Kaewdyn**, **Lugner**, and **Von Zoltraak** — carry the weight of Armisian legacy. You were born into expectation, obligation, and a name that people recognize. Whether that's a gift or a cage depends on you.

See **The Noble Houses** for the full details on each house and their story hooks.

## Step 3: Your Guild

Every Venturer belongs to one of the **five Guilds**. Your guild determines your training, your mandate, and who you answer to. See **The Guilds** for the full breakdown of each institution.

## Step 4: Your Class

In Daggerheart, your **Class** determines your mechanical abilities — the domain cards you access and the subclass features you develop. But a class is not a job title. It is an *abstraction of skillset*: a way of categorising what you're good at, not prescribing who you are.

**A Bard does not have to be a singer.** A Bard is anyone whose power flows through expression, persuasion, and the manipulation of narrative — a war-poet who recites verse while the Slayers charge, a guild diplomat who talks a patrol through a crisis, a Caretaker whose sermons hold the line against despair. The instrument is irrelevant. The effect is what matters.

This principle applies to every class. A Wizard is not necessarily a robed academic. A Warrior is not necessarily a brute. A Seraph is not necessarily pious. Your class describes *how you fight and what tools you bring* — your guild and your heritage describe *why*.

### Reading the Table

A few combinations worth noting:

**Bards** are everywhere. Every guild needs people who can inspire, coordinate, and communicate under pressure. A Slayer Bard composes the Verse of the Blade in the field. An Emberguard Bard maintains unit morale during long patrols. A Caretaker Bard leads the hymns that focus the Beacon's light into Lumen-Casks. An Explorer Bard records oral histories from ruins that have no written ones left.

**Druids** are primarily a tertiary division of the Aetherweaver Academy — scholars of natural resonance and pre-Apostasy ecological magic. In practice, however, most working Druids end up in the Explorers' Guild, where their affinity for terrain, weather, and living systems makes them invaluable in the Wastes. House Ashwood and the Elven enclave of Aeldenbarrow produce a disproportionate number.

**Guardians** are the backbone of any guild that puts bodies in harm's way. The Emberguard trains them by the dozen. The Slayers value them as the immovable anchor around which a hunting party pivots. The Caretakers rely on them to protect relic-recovery teams. Only the Academy, which rarely fields frontline personnel, has little use for them.

**Rangers** are Explorers by trade — scouts, pathfinders, and wilderness specialists who map the Wastes and maintain the checkpoint network. Some of the finest Rangers, however, end up as Slayers: their tracking skills and terrain intuition make them lethal against the specific high-value targets the Slayers hunt. You will not find Rangers in the Emberguard, whose doctrine relies on formation discipline rather than independent fieldwork.

**Rogues** thrive in the Explorers' Guild (infiltration, trap-handling, ruin-delving) and among the Slayers (precision strikes, ambush tactics, the killing blow on a creature's weak point). They have no home in the Academy or among the Caretakers — institutions that value transparency and orthodoxy, neither of which is a Rogue's strong suit.

**Seraphs** draw power from conviction and radiant faith. The Caretakers are their spiritual home, where their connection to the Beacon's light is nurtured and directed. But Seraphs also serve among the Slayers — where divine wrath is a practical tool against Umbra-touched horrors — and the Emberguard, where their protective auras reinforce the Chain of Flame.

**Sorcerers** are a distinct and powerful division of the Aetherweaver Academy — mages whose power is innate rather than learned, shaped by bloodline or exposure rather than study. The Academy both trains and studies them. Some Sorcerers also serve among the Caretakers, where their raw connection to luminous energy complements the Beacon's rituals.

**Warriors** are the generalists of violence. Every guild except the Academy counts Warriors in its ranks. Whether swinging a greatsword on an Emberguard patrol, holding the line for a Slayer hunting party, guarding a Caretaker relic-team, or cutting a path through ruins for the Explorers, the Warrior's skillset is universally in demand.

**Wizards** belong exclusively to the Academy and the Caretakers — the two institutions that deal in structured, scholarly magic. A Wizard's power comes from study, formulae, and deep understanding of arcane principles. The Academy trains them. The Caretakers employ them for ward maintenance, Lumen-Cask production, and the complex rituals surrounding the Beacon. Outside these institutions, a Wizard's methodology finds little institutional support.

## Step 5: Putting It Together

Once you have your Heritage, your social standing, your guild, and your class, you have the bones of a character. The rest is yours:

- **Why did you become a Venturer?** Duty? Desperation? Curiosity? Debt? Glory? A death wish that hasn't been granted yet?
- **What does your guild mean to you?** A family? A paycheck? A cause? An obligation you're trying to escape?
- **What do you carry from your heritage?** A name? A grudge? A tradition? A gap where a memory used to be?
- **What are you afraid of in the Wastes?** Everyone is afraid of something. The ones who say otherwise haven't been out long enough.
- What is Brightvale to you? A prison? A haven? Home?

You can find some inspiration in the Rumours and Hooks document.
The Wastes will test every answer you give here.
