# The Order of Brightvale
## Government, Institutions, and Faith

---

## The Council of Seven

Brightvale is nominally a monarchy. In practice, it is governed by the **Council of Seven** — a body established in 357 AA after the last absolute king immolated himself into the Beacon to end a famine he had caused. The Enduring decided, collectively, that one person should probably not hold that much power again.

The Council consists of seven seats:

### The Five Permanent Seats
These are held by the leaders of the Vale's core institutions. Their authority does not depend on elections — they hold their seats as long as they hold their positions.

| Seat | Holder | Represents |
|:---|:---|:---|
| High Caretaker | Kaelen (Infernis) | The Caretakers of the Beacon; religious doctrine; preservation of the Light |
| Lord Commander of the Emberguard | Varis Lugner (Infernis) | Internal military order; civil law; defense of settlements |
| Arch Aetherweaver | *(current holder not public)* | The Academy; arcane research; Brightvale's intellectual future |
| First Slayer | Makjar One-Eyed (Orc) | The Slayers; the war against the Umbra; Waste expertise |
| Grand Explorer | Mira Ravenna | The Explorers' Guild; outward expansion; Waste reclamation |

### The Two Rotating Noble Seats
These seats are occupied by Noble House representatives elected every two years. In theory, this gives the aristocracy a voice in governance. In practice, the five permanent factions collude aggressively during election cycles, backing allied Houses to secure proxy votes at the Council table. The Noble elections are, functionally, a proxy war between the Guilds. The aristocracy knows this and considers it the cost of relevance.

## The Monarchy

The royal family claims to be the **Sun-Born** — direct descendants of the Dawnfather himself. For centuries, this claim was backed by a visible, demonstrable ability to channel radiant holy light, which made it difficult to dispute openly.

The monarchy's absolute power ended following generations of mismanagement: famines, border closures, a civil war that nearly destroyed the Vale. Today, the King retains a ceremonial seat on the Council and the symbolic weight of the Dawnfather's legacy. Real authority rests with the Council.

**King Alfwyn**, the current sovereign, has not been seen publicly in seven years. He withdrew into the Central Temple-Castle — built directly over the Beacon's heart — and ceased all public appearances shortly after his coronation. The **High Steward** manages the crown's administrative duties and holds his Council seat as proxy. Theories about the King's condition are, at this point, a cottage industry in every common room in Medsen.

## The Guilds and the Houses

The fundamental tension of Brightvale's politics is between the **five Guilds** (which hold actual resources and practical power) and the **Noble Houses** (which hold historical legitimacy and land rights inherited from the Armisian Empire).

The Houses maintain feudal claims over surrounding lands, forts, and fiefs. They fund institutions, back Council candidates, forge alliances, and conduct proxy wars through commerce and election cycles. They are powerful.

The Guilds, however, control the things people cannot live without: the Beacon's maintenance, the walls' defense, monster culling, Waste mapping, and arcane research. In a crisis, the Guild that holds Emberfort's garrison matters more than the House that owns the farmland behind it.

This tension is not resolved. It is managed, imperfectly, season by season.

## The Three Faiths

Religion in Brightvale is inseparable from politics because the Beacon is simultaneously the city's primary theological object and its most important piece of survival infrastructure. Three positions divide the faithful — and the debate shapes every significant decision the Vale makes about its future.

### The Orthodoxy
*"The Light is divine and must not be touched."*

The dominant position, held by the Caretakers and a significant portion of the common population. The Beacon is the literal residue of the Dawnfather's sacrifice — sacred fire, holy beyond mortal comprehension. Its flickering is a divine sign, a response to the failings of the Enduring. To study it, to tamper with it, to treat it as a mechanism rather than a miracle, is blasphemy. The faithful weathered famines with prayer. They will weather the flickering the same way.

### The Stoics
*"The Light endures, and so must we."*

A quieter tradition. The Beacon is sacred, yes — but its fluctuations are not punishments. They are simply the nature of a divine thing existing in an imperfect world. The light waxes and wanes. The faithful do not panic when it dims, and do not claim credit when it brightens. They endure. The name is the prayer. This position is common among veterans, older citizens, and those who have spent time outside the walls — people who have seen enough to know that not everything that hurts is a punishment.

### The Pragmatists
*"The Light is power, and power must be understood."*

Favored by the Academy's progressive scholars and, quietly, several Noble Houses. The Beacon is extraordinary — perhaps the most significant magical artifact in the known world — but it is still an artifact. It was *made*. It operates on principles that can be studied. If the Beacon can be understood, it can be maintained. If maintained, it can be improved. If improved, the Enduring can stop merely surviving and start reclaiming what was lost. The Orthodoxy considers this heretical. The Pragmatists consider that a secondary concern.

## The Economy

Brightvale runs on **Ashgeld** — coins pressed from Beacon ash, warm to the touch, faintly luminous in complete darkness. The Caretakers control the mint. The currency is considered the most stable in any world that still has one.

The economic geography of the Vale:

- **The Halfling Hearthlands** — The agricultural heart. Produces the majority of Brightvale's food through communal agrarian techniques and a regulated supply of Beacon ash fertilizer from Medsen.
- **Hvergi** — The industrial heart. Produces nearly all Brightvale's iron, stone, fine crafted goods, and the arcane crystals that supply the Academy's research.
- **Veikryss** — The primary timber source. Supplies construction and commercial wood across the region.
- **Emberfort, Three Bridges, Veikryss** — The logistics spine. These three settlements are enriched beyond their size by serving as the supply funnel for Venturing expeditions: outfitting departing parties, supporting the checkpoint network, and processing the flow of recovered Waste artifacts into the Guild economy.

The wealthiest private individuals in Brightvale are, in order: the heads of the Guilds, the leading Noble Houses, and the merchants who figured out how to be useful to both.
