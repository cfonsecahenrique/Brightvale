# The Economy of Brightvale

Brightvale's economy is a closed, fragile system defined by survival, logistics, and the central distribution of resources. The geographic isolation of the valley dictates strict flows of goods between agricultural centers, industrial hubs, and the demanding capital city. Every major trade relationship in the Vale ultimately traces back to two scarcities: the Beacon's ash and the light it produces.

---

## Ashgeld: The Currency of the Vale

The standard unit of trade in Brightvale is the **Ashcoin**, colloquially called **Ashgeld**. These small, pale coins are minted from compacted Beacon Ash — the sacred residue of the Beacon's eternal flame. They carry a faint warmth to the touch and are said to glow faintly in complete darkness. Because they are backed by the Caretakers and the Beacon itself, Ashgeld is the most stable currency the Vale has ever known.

Crucially, Ashgeld is more than money. The same ash, in different forms and states of preparation, serves multiple critical functions across the Vale's economy and its frontier:

- **As fertilizer (Beacon Dust):** Unpacked, uncompacted Beacon Ash — called *Beacon Dust* — is distributed to the agricultural regions of the Vale where it dramatically improves crop yields. Crops grow in conditions that should not support them: dim light, poor soil, shortened seasons. The Dust carries residual vital energy drawn from the Beacon's consumption.
- **As portable beacon fire:** Venturers heading into the Wastes carry prepared Ashgeld containers — small sealed cases of compacted ash — that can be ignited to produce a flame that repels Umbra-touched creatures. A lit Ashgeld torch is not optional equipment on deep expeditions; it is the difference between life and death.
- **As warding medium:** Ash dissolved into a paste or carried in dry form can be used to draw temporary warding runes on surfaces, doorframes, and ground perimeters. Experienced Venturers know the basic ward patterns by heart.
- **As enchanting agent:** When a Hjalrim blade's forged crystal edge is touched with activated Ashgeld — the ash brought to a specific ritual heat — the resulting enchantment deepens. The blade becomes actively hostile to Umbra-touched flesh in ways that ordinary steel cannot achieve.

The Caretakers of the Beacon regulate the supply of raw ash. The Council of Seven sets the minting rate. This arrangement gives the Caretakers enormous quiet leverage over the Vale's monetary supply, though they rarely use it overtly.

> **GM Note:** The source material for Beacon Ash is not abstract divine energy. It is **bone** — specifically, the ground and incinerated remains of the *Marked*, those individuals who bore the deepest connection to the Beacon's power during their lives. The crops grow because the dead feed them. The coins circulate because the dead back their value. Brightvale's economy and its survival are, at their foundation, built on the consumption of its own people.

## Agriculture and the Breadbasket

The massive capital of **[[Medsen]]** is a dense metropolis with an ever-growing appetite. It consumes the vast majority of food produced across Brightvale, acting as the valley's largest economic sink.

To sustain this, the **[[Halfling Hearthlands]]** acts as the definitive breadbasket of the valley, producing the lion's share of crops. Their unparalleled agricultural yield is achieved not just through halfling agrarian techniques, but through a vital, regulated exchange: Medsen supplies the Hearthlands with unpacked Beacon Dust. Without this arrangement, the Hearthlands' output would collapse to a fraction of current levels.

**[[House Aurelian]]** plays a complementary and often underappreciated role in this system. Boasting lineage from old Armisian agricultural science, the Aurelians maintain the Vale's major seed vaults and experimental greenhouses — the genetic and botanical library that keeps Brightvale's crops viable across generations. They frequently supply food through Caretaker charitable operations and sell their harvested grain through House Drevorn's distribution networks, a relationship that generates constant commercial friction between the two houses.

While the Hearthlands and the Aurelian estates produce the bulk of the valley's food, other smaller towns and villages manage to produce smaller yields, often achieving agricultural self-sufficiency for their own populations.

## Mountain Exports: Cattle, Stone, and Steel

Closer to the Frost-Veil Peaks and the eastern ranges, the rugged terrain favors different industries.

- Mountain-adjacent towns such as **[[Hvergi]]** and **Orska** dedicate their outer foothills and plateaus to herding. They export the vast majority of Brightvale's cattle and cattle-based products — leather, cured meats, and bone tools. The logistics of this trade are managed primarily by **[[House Drevorn]]**, who control the critical distribution routes and maintain a lucrative commercial relationship with the Orska ranchers.
- **[[Hvergi]]**, the great Dwarven enclave, is the undeniable industrial heart of the Vale. From its deep mines and massive forges, it produces almost all of the valley's required stone, metalwork, and fine artisan crafted goods. While [[Medsen]] also produces its own share of high-end artisan and luxury goods, it fundamentally relies on consuming Hvergi's raw materials to fuel its local trade.

## The Arcane Economy: Hvergi Crystal and Aether

Beyond stone and steel, Hvergi's most strategically significant export is **crystal** — and this gives the enclave quiet but substantial leverage over the Vale's arcane economy.

Hvergi crystals are not a uniform commodity. Different seams produce crystals with different resonant properties, and the three ruling Dwarven clans each relate to the material differently:

- **[[Clan Stiórmund]]** are the enclave's primary miners and geological readers. They trace seam-lines and pressure gradients to locate the right crystals — including the resonant deep-seam crystals that vibrate at the Beacon's particular frequency, which the Caretakers of the Beacon depend on for their High Rituals.
- **[[Clan Hjalrim]]** work crystal shards directly into steel during the smelt, producing the legendary **Hjalrim blades** — weapons that glow with a pale blue-white light in the darkness of the Wastes and carry a natural resonance that makes them lethal against Umbra-touched flesh. There are perhaps a hundred such blades in existence across all of Brightvale. Each was made by a Hjalrim smith. Each has a name. Only recently, in response to surging demand, has Clan Hjalrim authorized selling blades to outside buyers for the first time in the clan's history — though at extraordinary prices, and only to buyers the elders consider qualified.
- **[[Clan Sigr]]** discovered something more transformative: that crystal lattices can be prepared to *store* Aether rather than merely channel ambient resonance. A properly prepared Sigr crystal is, functionally, a magical battery — pre-loaded Aether that can be drawn upon later. This breakthrough has made Clan Sigr the most externally connected of Hvergi's families, collaborating actively with [[House Von Zoltraak]]'s Crucible laboratories, [[House Thalum]]'s engineering corps, and researchers at the [[Aetherweaver Academy]].

The **Aetherweaver Academy** is the primary institutional consumer of Hvergi crystal. Aetherweavers throughout the Vale rely on Hvergi crystals to channel and manipulate the Beacon's light — without them, most applied aetherweaving would be significantly less precise. Clan Sigr supplies both the [[House Lugner]]-backed Old Magic faction and the [[House Von Zoltraak]]-backed Progressive faction with standard-grade crystals, carefully maintaining neutrality by never granting either side a monopoly.

The Academy is also a major consumer of raw timber — for construction, furnishings, and experimental infrastructure — placing it in a practical supply dependency on **[[House Ashwood]]**, which controls the Vale's critical timber reserves.

## Timber and Venturer Trade Dynamics

Forestry and lumber are primarily sourced from towns situated near the valley's wooded areas. **[[Veikryss]]**, the central hub, is a major producer of timber, acting as the primary supplier of wood for construction and commercial use across the region.

However, the most dynamic economic engine outside of raw material production is **Venturer Support Logistics**.

The settlements arranged along the primary southern arteries — **[[Veikryss]]**, **[[Three Bridges]]**, and **[[Emberfort]]** — experience massive, bustling trade uncharacteristic of standard rural towns. Because they serve as the logistical funnel leading toward the Venturer's Trail, their local economies are supercharged by:

- Housing, feeding, and supplying departing [[Venturers]] and the large entourages that support them.
- Facilitating the flow of heavy logistical caravans that continuously transport supplies back and forth to maintain the outer Checkpoints and Venturer outposts established deep in the Wastes.

Emberfort's **Lower Town Markets** — a dense stretch of stalls, workshops, and storefronts — cater almost exclusively to Venturers and the people who outfit them. Three Bridges functions as a major staging point where supplies from across the valley are taxed and consolidated before the final march south. Stories and news from the outside world first spread through its inns.

## The Waste Economy: Artifacts and Recovery

The most volatile and potentially lucrative economic activity in Brightvale is the recovery of pre-[[Apostasy]] Armisian artifacts from the Wastes. Ancient relics — tools, instruments, weapons, texts — surface in the ruins beyond the valley's walls and are brought back by Venturers at considerable personal risk.

These artifacts flow primarily to the **[[Aetherweaver Academy]]**, which depends on them for its research into the Deep-Weave (the lost magical science of the Armisian Empire). **[[House Von Zoltraak]]** and **[[House Lugner]]** both fund artifact recovery through the Explorers' Guild in exchange for research access or priority study rights. **[[House Drevorn]]** holds co-financing arrangements with the Guild that grant them priority commercial rights on recovered Waste goods.

The constant flow of guild funding, specialized gear, recovering Venturers, and the influx of highly valuable artifacts makes Veikryss, Three Bridges, and Emberfort critical, wealthy nodes of commerce outside the capital — arguably the most liquid markets in the Vale after Medsen itself.

## Logistics, Infrastructure, and Information

Behind every trade relationship in Brightvale sits a logistics layer controlled by a small number of houses with significant structural power.

**[[House Drevorn]]** is the Vale's dominant logistics operator. Elevated from merchants who outperformed the old nobility during the Apostasy crisis by controlling grain storage and distribution, the Drevorns now hold title to critical physical assets, warehouses, and trade routes. They supply the Emberguard, distribute Aurelian grain, broker livestock from Orska, and hold priority rights on expedition recovery goods — all while maintaining a towering ledger of noble debt that gives them quiet financial leverage over almost every other faction.

**[[House Kaze]]** controls the one communication network that moves faster than caravans: horses. In Brightvale, horses are extraordinarily rare and incalculably expensive — the small herd that survives is descended from animals that reached the valley shortly after the Apostasy. The Kaze maintain a system of secure stable-checkpoints along all major valley roads and can carry a vital message from Emberfort to Medsen in under four hours. No other service can match this. An Orc's horse, it is said, is worth more than everything else they own.

**[[House Ravenna]]** trades in movement and information. Originally elevated for mapping the first stable routes into the Vale during the Apostasy, the Ravennas now run an impeccable courier service that doubles as an intelligence network. Where House Kaze carries the message, House Ravenna often already knows what it says.

These three houses together form the invisible connective tissue of Brightvale's economy — without their logistics, communication, and financial infrastructure, the resource flows between Hvergi, the Hearthlands, Medsen, and the southern frontier would fracture within a season.
