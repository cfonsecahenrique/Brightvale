# Cult of the Goddess

*A heresy with no scripture, no priesthood, and a thirteen-year head start on the [[Caretakers of the Beacon|Order]].*

---

## Origin

The cult traces — though it does not name — its origin to the failed expedition of 387 AA (the **[[Official Expedition Report — The Aldred Commission|Aldred Commission]]**). The two survivors, [[Zorael Lugner]] and [[Yuriko Ruinweaver]], were quarantined and discredited on their return, but not before they (and possibly the dying members of their company) seeded fragments of what they had witnessed into the hands of strangers in the streets of [[Medsen]]: a scrap of cloth pressed into a child's hand, a page torn from a journal, a name whispered to a guard at the gate.

The Caretakers' official account labelled all such accounts the babble of Umbra-corrupted minds. In the years since, those fragments have begun to find one another.

---

## What the Cult Believes

There is no single doctrine. The cult is held together not by creed but by **iconography** and a small, consistent set of claims that recur, with eerie precision, across people who have never met:

- There is a **goddess**, older than the [[The Beacon|Beacon]], whose name was either lost or deliberately forgotten.
- She is depicted **veiled or hooded**, eyes covered, holding a single long black-shafted feather between two raised hands.
- She is a goddess of **passage** — the transition from life to death to whatever comes after — not of death itself.
- Her temples exist throughout the Wastes. They are **waking** as the Beacon dims.
- The Beacon, by extension, is doing something *other* than protecting the Enduring. The cult is unclear on what. Its most extreme adherents whisper that it is **consuming** them.

The cult does not, in 400 AA, possess the full truth recorded in the *[[The Veiled God's Lantern — GM Notes|Lantern]]* expedition. It possesses a shape, a feeling, and a growing body of confirmations.

---

## Membership and Spread

Adherents skew toward those for whom the Order's authority sits uneasily:

- **Venturers** of the [[Explorers' Guild]], who have begun reporting (and, increasingly, mapping) temples matching the cult's iconography. Some have begun inking the **veiled feather** onto their skin before a long expedition.
- **Concordance scholars** of [[Aeldenbarrow]], who privately consider the goddess's network a probable companion to their own Primeval Aether research. [[House Aerincorvus]] has been quietly cultivating contacts among the cult since its political demotion in 388 AA.
- **Aetherweavers** of a more progressive bent, particularly those who have always treated the [[The Beacon|Beacon]] as an artifact rather than a divinity.
- **Lay folk** in the lower wards of [[Medsen]] and [[Emberfort]], among whom the cult spreads slowest but most stubbornly — usually through someone who once received a fragment, kept it, and only now begins to understand why.

The cult has no clergy. Its closest thing to a leader is whoever, in a given room, holds the oldest fragment.

---

## Iconography

- **The Veiled Face:** A woman's face, eyes covered by a deep hood or wrap, mouth serene. A single tear descending from beneath the veil is common but not universal.
- **The Feather:** Large, black-shafted, with a slight iridescent sheen. Often depicted held between two raised hands.
- **The Empty Cartouche:** A blank space where a devotional name would otherwise be carved. Adherents leave the name blank deliberately. *To name her is to lose her.*
- **Blue Fire:** A quieter, deeper blue than the Beacon's amber-white. Used in private rites where adherents kindle [[Ashgeld]] in vessels of their own making.

---

## The Order's Response

The [[Caretakers of the Beacon|Caretakers]] regard the cult as **heresy** and have lobbied successfully for the Council to treat the veiled-feather mark as grounds for civic suspicion (though not, yet, for prosecution). [[Emberguard]] patrols in the lower wards have been instructed to note the symbol.

Caretaker sermons since the 388 cycle have escalated their characterisation of the cult from "rumour" to "spiritual contagion." The Order's preferred theory — disseminated through the parish presses — is that the cult is a *foreign* import, brought in by Wastes-exposed Venturers whose minds have been hollowed by [[Umbra]] dreams.

The Order does not, in any public document, name the goddess. Neither does the cult.

---

## Tensions

- The [[Explorers' Guild]] has refused, twice, to surrender its informal register of "anomalous temples" to the Order. [[Grand Explorer Mira Ravenna]] has publicly stated that the register is a matter of operational safety; privately, she has not corrected anyone who calls it something else.
- The [[Aetherweaver Academy]] is split. Several senior scholars have published papers treating the goddess sites as legitimate sources of pre-Apostatic aetheric data. Others have called for those colleagues to be censured.
- The [[Concordance]], through [[House Aerincorvus]]'s lingering channels, treats the cult as a parallel research stream — neither endorsed nor disowned.
- The Caretakers have begun, quietly, to send their own observers into the Wastes to locate and **deface** the iconography at known temple sites. Several such observers have not returned.

## Story Hooks

- A Venturer returns from the southwestern Wastes wearing the veiled-feather mark openly. The Emberguard detain them at the gate. The [[Explorers' Guild]] demands their release. The Council deadlocks.
- A young Caretaker novice begins receiving the same dreams [[Aldred Gwyn]] reported in 387 AA. They cannot tell anyone in the Order without ending their career.
- A scrap of cloth, water-damaged and faded, is pressed into a stranger's hand in a Medsen market. The handwriting on the inside is recognisably that of a dead Venturer. The recipient has thirteen years of context they did not ask for.
- The next Oracle cycle is approaching. The Caretakers know that what the Oracles say will depend on whether the cult's seed took root. Several of the highest-ranking Caretakers — including those who already know the truth — are preparing to bury the next vision before it can be transcribed.
