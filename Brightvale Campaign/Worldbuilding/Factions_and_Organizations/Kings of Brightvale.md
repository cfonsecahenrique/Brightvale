# Kings of Brightvale

A record of the **Sun-Born Sovereigns** — rulers of the [[The Monarchy|Monarchy of Brightvale]] who have held the throne since the [[Apostasy]]. The line claims direct descent from the **Dawnfather**, whose sacrifice at **Year 0** created the Beacon and established the haven at [[Medsen]].

Dates are recorded in **AA (After [[Apostasy]])**. The current year is **400 AA**.

## The Sun-Born Line

| # | Name | Reign | Epithet / Notes |
|:--|:-----|:------|:----------------|
| **I** | **The Dawnfather** | Pre-AA | Not formally a king — the mythic founder whose sacrifice created the Beacon. Claimed as the divine ancestor of all Sun-Born rulers. His true name is unknown or unrecorded. See [[Myth and Legend]]. |
| **II** | *(Unknown — The First Stewards)* | 0–~150 AA | The early post-Apostasy period is poorly documented. Governance was communal rather than monarchical; the Sun-Born bloodline was recognized as symbolic authority but formal kingship had not yet been institutionalized. |
| **III** | **King Cenwald** | *? – 168 AA* | *The Writer.* Credited with ordering the first formal governance structures of [[Medsen]]. His death passed the throne to his heir. |
| **IV** | **King Cynred** | 168–284 AA | *The Bitter.* Began his reign as an isolationist, closing [[Medsen]] to most outside contact. His 116-year reign is the longest in recorded history and produced little outward ambition but significant internal consolidation — and, increasingly, institutional rot. The [[Scholastic Schism]] (**270 AA**) and the founding of the [[Aetherweaver Academy]] (**271 AA**) both occurred during his reign, without royal endorsement. The resentments left by his neglect would erupt into the [[The Brightblood War\|Brightblood War]] within a decade of his death. |
| **V** | **King Aldhelm** | 284–297 AA | *The Unyielding.* Cynred's grandson. Inherited a realm corroded by a century of neglect and attempted ambitious reforms — redistributing Crown resources, demanding updated tax ledgers, granting guild autonomy — that were necessary and catastrophically implemented. The resulting fracture erupted into the [[The Brightblood War\|Brightblood War]] (**293 AA**). When the Beacon flickered and a Horde struck [[Emberfort]], Aldhelm rode south at the head of a combined loyalist-rebel force and broke the Horde at **Three Bridges** with the full radiance of the Sun-Born blood. He died of his wounds three days later in the Dawnfather Temple — the last Sun-Born to die in battle. |
| **VI** | **Queen Wendreda** | 297–328 AA | *The Graceful.* Aldhelm's consort, crowned in the Dawnfather Temple before her husband's body had cooled. Wendreda's first acts were calculated mercy and calculated severity: pardons for the Reform Coalition's lesser members, the public sacrifice of the most nefarious rebel leaders to the Beacon, and the role of the Pardoner to heal the social rift. The fire burned bright and steady, inaugurating the **Golden Reprieve** — a decade of recovery that produced a renaissance in aetheric research and the development of Ashgeld Ingots for field use and Ashline wards. Her later reign oversaw sustained cultural prosperity and the urban expansion of [[Medsen]]. Wendreda is remembered as the queen who rebuilt a shattered realm through an impossible combination of compassion, ruthlessness, and grace — the epithet she earned, not inherited. The only confirmed queen regnant in the Sun-Born line. |
| **VII** | **King Oswin I** | 328–349 AA | *The Venturer.* Wendreda's brother; a man of martial obsession who redirected the kingdom's ambitions outward. Founded the [[Explorers' Guild]] (**329 AA**) and named **Ekbert, Prince of Glass**, as its first Grand Explorer. His reign marks the beginning of systematic Waste exploration. |
| **VIII** | **King Raedfryd** | 349–356 AA | *Exarch of Cinders.* Oswin's successor. His heavy-handed mismanagement of [[Medsen]]'s resources is the primary cause attributed to the **Great Famine (354–356 AA)**. Facing a starving and rebellious population, he sacrificed himself into the Beacon's Great Pyre in **356 AA** — an act that produced a surge of sunlight that restored the crops. Whether this was genuine devotion, calculated martyrdom, or a politically engineered exit remains debated. His death directly caused the formation of the [[Order in the Vale\|Council of Seven]] (**357 AA**). |
| **IX** | **King Edric I** | 357–371 AA | *The Hollow Crown.* The first king to rule entirely under Council authority. His reign was defined by the quiet, humiliating work of learning what the crown now meant: ceremonial attendance, symbolic signature, endorsing decisions he had no hand in making. He did so without formal complaint. Historians disagree on whether this was wise pragmatism or a failure of ambition. He coined the phrase still used among the palace staff: *"The crown is heavy because it is empty."* |
| **X** | **King Halvard** | 371–383 AA | *The Patient.* The only Post-Council monarch to directly challenge the Council's authority. In **379 AA**, he refused to sign a confiscatory ration decree — an act with no constitutional precedent — and forced a three-month governance crisis before the Council legislated around his refusal. He never acted again, and the Council quietly closed the loophole he had exploited. Whether this makes him consequential or merely the last to try is debated. He is remembered with mild but genuine warmth by the common folk, who appreciated that someone tried. |
| **XI** | **King Aldric** | 383–393 AA | *The Luminous.* [[King Alfwyn]]'s father. The last fully public monarch — he attended all Council sessions, made regular appearances in [[Medsen]]'s lower districts, and maintained the performance of engaged rulership with apparent sincerity, even as he exercised no real power. Some historians suggest this was a deliberate strategy: giving his son an institution that still *looked* functional. Others note that Aldric's final years showed early signs of the same retreat that would later consume Alfwyn — increasing absences from court, longer periods spent in the Central Temple-Castle's inner chambers, a growing preoccupation with the Beacon's deep mechanisms that his advisors found difficult to redirect. |
| **XII** | **King Alfwyn** | 393 AA–present | *The Silent Sovereign.* The current ruler. Retreated into the Central Temple-Castle of [[Medsen]] in **393 AA** and has not been seen publicly since. Governs entirely through written decree and the voice of his **High Steward**. See [[King Alfwyn]]. |

## Notes on the Line

### Legitimacy
The Sun-Born claim descent from the Dawnfather through an unbroken bloodline. This lineage is accepted as political and spiritual fact by the [[Caretakers of the Beacon]] and the common population. The [[Aetherweaver Academy]] maintains private skepticism — their position is that the holy radiance attributed to Sun-Born rulers is a product of prolonged Beacon exposure rather than divine inheritance. King Aldhelm's display at **Three Bridges** remains the strongest counter-argument to this position.

### The "Sun-Touched" Phenotype
Several monarchs have been recorded with the distinctive pale, luminous skin and gold-toned eyes associated with the Sun-Born. This phenotype is most pronounced in those raised within the Central Temple-Castle and is considered evidence of divine blood by the faithful and of long-term Beacon resonance by the scholars.

### The Names
The royal naming tradition has shifted across the centuries — early kings used Armisian-lineage names (**Cenwald, Cynred, Aldhelm, Raedfryd**), while Queen Wendreda and King Oswin represent a softer, more Brightvalian-inflected tradition. **Alfwyn** is considered a fully post-Apostasy name with no clear Armisian root, which some historians view as meaningful and some view as coincidence.
