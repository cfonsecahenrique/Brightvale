# The Concordance

The Concordance is a **corpora** — not a single work but a living accumulation of manuscripts, illuminated field-journals, carved tablets, woven mnemonic tapestries, annotated song-cycles, and transcribed oral traditions, all gathered under one roof and treated as a unified body of scholarship. Its physical home is [[Aeldenbarrow]], the hidden Elven settlement in the Whisperwood Valley, where [[House Aerincorvus]] has maintained and extended it for three generations. It is the largest and most systematically organized repository of folk magic, druidic primeval practice, and Fae-contact documentation in the known world.

Most of Brightvale ignores it. The [[Aetherweaver Academy]] classifies it as folklore. [[The Council of Seven]] regards it with polite indifference. House Aerincorvus has spent decades compiling irrefutable evidence that the Concordance's findings deserve to be treated as a legitimate research priority — and so far, the rest of the Vale has declined to be irrefuted.

---

## Origins and Custodianship

The oldest strata of the Concordance predate the [[Apostasy]] by centuries. Druidic communities and [[Halflings|Halfling]] cultivators, operating largely outside the Armisian Empire's formal arcane institutions, were the first to compile their accumulated observations of folk magic into transmissible form. The name *Concordance* — a finding-of-agreement — was applied retroactively, when scholars recognized that independent compilations from dozens of communities were documenting the same underlying phenomena through different vocabularies.

The [[Apostasy]] destroyed a catastrophic portion of the original record. Much of the oldest material, held in outdoor sanctuaries and forest groves, was lost to the advancing [[Umbra]]. What survived did so because Halfling communities had duplicated it in domestic forms — in woven patterns, seasonal songs, the names given to days and weather — and because the Elves of Aeldenbarrow had maintained their own parallel archives within glamour-protected settlement walls.

[[House Aerincorvus]], the Vale's only noble house of established Elven-Human lineage, took formal custodianship of the surviving corpora following the Apostasy. Their mixed heritage gave them access to both the Halfling oral traditions and the Elvish documentary record, and their seat within Aeldenbarrow provided a secure home for the physical materials. Over the generations they have expanded the Concordance with new testimonies, updated field observations, and the dimming recollections of Aeldenbarrow's oldest Elven residents — some of whom remember the pre-Apostasy world directly.

## What the Concordance Contains

The Concordance is organized by **phenomenon** rather than by theory. It does not attempt to explain *why* folk magic or druidic practice works. It documents *what it does*, *under what conditions*, and *with what consequences* — including conditions that have not been reproducible since the Apostasy, recorded as historical observation without claim to current applicability.

**Primeval Attunement** — the oldest and largest section — records the behavior of natural forces when a practitioner is present with the right quality of attention. Wind, stone, root, seasonal light, the personalities of old hills and the memories of ancient trees. These passages resist being stated as rules; they read more like precise, observation-rich poetry about how a particular grove behaves in spring, and what a practitioner who has been there long enough can do with that knowledge.

**Pact-Lore** documents the terms, histories, and known consequences of agreements between communities and non-human entities. Fae pacts constitute the most extensive and most carefully guarded section. Some agreements recorded there remain, in the understanding of Aerincorvus archivists, potentially binding — meaning that an elder who has memorized the relevant passages holds not just historical knowledge but an active relationship.

**Herbalism and Grounds-Working** is the Concordance's most accessible material and the most widely pirated. The [[Aetherweaver Academy]]'s reagent markets in [[Aurore]] trade heavily on knowledge that ultimately derives from Concordance-documented groundswork. The Academy rarely acknowledges the source.

**Fae Documentation** is the Concordance's most significant and most contested body of scholarship, and the one House Aerincorvus regards as its central purpose. See below.

## The Fae Records

The Concordance is the greatest repository of knowledge about the [[Fae]] in existence. It establishes, across hundreds of independent accounts from dozens of communities over centuries, several conclusions that its archivists treat as settled — and that the rest of the Vale's institutions treat as speculation.

**The Fae are pre-Apostasy.** They did not originate with the Apostasy, were not created or altered by it, and their fundamental nature predates the conditions of the current world. This matters because it implies they carry information about the world as it was — and about what the Apostasy actually changed.

**The Fae are immune to the [[Umbra]].** Completely. The shadow does not corrupt them, does not touch them, does not register their presence in the way it registers everything else. The Concordance documents this not as a theological claim but as an empirical observation, repeated across sources with no apparent motive to agree with one another. The [[Aetherweaver Academy]] has no theoretical framework for this immunity. The Concordance does not claim to have one either — it simply records that it is true, consistently and without exception, across every credible account in the corpora.

**Some Fae willingly assimilate the Umbra.** This is the Concordance's most disturbing documented phenomenon, and its most carefully hedged. The Dark Fae — those who revel in entropy and chaos rather than grieve it — do not *become* Umbra-touched. They cannot. But accounts describe them reaching *into* the Umbra, taking on its mutations deliberately, using its corruption as a tool or an aesthetic. The Concordance is careful to distinguish this from actual corruption: a Dark Fae working through the Umbra is entropy with intention, with craft, with something that reads almost like humor. The Umbra itself remains mindless. The Fae remains sovereign.

**Encounters in the Umbra.** The Concordance contains a significant archive of accounts describing Fae beings observed *within* the Umbra — not corrupted, not fleeing it, but present and apparently purposeful. These accounts derive from two sources: the dimming recollections of Aeldenbarrow's oldest Elves, who remember the world before the Apostasy and have fragments of memory that don't fit any current framework; and the folklore of Vale communities, which has preserved, in story and ritual form, descriptions of encounters that the people telling them no longer fully understand. The Concordance archivists do not reconcile these accounts. They preserve them, cross-reference them, and note what patterns recur.

## The Archivists and the Primeval Aether Theory

Within Aerincorvus's scholarly community, a deeper sect has developed around a specific interpretation of the Fae documentation. They call themselves **Concordance Archivists** — a title that, to outsiders, sounds redundant, since all of them are archivists. Within the community, it marks a specific theoretical commitment.

The Archivists' central claim is this: **Fae magic and Primeval Natural Aether are not merely similar — they are indistinguishable.**

The Primeval Aether is the term they use for the undifferentiated magical force present in specific locations throughout the known world: certain ancient groves, megaliths arranged in pre-Apostasy patterns, springs that have never run dry, glade-clearings where the air tastes different. These points are not evenly distributed. They are not understood. The Concordance documents them, but has never produced a theory of why they exist where they exist.

The Archivist position is that these locations are not merely *near* Fae phenomena — they *are* Fae phenomena, or the trace left by them. That the material world, in these places, has been saturated with something functionally identical to the essence of the Faewilds. And that this saturation, if a practitioner knows how to work with it, can be used for one specific and desperately relevant purpose: **warding against the Umbra**.

The logic follows from the Fae's documented immunity. If the Fae cannot be touched by the Umbra, and if their fundamental nature is inseparable from the Primeval Aether, and if that Aether is present and manipulable at specific points in the world — then those points, properly worked, should repel the Umbra the way the Fae themselves do. Not the Beacon's way, which pushes back with light and sacrifice. But the Fae's way, which is simply *not registering* as something the Umbra can affect.

This theory has not been proven. The Archivists have conducted field work at a number of documented Primeval Aether sites, with results they describe as "promising" and their critics describe as "anecdotal." The [[Aetherweaver Academy]] has not engaged with the theory on its merits — the Old Magic faction has expressed interest in the underlying data, and been gently declined.

What the Archivists have not told anyone outside Aeldenbarrow is that several of their field sites have shown what they consider meaningful results in the months since the [[The Beacon|Beacon]] began visibly dimming. The connection between the Beacon's decline and the apparent strengthening of Primeval Aether sites is a pattern they are still documenting. They are not yet certain what it means. They are quite certain that the timing is not coincidental.
