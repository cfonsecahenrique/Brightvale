# The Noble Houses of Brightvale

*"The Houses are the Empire's echo — the part that survived by being useful."*

The **Noble Houses** are the remnants of the Armisian Empire's aristocracy. Some trace their lineages to before the Apostasy. Others were elevated afterward for services rendered during the collapse. Together, they form the feudal backbone of Brightvale's social order — holding land rights, funding institutions, competing for the two rotating Council seats, and maneuvering against each other in ways that would be exhausting if the Umbra wasn't also trying to kill everyone.

---

## The Old Houses
*Pre-Apostasy lineages. They were noble before the world ended.*

---

### House Aerincorvus
**Motto:** *"Between two worlds, we are the door."*
**Seat:** The Overgrown Palazzo, Medsen
**Heraldry:** A crow on silver ivy; deep forest black and iridescent green.

The Vale's only noble house of established Elven-Human lineage, Aerincorvus balances Medsen court politics with deep ties to druidic communities and Fae researchers. Their defining project is the **Concordance** — a massive archive documenting Fae-contact anomalies throughout the Vale. They believe the Faewilds were *sealed*, not destroyed, during the Apostasy, and they are quietly compiling evidence to prove it and force the Council to treat Fae studies as a legitimate research priority.

Most of the Vale considers this folklore. Aerincorvus considers the Vale's opinion a data point.

#### The Concordance

The Concordance is not a book. It is a *corpora* — a living accumulation of manuscripts, illuminated field-journals, carved tablets, woven mnemonic tapestries, annotated song-cycles, and transcribed oral traditions, all gathered under one roof and treated as a unified body of scholarship. Its physical home is Aeldenbarrow, where House Aerincorvus has maintained and extended it for three generations. It is the largest systematically organized repository of folk magic, druidic practice, and Fae-contact documentation in the known world.

The **Aetherweaver Academy** classifies it as folklore. The **Council of Seven** regards it with polite indifference. House Aerincorvus has spent decades compiling what it considers irrefutable evidence that the Concordance's findings deserve to be treated as a legitimate research priority. So far, the rest of the Vale has declined to be irrefuted.

The Concordance is organized by phenomenon, not by theory. It documents *what folk magic and druidic practice do*, under what conditions, and with what consequences — without claiming to explain why. Its four major bodies of scholarship:

- **Primeval Attunement** — the oldest and largest section. Records how natural forces respond when a practitioner is present with the right quality of attention: wind, stone, root, seasonal light, the personalities of old hills, the memories of ancient trees. These passages resist being stated as rules. They read like precise, observation-rich poetry about how a particular grove behaves in spring — and what someone who has been there long enough can do with that knowledge.
- **Pact-Lore** — the terms, histories, and known consequences of agreements between communities and non-human entities. The Fae section is the most extensive and most carefully guarded. Some agreements recorded there remain, in the archivists' understanding, potentially binding — meaning an elder who has memorized the relevant passages holds not just historical knowledge, but an active relationship.
- **Herbalism and Grounds-Working** — the Concordance's most accessible and most pirated section. The Academy's reagent markets in Aurore trade heavily on knowledge that ultimately derives from Concordance-documented groundswork. The Academy rarely acknowledges the source.
- **Fae Documentation** — the Concordance's central purpose and most contested body of scholarship. Hundreds of independent accounts, from dozens of communities across centuries, establishing three conclusions the archivists treat as settled: the Fae predate the Apostasy and carry information about the world as it was; the Fae are completely immune to the Umbra — documented empirically, not theologically; and some Fae willingly work *through* the Umbra, treating its corruption as a tool, without themselves becoming corrupted.

Within Aerincorvus's scholarly community, a deeper sect has developed around a specific interpretation of the Fae records. They call themselves **Concordance Archivists** — a title that sounds redundant to outsiders, since all of them are archivists. Within the community it marks a theoretical commitment: that Fae magic and a force they call *Primeval Aether* — present at specific ancient sites throughout the Vale, groves and megaliths and springs that have never run dry — are not merely similar but indistinguishable.

The theory follows from the Fae's documented immunity. If the Fae cannot be touched by the Umbra, and if certain locations in the material world carry that same fundamental nature, then those locations, properly worked, might *not register* as something the Umbra can affect. Not the Beacon's way, which pushes back with light and sacrifice. The Fae's way: simply not being something the shadow can touch.

The theory has not been proven. The Academy has not engaged with it on its merits. What the Archivists have not told anyone outside Aeldenbarrow is that several of their field sites have shown what they consider meaningful results in the months since the Beacon began visibly dimming. They are not yet certain what the connection means. They are quite certain the timing is not coincidental.

*Good for characters with Elven heritage, druidic connections, or interest in the Faewilds.*

### House Aurelian
**Motto:** *"We remember the sky."*
**Seat:** The Solarium, Medsen
**Heraldry:** A golden eagle above a half-submerged sun; pale gold and faded white.

Formerly the Armisian Empire's agricultural nobility, the Aurelians worship the sun as the practical source of all life — and grieve, quietly and perpetually, its absence. They manage the Vale's major seed vaults and experimental greenhouses, stubbornly cultivating crops to thrive under Beacon-light. Their warm alliance with the Caretakers and their funding of astronomical research with House Lugner make them one of the Council's most reliable conservative votes.

Their optimism is genuine, and it occasionally makes them easy to exploit.

*Good for characters with religious convictions, agrarian backgrounds, or interest in what the world looked like before the veil closed.*

---

### House Iyengar
**Motto:** *"In every tongue, one word: endure."*
**Seat:** The Amber Court, Medsen
**Heraldry:** A crossed blade and quill over an open fist; amber and deep teal.

Half-orc by origin — descended from an ancient marriage between an Armisian noble and an Orcish Prince — the Iyengar are Brightvale's neutral arbitrators. They combine institutional governance with Orcish directness and a deep reverence for covenants. Their Paladins draw power from personal ideals rather than divine light, a theological independence the Caretakers find quietly offensive.

They want to become an official Council-chartered arbitration body. They also want formal legal recognition extended to Orcenfolk communities outside Medsen. They have wanted both for a long time.

*Good for characters with diplomatic, legal, or Orcish heritage; Paladins who draw on personal conviction.*

---

### House Kaewdyn
**Motto:** *"The blood remembers what the mind forgets."*
**Seat:** The Ashen Hall, Medsen
**Heraldry:** A silver lion holding a pale dragon's scale; deep charcoal and bone-white.

Kaewdyn carries draconic resonance from an ancient union with the extinct House Rjioha. Their "Ashen-Heirs" share a striking pale physiology and can physically sense the Umbra's malignant resonance — a gift that comes with a heavy psychological toll. Mandatory service in the Emberguard precedes any Kaewdyn claim to leadership.

They are warriors, not politicians. Medsen court life bores them. Their ambition is to officially merge the House into the Emberguard's highest command, which Lord Commander Varis Lugner supports in principle and Medsen's aristocracy opposes in practice.

*Good for characters with draconic or martial heritage, or those who want an instinctive connection to the Umbra's presence.*

---

### House Lugner
**Motto:** *"Truth is buried. We dig."*
**Seat:** The Pale Sanctum, Medsen
**Heraldry:** A lidded eye over a burning candle; white and ash-grey.

Brightvale's strictest intellectual force — and one of its most controversial. Pre-dating the Apostasy, House Lugner was a lineage of magisters and deep-weave scholars. When the Apostasy fell, their ancestral matriarch struck a forbidden pact with a Void-Binder to preserve their arcane legacy. The consequence: many Lugner descendants are born as Infernis Tieflings, bearing the "Azure Stain" of gray-blue skin and dark horns.

They fund the Old Magic faction at the Academy and believe, passionately, that recovering the pre-Apostasy Deep-Weaves is the only path to real survival. Their most famous member, Lord Commander Varis Lugner, abandoned his arcane studies to command the Emberguard. The House has not forgiven him.

*Good for Infernis characters, scholarly types, or those interested in pre-Apostasy magical history.*

---

### House Von Zoltraak
**Motto:** *"The old lamp lit the road to the cliff. We build a new one."*
**Seat:** The Crucible, Aurore mid-tier
**Heraldry:** A flame in an open hand; deep violet and burnished copper.

The progressive counterweight to House Lugner. The Von Zoltraaks are unsentimental about their Armisian lineage and blunt about their conclusions: Old Magic failed. The Apostasy proves it. The future lies in new arcane forms engineered specifically to fight the current Umbra reality — not in resurrecting what already failed once.

They fund the Progressive Aetherweaving faction at the Academy and maintain the Crucible's independent labs. Their greatest ambition: engineer a portable, non-Beacon-dependent Umbra-suppression field. Their strategist Drago Von Zoltraak was publicly sacrificed to the Beacon by Queen Wendreda after the Brightblood War, despite having fought for the Reprieve at Three Bridges. A portrait of Drago hangs in the Crucible's central hall, facing the door.

*Good for progressive mages, engineers, or characters motivated by practical survival over historical reverence.*

## The Recent Houses
*Post-Apostasy elevations. They earned their titles in the new world, not the old one.*

---

### House Ashwood
**Motto:** *"The grove outlasts the storm."*
**Seat:** The Greenmantle Lodge, Northern Plateau edge
**Heraldry:** A great oak with extending roots; deep forest green and earthy brown.

Elevated from common foresters who defended vital wilderness from the Umbra after the Apostasy, Ashwood is Brightvale's steadfast ecological advocate. They maintain critical timber reserves and supply the timber the Academy depends on for construction and infrastructure — leverage they manage carefully. Their ongoing battle to enshrine a permanent Forested Reserve into Council law has been their primary political project for three consecutive Council cycles.

Progress is slow. The trees, at least, are patient.

*Good for rangers, druids, characters with roots in the common people, or those motivated by conservation.*

---

### House Drevorn
**Motto:** *"What is held cannot be taken."*
**Seat:** The Ironweight Vault, Medsen
**Heraldry:** A closed fist gripping scales; deep burgundy and grey.

The Drevorns did not fight their way to nobility, nor were they elevated for bloodline. They out-logisticed the old elite during the Apostasy crisis — storing grain, extending credit, managing supply lines when the Old Houses were busy losing a war — and never stopped. Today they control critical physical assets, trade routes, and a ledger of noble debt so extensive that half the Vale's aristocracy owes them money.

They are widely resented. Patriarch **Corvin Drevorn** openly intends to secure a permanent Council seat within his lifetime, and he has twenty years of accumulated favors to spend on it. The Old Houses hate that he might succeed.

*Good for merchant backgrounds, information brokers, or characters navigating Medsen's political underworld.*

---

### House Kaze
**Motto:** *(None recorded)*
**Seat:** Multiple relay stations; administrative office in Medsen
**Ancestry:** Predominantly Orcenfolk

Kaze rose to nobility through an absolute monopoly on speed. They own and operate Brightvale's horse relay system — and in a world where horses are extraordinarily rare, this means they own the fastest communication network in the Vale. A message travels the 30 kilometers from Emberfort to Medsen in under four hours. In a world where delayed military reports can mean an Umbra breach, this makes them indispensable to both the Council and the Emberguard.

The Kaze know exactly what that indispensability is worth.

*Good for Orcish characters, messengers, spies, or those with connections to the Vale's transportation networks.*

---

### House Ravenna
**Motto:** *"Every road leads somewhere. We know where."*
**Seat:** The Wayhouse, Medsen courier district
**Heraldry:** A crow in flight over a forked road; charcoal and slate blue.

Elevated from scouts who mapped Brightvale's first stable routes during the Apostasy, House Ravenna runs the definitive courier and intelligence network of the Vale. Their impeccable courier service doubles as an information operation. No one lies in a Ravenna post, because Ravenna clerks recognize inconsistencies.

Their ambition: legitimize what they already are. Transmute their private courier service into a state-funded, Council-protected intelligence body. Every major faction has considered what that would mean for their own secrets. Most have concluded it would not be ideal.

Grand Explorer Mira Ravenna shares the House name and operates with them intricately.

*Good for rogues, information traders, scouts, or characters with shadowy institutional connections.*

---

### House Thalum
**Motto:** *"The mountain holds. So shall we."*
**Seat:** The Marrow Hall, Medsen's lower plateau
**Heraldry:** A pickaxe and mason's level crossed; slate grey and iron brown.
**Note:** Engineering lineage pre-dating the Apostasy; elevated formally after it.

House Thalum built Brightvale before the Apostasy struck. Literally: their engineering corps designed and constructed the plateau's walls, fortifications, and infrastructure. They maintain them still, and they are perpetually frustrated by politicians who defer crucial maintenance until structures fail. Their ongoing rivalry with Hvergi's Dwarven mining community over quarrying rights has been running for generations without resolution.

They own the plateau's bedrock by right of first survey. They consider this a serious legal claim.

*Good for engineers, soldiers, or characters from Medsen's working class with little patience for aristocratic theater.*

## The Extinct Houses
*They did not survive. Their stories explain why.*

---

### House Rjioha
**Status:** Extinct — Armisian High Nobility
**Heraldry:** A ship's prow cresting a wave with a star compass; deep sea-blue and silver.

Once the Armisian Empire's seafaring arm — responsible for intercontinental exploration, diplomacy, and trade. Their entire fleet was caught at sea when the Apostasy struck. Not a single vessel returned. The house was wiped from existence in hours.

Their genetics survive in House Kaewdyn through an ancestral marriage, which is the source of the Ashen-Heirs' draconic resonance. Modern Explorers maintain a small shrine to House Rjioha at Emberfort, considering them the patron saints of the lost expedition.

---

### House Volex
**Status:** Extinct — Armisian High Nobility
**Heraldry:** A dragon rampant with spread wings; crimson and black.

The military linchpin of the Armisian Empire, Volex held a biological bond with war-dragons. They were the first house to fall in the Apostasy — not to the Umbra directly, but to a mysterious biological breakdown of their draconic bond. As the bond failed, the dragons mutated and Hollowed, bringing the Volex riders down with them.

Their last physical legacy: the Aetherweaver Academy's secured vaults hold the final confirmed surviving dragon eggs in stasis, their viability and potential Umbra-taint unknown. Whether the breakdown of the draconic bond was the first symptom of the Apostasy — or one of its causes — is a question scholars have argued about for four centuries.
