# The Veiled God's Lantern — GM Notes
*One-Shot | Year 387 AA | 4 Players, Level 5 | Daggerheart SRD*

---

> **PREMISE.** The Council of Seven, following the 386 AA Oracle cycle, dispatches a team of elite Venturers to locate a site of divine power in the Wastes. The Caretakers frame it as a mission to find something that could strengthen the Beacon. What the party finds instead is the truth behind it.
>
> **TONE.** Rogue One. Tragic. These characters are remembered, not saved.

---

## OPENING CUTSCENE — The Oracle Chamber, 386 AA
*Read this aloud before the session begins. Do not take questions. Let it breathe.*

---

*The chamber smells of hot stone and something sweeter — like hair too close to a candle. Thirteen husks sit in a perfect circle, unmoving, facing inward. Their lips are sealed. Their hands rest folded in their laps. Three senior Vision Readers stand at the threshold in ceremonial red. The eldest steps forward.*

**FIRST READER:** "The cycle turns. The Beacon endures. We descend to listen."

*Silence. Then — not from one husk, but from the room itself — a sound like many voices breathing the same word at the wrong time.*

**ORACLES:** "You descend. You have never ascended."

**SECOND READER:** *(quietly, to the others)* "Standard deflection. Press for the vision."

**FIRST READER:** *(louder, formal)* "The Beacon dims. What does the fire hunger for?"

**ORACLES:** "The fire hungers for nothing. The fire takes everything. You have mistaken consumption for protection for three hundred years."

**THIRD READER:** *(making notes)* "Metaphor. The Oracle conflates the Umbra threat with the Beacon's light. Standard distortion."

**SECOND READER:** "Where? Speak of the place. Speak of the vision."

**ORACLES:** *"...She still weeps. In the valley where the sky was once a different colour. She holds the feather and she weeps, and the old passage holds open beneath her stone hands. We sent you a door. We sent you a door and you called it a prize."*

**FIRST READER:** *(to the others, satisfied)* "There. A location. A source of power." *(to the Oracles:)* "An artifact. To strengthen the light."

*A pause. The temperature in the room drops slightly.*

**ORACLES:** *"...Send those who will carry fire. Send those who will listen when we speak plainly. Do not send your readers. Your readers only read what they already believe."*

**THIRD READER:** *(snapping shut his journal)* "The vision is confirmed. Location bearing southwest. Possible divine artifact." *(quieter, not for the record:)* "The rest is degradation. Ignore it."

*The Readers turn to leave. The wards begin to reseal. Just before the door closes:*

**ORACLES:** *(barely audible)* "We have been trying to tell you for three hundred years. We will try again. We will always try again."

*The door seals. The eldest Reader does not look back.*

## ACT ONE — Emberfort's Gates
*The party has already received the Council Commission. They assemble at the gates of Emberfort, ready to descend into the Wastes.*

### Scene Goal
Establish the party as a unit. Establish Aldred. Depart. This act is short — atmosphere and character, not mechanics.

### Aldred — First Impression
Aldred is a mid-ranking Caretaker bureaucrat, fifties, soft around the edges. He arrives slightly late, flustered, carrying too many satchels. He mispronounces one of the party member's names. He is enthusiastic in a way that feels performative, asks unnecessary questions about supply counts, and at one point drops his notes in a puddle.

He is playing a role. Do not tip this yet.

> **GM NOTE:** Aldred's buffoonery should be charming, not annoying. The reveal in Act Three lands harder if the players have grown fond of the idiot. Give him one genuine moment of warmth — he compliments whoever seems most nervous about the mission, sincerely.

### What to Establish in Act One
- The mission is prestigious and official — the Council does not do this lightly
- The Beacon has been visibly flickering for months; people in Emberfort have noticed
- The Caretakers' framing: a source of power awaits; bring back what you find
- Aldred carries a sealed Caretaker map pouch that he will not open in front of the party ("Protocol. We review the directional records once we're moving.")
- The descent into the Wastes is treated as a serious moment by the Emberguard on the gate — a brief, respectful send-off

## ACT TWO — The Wastes
*Tutorial act. Three encounters of escalating weight, punctuated by clock progress. Aldred the buffoon accompanies throughout.*

---

### THE SEARCH CLOCK — 6 Segments
*Used to track progress toward the temple. Advance the clock through player actions, successful rolls, and encounter resolutions.*

| Segment | Narrative Beat |
|:---:|:---|
| **1** | The plateau edge falls away. The Wastes spread below — grey, vast, quiet. |
| **2** | The ash here is finer than near Medsen. Something has been burning a long time, far away. |
| **3** | Vegetation changes. Black-veined flowers grow in clusters, all shut tight, facing nowhere. |
| **4** | A Revenant patrol — moving with purpose, toward something specific. |
| **5** | The Umbra mist thickens. But it parts ahead, as if reluctant, around a shape in the distance. |
| **6** | The temple. Half-swallowed by the earth. Older than anything any of them have ever seen. |

**Advance the clock by 1:** Successful navigation or survival roll; Aldred consulting his notes (he knows more than he admits).
**Advance by 2:** Resolving an encounter with no major injuries; a critical success on any exploration roll.
**Complication on failure:** Clock still advances by 1, but add a consequence — an injury, a lost resource, a horde drawn to their light.

---

### ENCOUNTER 1 — Revenant Patrol
**Purpose:** Teach basic combat. Hope/Fear. Action economy.

3–4 Standard Revenants. They move in formation — flanking, patient, waiting for the party's lights to weaken. Use this encounter to show that Revenants are not mindless — they read the battlefield.

> **Narrative beat:** One Revenant is wearing tattered Venturer's gear. An Explorers' Guild patch, barely legible. Someone came this way before and did not return.

**After the encounter:** Aldred is shaken in a way that briefly drops the performance. He stares at the dead Venturer's patch for a moment. Then he straightens up and says something self-deprecating to break the tension. The players may notice the pause.

---

### ENCOUNTER 2 — Umbra-Horde
**Purpose:** Environmental hazard. Teach Endless, AoE, terrain as resource.

The terrain here is corrupted — bloated, faintly luminescent ground, the Wastes at their most organic and wrong. Use the **Umbra-Horde statblock**.

> **Environment feature — Ashgeld Lantern Flare:** A PC can use their Ashgeld lantern as an action. Spend 1 Hope: all creatures within Close range take **2d6 magic damage**. The Horde cannot use **Endless** until the end of the next GM turn.

**Narrative beat:** The Horde is drawn toward the party's warmth and light. But the density of corruption here — the reason there are so many — is that they are at the edge of something. The Umbra clusters around the temple like moths at glass.

---

### ENCOUNTER 3 — The Approach
**No combat.** Investigation and dread.

A threshold stone blocks the path — a piece of ancient architecture half-emerged from the earth. It depicts a figure whose face has not been destroyed. It has simply been worn smooth by centuries of wind and forgetting.

The party can examine it freely. Investigation and lore rolls reveal:
- This is pre-Apostasy. Older than the Armisian Empire. The architectural style is unlike anything in Medsen's records.
- The script on the base repeats one word, over and over, in a language no one fully knows. A scholar in the party might partially translate: the word means something between *passage* and *release.*
- The figure's posture is devotional — not commanding, not triumphant. Patient.

> **GM NOTE:** Aldred examines this stone for a long moment. If pressed, he says only: "I've seen this script before. In fragments. In places the Order keeps locked." He doesn't elaborate. Another crack in the façade — but small.

**Clock advances to 6 after this scene.** The temple is visible ahead.

---

## ACT THREE — The Veiled God's Temple

---
### Entering the Temple
The exterior is half-consumed by the earth, as if the Wastes grew around it slowly, cautiously. The stone is pale and close-grained. It does not crumble when touched. Some deep preservation is at work — not aetheric, not Beacon-fire. Something older, quieter, and still present.

Inside: silence of a particular quality. Not empty silence — occupied silence, the kind that exists in a room where something has been waiting.

---

### The Veiled God's Face — Read Aloud When the Party First Sees It

*At the far end of the temple, rising from floor to ceiling, is a face carved in relief.*

*She is beautiful — not in the idealized way of royal portraits, but in the way of something very old that was made with great care. Long hair overflows from a deep hood that covers her eyes entirely. Her hands are raised before her, both holding a single long feather between them — the gesture of an offering, or perhaps a vow.*

*From the corner of one covered eye, a single tear descends. Carved in the stone, but dark — stained with something that was never quite cleaned away. Something that seeped from the rock itself.*

*She has no name written beneath her. The space where a devotional title would be carved is blank. Not defaced. Not chiselled away. Simply... empty. As if whoever built this temple could not bring themselves to write her name somewhere it might be forgotten.*

---

### Investigation Finds
Let the party explore freely before anything else happens.

- **The offerings:** On the altar below the carved face, dried offerings — coins, faded flowers, a few strips of cloth. And among them: **Ashgeld**. Current-minted Ashgeld, from within the last decade. Someone has been coming here. Recently. Deliberately.
- **The feather:** Identical feathers — large, black-shafted, with a slight iridescent sheen — appear carved in repeating motifs throughout the temple. In one alcove, an actual feather, physical and preserved, is pinned beneath a stone. It does not decay. When touched, it is faintly warm.
- **The dominion:** With a successful lore roll, a character can piece together this god's purpose from the iconography — transition, passage, the border between states of being. A deity who presided over the crossing from life to death to whatever came after. Not a god of death itself. A god of *continuation.*
- **Aldred's reaction:** He is quiet throughout the investigation. When the party turns to him, he is standing in front of the god's face, looking up at her. He says, very quietly: "I've dreamed of this room. Three times. The same dream. I never told anyone."

---

### Aldred's Reveal
*This happens naturally — when the party has finished their investigation and finds no artifact, no obvious power source, nothing the Caretakers claimed would be here.*

Aldred sets down his satchels. All of them, deliberately, on the floor. He straightens up. The flustered energy is gone.

> **Read aloud:**
>
> *"There is no artifact. You've already worked that out. There was never going to be one."*
>
> *He looks at the god's face, not at you.*
>
> *"I've attended seven Oracle cycles. Seven. Each time, I watched the Vision Readers dismiss anything the husks said that didn't confirm what the Caretakers already believed. Seven times, I transcribed 'metaphor' and 'degradation' into the margins. And then, four months ago, I started dreaming in a voice that wasn't mine. Not nightmares — instructions. A face I'd never seen. A room I'd never been in. This room."*
>
> *He turns to face the party.*
>
> *"I don't know what they're going to tell us. I don't know what's behind it. But I know the Vision Readers heard something true last year and buried it. And I know whoever built this temple wanted us — specifically us, specifically now — to light a fire inside it."*
>
> *He pulls from his coat a small Ashgeld ingot — the kind used to kindle a Beacon-flame in the field. "Protocol says we establish respite before concluding a search." A pause. "That part, I was told to follow exactly."*

He lights the fire.

---

### The Blue Flame — The Oracles Speak
*The Ashgeld flame catches — and turns. The amber-white of the Beacon's warmth is gone. What burns is a deep, quiet blue, like the bottom of something very old and very cold.*

*And then they are here.*

*Thirteen translucent figures, blue and barely solid, seated in the same circle they have held for three hundred years — now surrounding the flame, surrounding the party. They sit with their hands folded in their laps. They do not look at anything. But they are present in a way that fills the room.*

*When they speak, the voices come from all of them at once, sometimes overlapping, sometimes completing each other mid-sentence.*

---

> **ORACLE DIALOGUE — Read slowly. Pause between each passage.**

**"The Beacon does not protect you."**
*A pause.*
**"It burns you. Every soul within its light — slowly. Imperceptibly. You will not feel it until there is nothing left to feel."**

---

**"She above you — she shepherded the passage. Life to death to what comes after. The passage is blocked."**
*A pause.*
**"The dead do not move on. They become fuel. You eat with their hands and spend their faces as coin and call it providence."**

---

**"The highest Caretakers have always known. The choice was made for you, in the dark, without your voice."**
*A pause.*
**"They chose a slow death you survive over a true death you deserve. They are not wrong."**
*A longer pause.*
**"They are not forgiven."**

---

**"There are other places like this one. They wake as the Beacon dims. When the light fails enough —"**
*— the voices finish together —*
**"— they will answer. Someone must be ready."**

---

*One final beat. All of them, at once, with something almost like relief:*

**"You heard us."**

*The ceiling breaks.*

---

### The Shatter-Wing Descends — Aldred Dies

*A sound like a cathedral collapsing inward. Six wings of jagged black glass tear through the stone above. The blue flame gutters but holds.*

*The Shatter-Wing does not look at the party. It does not look at the exit or the flame.*

*It looks at Aldred.*

*He has just enough time to meet its gaze. His expression is not fear — it is something closer to recognition.*

*Then it strikes.*

> **Aldred dies instantly.** The Shatter-Wing's first action is a Soul Sever directed at him alone — the Angel is drawn to his "holiness," the divine resonance of a man who received the Oracles' visions directly. The result is not a body. What remains is already ash.

> **GM NOTE:** This moment sets the stakes immediately. Aldred was not a prop — the players liked him. His death should land. Give it a beat of silence before the next wave crashes in. Then: two Umbra-Hordes pour through the broken ceiling behind the Angel.

**Enemies present:**
- 1× **Umbra-Touched Angel** (Void Seraph) — Solo, Tier 3 — see statblock
- 2× **Umbra-Horde** — Tier 2 — see statblock

**The Angel's priority:** It perceives the Beacon's fire as an insult and the party as carriers of it. It will target whoever holds the Ashgeld light source first after Aldred.

**The blue flame:** The Veiled God's residue repels the Angel while the flame burns. It cannot enter Very Close range of the flame without taking **2d8 magic damage** (no roll). It will attempt to extinguish the flame — if it succeeds, this protection ends.

**The temple is not defensible.** The goal is not to kill the Shatter-Wing. The goal is to escape.

## ACT FOUR — The Flight
*The party must get out of the temple, across the Wastes, and back to Brightvale. The Shatter-Wing pursues. The Hordes pursue. The terrain from Act Two is now a gauntlet.*

### Design Principle
Every player should face a moment where staying to fight is what allows others to escape. Do not script deaths — but design the math so that at least one character, and probably more, does not make it to the walls. The knowledge must reach Brightvale. The people carrying it may not.

### Scene Beats

**Exit the temple:** The blue flame can be carried in an Ashgeld vessel — one PC can take it. It continues to repel the Angel at Very Close range but costs an action to maintain each round. If dropped or extinguished, the protection ends immediately.

**The Wastes — the gauntlet:** Use the Act Two terrain in reverse. The black-veined flowers are open now, facing the noise. The corrupted ground from Encounter 2 is active — Horde creatures move through it as if it were open water. Bottleneck points exist: a collapsed archway, a narrow ravine, the threshold stone from Encounter 3.

**The Shatter-Wing:** It does not close in immediately. It circles, higher than attack range, its Inverted Hymn Countdown already running. The pressure is constant but not always direct — until a PC separates from the group.

**The walls of Brightvale:** At the Beacon's boundary, the Shatter-Wing stops. It does not cross. It hovers at the edge for a long moment, watching whoever made it back, then turns and climbs away into the dark. Whether this reads as defeat or satisfaction is deliberately ambiguous.

### The Final Problem
Whoever survives cannot simply walk to the Dawnfather Temple and announce what they know. They have moments before the escort arrives.

**Give each surviving player one beat to choose how they pass the knowledge.** Not to whom — to what. A name spoken to a stranger. A page torn from a journal. A piece of Aldred's coat with something written inside it, pressed into a child's hand in the crowd. The method is theirs. The seed is planted.

## EPILOGUE A — The Official Version
*For use if one or more players survive.*

The escort is already waiting at the gates — Senior Caretakers in formal white, flanked by Emberguard. Not a welcoming party. An interception.

The mission is reviewed on the spot. No artifact. No source of divine power. No evidence of anything recovered. The Caretaker liaison is dead. The temple was not found.

> **The Caretakers' framing, delivered formally:**
> *"The expedition encountered extreme Umbra density and was forced to retreat. The mission is marked incomplete. Standard protocol requires a debrief under supervised conditions before the Venturers return to active duty."*

The debrief is an arrest. The Emberguard close in politely, firmly, and the party is taken to separate rooms.

They are not harmed. They are kept for as long as it takes — days, possibly weeks — for the Caretakers to be satisfied that the knowledge has not spread. Eventually they are released, quietly, back into their lives.

By then, word has moved through the taverns and Guildhalls of Medsen: the Venturers returned empty-handed, shaken, speaking of visions in the Wastes. A shame, what extended Umbra exposure does to even the best of them.

They are not imprisoned. They are simply no longer believed.

> **Final image:** One of the surviving characters passes, days later, the person they pressed the knowledge into. A stranger in a market. A guard at a door. A child with a scrap of cloth. The person gives no sign of recognition. The character does not stop.

## EPILOGUE B — The Cycle Turns Again
*Year 399 AA. Read this aloud at the very end of the session, after Epilogue A lands.*

---

*Thirteen years pass.*

*The Oracle chamber beneath the Dawnfather Temple. The wards unseal on schedule — they always do. Three Vision Readers descend the stairs. Two are young, newly appointed, carrying fresh journals and the careful energy of people who want to do this correctly. The third is older. He was here before. He remembers the last time.*

*He remembers the mission he sent.*

*The chamber is unchanged. It is always unchanged. The thirteen husks in their circle, the blistering heat, the silence that is not quite silence. The eldest Reader steps forward and speaks the ritual opening.*

**ELDEST READER:** "The cycle turns. The Beacon endures. We descend to listen."

*A pause — longer than usual. The two younger Readers exchange a glance.*

*Then the room speaks.*

**ORACLES:** *"The previous expedition. The ones you sent to where she weeps."*

*A silence.*

**ORACLES:** *"What became of them?"*

*The two younger Readers look to their senior. He does not look back at them. He is looking at the husks — at the one nearest him, whose folded hands have not moved in three centuries.*

*He opens his mouth. Closes it. Opens it again.*

**ELDEST READER:** *"...They were debriefed. The mission was marked incomplete."*

*The room waits.*

**ORACLES:** *"That is not what we asked."*

*Another silence. Longer.*

**ELDEST READER:** *(quietly, to no one in particular)* "They were released. Eventually."

*The husks do not respond. They do not need to. The question has been asked and the answer has been given and the room holds both of them at once. One of the younger Readers has stopped writing.*

*After a long moment, the Oracles speak again — and this time their voices are not addressed to the Readers at all.*

**ORACLES:** *"The seeds were planted. Some took root."*

*A pause.*

**ORACLES:** *"We will wait."*

*The eldest Reader closes the ritual. He speaks the words correctly. He seals the chamber. He walks back up the stairs without looking at the other two.*

*He does not sleep well that year.*

### THE MONTAGE — Seeds in 399 AA
*After reading the above, present these as slow, cinematic vignettes — brief sketches of people living in Medsen and Emberfort in 399 AA. These are proposals for main campaign characters. Invite the players to choose the seed they want to develop.*

*Pause between each one. Let them breathe.*

---

**THE KEEPER**

*A back room in the lower quarter of Medsen. A young adult — grown up fast, grown up in the margins. On the wall above their cot, pinned with a nail, is a scrap of cloth. Old, stained, clearly cut from a coat. Something is written on the inside in faded ink, in a handwriting that isn't theirs.*

*They have read it so many times they no longer need to look at it.*

*They work as a lay Caretaker. They perform the rites correctly. They ask too many questions about the Beacon's mechanics and have been told, three times now, to stop. They have not stopped. They are waiting for the person who will know what the writing means.*

---

**THE SCHOLAR**

*A lower archive in the Aetherweaver Academy. Late. Everyone else has gone home.*

*A woman in her thirties surrounded by fifteen years of notes — cross-referenced, annotated, connected by lines of ink across a dozen pages pinned to the wall. At the centre of the web: a single torn page, not in her handwriting, that someone pressed into her hands in a crowd when she was a student and she has never been able to explain or discard.*

*Her peers call her work folklore. Her superiors have twice declined to review it formally.*

*The Beacon has been visibly flickering for months. Her notes say this was expected. Her notes say what comes next.*

---

**THE SOLDIER**

*A veteran's quarters in Emberfort. Worn boots. A bunk that's been slept in for fifteen years.*

*He's still here — First Slayer rank now, or close to it. On the last night of a rotation, when the younger Venturers are asleep, he sometimes takes a small notebook from under his mattress. The cover is water-damaged. The handwriting inside belongs to a dying Venturer who found him at the gates thirteen years ago and made him repeat every word back.*

*He still can. He has never shown it to anyone.*

*He is starting to think that was the wrong choice.*

---

**THE READER**

*Aeldenbarrow, the hidden Elven settlement. The Concordance library — the real one, not the public collection.*

*A figure of mixed Elven-Human lineage sits at a reading table with two books open side by side: one is an old field journal, unsigned, covering an expedition into the southwestern Wastes. The other is a section of the Concordance — the Primeval Aether research, the chapter on nodes.*

*The figure has drawn lines connecting passages in both books.*

*The lines converge on a location. A direction. A temple half-swallowed by the earth, where a goddess with covered eyes still weeps into the stone.*

*They have been waiting for someone to go back.*

---

**THE CHILD**

*A market in Medsen's middle quarter. 399 AA. The Southern Quake was last month — there are still cracks in some of the older walls.*

*A young adult, twenty-something, sells bread from a stall. They are unremarkable in every visible way. When business is slow they reach into their pocket and touch, without looking at it, a small folded piece of cloth with writing on it that they have carried since they were a child.*

*Someone pressed it into their hands in a crowd when they were very small. They remember the face — hollow-eyed, blood on the sleeve, moving very fast. They remember being told: keep this. Don't show anyone. Wait.*

*They have been waiting for a long time.*

*They are starting to wonder if the person who told them to wait is still alive to tell them when to stop.*

## KEY NPCs

### Aldred
Mid-ranking Caretaker, fifties. Plays a bumbling bureaucrat throughout Acts One and Two. In Act Three, drops the performance entirely — calm, certain, and at peace with what he suspects is coming. He received private visions from the Oracles for four months before this mission. He does not know the full truth. He knows enough to know the Vision Readers are lying.

**He is not a hero. He is a man who, for once, did not look away.**

**He dies instantly when the Shatter-Wing arrives.** Do not linger. Move.

### High Caretaker Kaelen
*Not present, but relevant.* The inner circle of the Caretakers knows the truth of the Beacon. Kaelen is partially blind from staring into the Beacon's heart. His severity is not cruelty — he has made the same calculation every High Caretaker has made, and he has decided it is correct. He is not a villain. He is a man who believes that slow death is preferable to no survival at all, and who has structured an entire civilization around protecting that belief.

### The Shatter-Wing Angel
The messenger of the Veiled God, corrupted by centuries in the Umbra. It does not remember its god. It does not remember itself. It guards the temple out of a compulsion it cannot name, and it perceives the Beacon's fire as a profound and personal insult. It is not evil. It is grief that has been in the dark for four hundred years.

**Use statblock:** Umbra-Touched Angel (Void Seraph), Tier 3 Solo.

## GM SECRETS — Do Not Share

- **The Beacon** consumes the souls of all living beings within its light, slowly and imperceptibly. The dead do not pass on — they become fuel. The Caretakers' Last Rites do not send souls onward; they ensure the dead are processed efficiently. **Beacon Dust is ground bone.** The currency of Brightvale is minted from its own people.
- **The Veiled God** is the deity of passage — the transition from life to death to the afterlife cycle the Gods once maintained. She was not destroyed by the Apostasy. Her divine essence somehow lingered in places of her worship. Souls that die in her temple pass on correctly.
- **The nodes** — temples and sites of Primeval Aether linked to the Veiled God's network — are waking as the Beacon dims. They are not a replacement for the Beacon. They are what existed before it, and what will remain after it fails.
- **The Oracles** have been trying to communicate this for 300 years. The party in 387 AA is the first group to ever hear them clearly. Whether the knowledge survives is entirely up to the players.
- **The next Oracle cycle** opens in **399 AA** — one year before the main campaign. The Oracles will try again. What they say will depend on whether the 387 party's seed took root.
