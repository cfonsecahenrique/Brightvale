# House Drevorn

**Motto:** *"What is held cannot be taken."*
**Status:** Recent House (Post-[[Apostasy]])
**Seat:** The Ironweight Vault — a fortified compound in [[Medsen]]'s lower merchant district, identifiable by its black iron doors etched with interlocking chain-link patterns.
**Heraldry:** A closed fist gripping a set of scales on a field of deep burgundy and grey.

---

## Origins

[[House Drevorn]] holds the rare and deeply resented distinction of having *bought* its noble patent. Before the [[Apostasy]], the Drevorn were a clan of successful but entirely unremarkable merchants and warehouse operators working out of a city long since swallowed by the Wastes. When the world collapsed, they survived not through martial valor or ancestral magic, but through something the Old Houses have always underestimated: **logistics**.

As the survivors poured into the plateau that would become Brightvale, the Drevorn were already cataloguing, rationing, and storing. While lords argued over bloodlines and guilds scrambled for territory, the Drevorn quietly became the people everyone came to when they needed something. They built the first private cold-storage vaults beneath [[Medsen]], organized the grain routes between [[Aurore]] and the capital, and — most controversially — extended lines of credit to noble houses in desperate need during the first brutal winters.

A generation later, sitting on a ledger of debts owed by half the Vale's élite and controlling more stored resources than any entity besides the Crown, the Drevorn petitioned the [[Order in the Vale|Council of Five]] for a patent of nobility. The Council, itself indebted to the family, granted it. The outcry from the Old Houses was immediate and has never fully subsided.

---

## Character & Culture

[[House Drevorn]] runs itself like a merchant company that happens to have a noble crest. Their household is meticulous and unsentimental. They keep detailed ledgers on *everything* — not just finances, but favors, alliances, slights, and debts of honor. Family members are raised to understand that **information is inventory** and that nothing, no obligation and no relationship, is without terms.

They are not cruel, but they are *precise*. They do not forget what they are owed, and they do not pretend that generosity comes without expectation. This makes them both indispensable and quietly feared. Most citizens of the Vale have interacted with a Drevorn agent without ever knowing it.

Despite their reputation for cold calculation, the Drevorn have invested heavily in [[Medsen]]'s civic infrastructure — public granaries, the lower district's water channels, a free apothecary for guild workers. They frame this as civic duty. Their rivals frame it as the longest-running reputation campaign in the Vale's history. Both are probably correct.

## Relationships

- **The Old Houses (Tensions):** [[House Von Zoltraak]], [[House Iyengar]], and others regard the Drevorn with barely concealed contempt, dismissing them as *coin-counts with a crest*. The Drevorn respond to this by quietly holding notes on their debtors and waiting.
- **The [[Emberguard]] (Cooperative):** [[House Drevorn]] supplies a significant portion of the Emberguard's non-military provisions. [[Lord Commander Varis Lugner]] maintains a strictly professional relationship with the house — useful, but careful.
- **The [[Explorers' Guild]] (Partners):** The Drevorn fund a number of expeditions into the Wastes in exchange for first-rights on any recovered Old World trade goods or currency. [[Grand Explorer Mira Ravenna]]]] considers them useful but slippery.
- **[[House Ravenna]] (Cold War):** Both houses trade in information and access, making them natural rivals. They maintain a facade of civility in public while actively working to undercut each other's networks. The current generation of Ravenna and Drevorn scions reportedly detest one another on a personal level as well.

---

## Current Standing

The current head of the house is **Corvin Drevorn**, a sharp-eyed man in his late fifties who has never raised a sword but has ended careers and bankrupted lesser houses without leaving his study. He has one public ambition: to have a Drevorn appointed to the [[Order in the Vale|Council of Five]] within his lifetime. He has been working toward this for twenty years.

His daughter, **Sela Drevorn**, runs the family's field operations — the couriers, vault-keepers, and market agents. She is considered more dangerous than her father by those who have dealt with both.

## Story Hooks

- A party member discovers they have an outstanding Drevorn debt — inherited from a deceased parent, or incurred unknowingly via a middleman.
- The Drevonn Vault contains something that predates the [[Apostasy]], recovered from the Wastes and quietly locked away. Someone wants it back.
- Corvin Drevorn approaches the party to retrieve a stolen ledger — one containing the names of every noble house currently in debt to the Drevorn. The wrong hands getting that document could destabilize the entire Vale.
