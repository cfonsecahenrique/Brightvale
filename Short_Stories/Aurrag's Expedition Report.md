Excerpt: The Thawed Silence
by Miguel Belbute

•


Year 188 FR. Storified excerpt from Slayer Aurrag's expedition report.


- The Dawnfather has forsaken us...

- Silence! Do you hear yourself speak? The Dawnfather has granted us survival this far, and we will not let that blessing go to waste.

Aurrag stood purposelessly facing the cavern's entrance.

- The snowstorm will cover our tracks. We are safe here - he ensured, with a clear hint of insecurity in his voice.

- Survival? You call this survival? You call what happened to Emilia survival?

The others continued their debate as if Aurrag's leadership was now a distant ghost howling with the wind, as if his words were quieter than the water droplets that rained from the ceiling of the mountainside hole they were stuck in.

- Get a grip on yourself! - the sorceress demanded. There was nothing we could have done to save her.

- Oh, so that's the problem, huh? Simply a number's game? Let me tell you my little fucking theory on this one: perhaps, just perhaps, the search for this stupid paperweight has blinded our leaders into believing it's worth all of our lives! Have you thought about that? Perhaps we're just cannon fodder! May-fucking-haps, the bullshit they have for brains has been royally penetrated by unrealistic promises of what a fucking lantern could bring to Emberdawn!

The knight continued his furious rant, pacing back and forward in the uneven floor. The sorceress faced her back to him and was left in solitude with her own thoughts.

- Enough! This won't bring us any good - Aurrag finally intervened.

- Not you too, Aurrag? Do you really think all's fine and dandy? That we're just gonna let the storm pass through and stroll back to Emberdawn? I mean, we definitely could... Were it on for a horde of Umbra's having had a taste of our blood! 

The knight shook his head in disbelief and continued.

- Look what they did to Emilia! Look what they did to Rakan! I doubt he'll ever... Rakan?

Only then did the sorceress turn around. Rakan, their main explorer, was hunched over himself, lying down on the hard, cool rock. You could tell that his distant eyes had just visited his childhood home, sat atop a green prairie, near an orchard where he would always walk side by side with his mother.

- Rakan... - the sorceress whispered.

She knew exactly which orchard Rakan's eyes spoke of - it too was her orchard, the trees that had seen her mature alongside her dear brother.

Aurrag looked more disappointed than anguished, for he had been the one to apply the quick medical patchwork that he could, despite recognising the wavering consciousness of the paladin.

- He... He was a good man - the knight dug for words but could only find simple syllables of generic empathy.

The sorceress, now crumbling to her knees, cried on Rakan's shoulder. 

- You know what we must do...

- No! We will not, he is my brother! - the sorceress screamed.

- And soon he will stop being him. Better than anyone, you know the rules, Cassandra - the knight now begged a quick resolve to the new argument Aurrag had just started.

- The Dawnfather will keep his light. But you know - 

- No! You will not lay a finger on him, Aurrag! Not if you want to leave here with your head still attached to your shoulders.

- The Umbra manifests quickly. The shadows will quickly consume his soul if we do not -

The knight was suddenly interrupted by the flickering of their torches and a loud gasp for air echoing throughout the cavern's pathway, which prolonged much farther than where the Venturers had decided to stop their getaway.

- Aurrag?...

- I heard it. Cassandra, pick up your staff and let's leave.

Cassandra wept ever more furiously at the idea of leaving the corpse to die two deaths: one by the Umbra, and another by the flesh-eating minions that the Umbra had summoned.

- Cassandra, you heard him. Cassandra! 

- Fuck you! I will not leave him.

The breaths coming from the dark grew closer and bolder. They could hear the wake of the void creeping with every second they took to get out of the cave.

Aurrag nodded to Jayce, who responded with an equally fearful gaze. The knight quickly grabbed the sorceress by the waist, lifting her over his shoulder and immediately started running towards the cavern's entrance. Aurrag followed after, not before taking one last look at Rakan. He unsheathed his blade, and with Cassandra's screams fueling his hesitation, he pondered whether he should let his companion meet the mercy of steel. 

- No! Let me go, you fucker! Don't you fucking touch my brother, Aurrag!

The knight struggled to keep her on top of him, but, alas, they had entered the storm's embrace, with Cassandra's pleas and Jayce's grunts being engulfed in snow.

Aurrag's hesitation was no more. He pierced his blade through Rakan's neck, twisting it - not in hatred, but instead in a cautionary movement which he deemed necessary. As he sheathed his sword and finally turned to the cave's entrance, he heard a voice.

- Aurrag... Why would you leave me?

His immediate reaction was to stiffen his duelling arm and spin back to Rakan's lifeless vessel. The torches now withered in ash, but in the dim light, Aurrag could recognise that it was not him.

- Aurrag... You left me to die. 

His eyes met with the endless void that siphoned the light - the cavern grew smaller and tighter into the deep crevice from which the breaths were first heard. Out of the void, a familiar face.

- Emilia?... 

Her eyes wept a black ooze that had already started to flood Aurrag's feet. Her mouth filled with dark smoke, as if she had burned from the inside.

- Help me, Aurrag. Don't leave me alone...

Aurrag knew what he was witnessing. Yet, he could not force himself to disbelieve that it was Emilia - his Emilia. What broke him from the dream was Cassandra's screams once again being audible amidst the blizzard, though this time they signalled physical pain.

Aurrag turned and ran outside as fast as he could. But the snowfall was already too thick to know where his companions were. He could only scream Cassandra's and Jayce's names as he ventured forth, away from the cave. Away from Emilia.

…

The expedition’s failure persistently pushed Aurrag deeper into the thick snow. Keeping any sense of direction was not only utopic but made astonishingly harder by the concoction of blood, sweat and tears which had begun to blur his eyesight. Nevertheless, his companions' tarnished screams still echoed in the wind and clawed at his mind - he pondered whether these were Umbra’s tricks or if Cassandra and Jayce were really there, just out of reach, just out of sight…

But alas, his forces were depleted - Aurrag finally succumbed to despair and fell onto his knees, the prickly ice now invading his already frostbitten fingers. He could go no further; he could walk no more. The mourning and the loss were heavier than any snow that could mitigate his progress.

As a last gesture of rebellion against the bleak winterscape, Aurrag looked up, hoping, through the mist that clouded his eyes, to spot the nostalgic beacon that protected Emberdawn’s walls. Nothing. He could see only darkness in the white that surrounded him. However, amidst that darkness, a tall figure made itself known.

He could have sworn it was another trick or a near-death vision, similar to the one Rakan must have had when he met his fate. He was hoping to see Emilia, her golden locks of hair, and greyish green eyes, the likes of which he frequently said to have never witnessed. He would have died happy thinking about her. Aurrag was ready to let go…

In a seemingly instantaneous motion, a heavy shield crashed and was buried by his side. He fell towards it and leaned on it for protection from the cold needles that had been torturing his body for innumerable hours. The shield's crevices lit up in ember and warmed his quivering soul, as the towering figure bent their knee and covered Aurrag with their cloak…

…

After this, the Aurrag only recalls waking up with the quietude of the mountainscape silencing his memory of the blizzard. An area of freshly thawed grass encircled him - a frontier from the endless snow prairies, where multiple Revenants' corpses lay half-buried in blood-soaked flakes. His heart grew older with each familiar face: Emilia, Jayce, and Cassandra rested now in the sun’s flaring rays.

His unfrosted gaze, though still unreliable, left the corpses to finally peer at the Emberdawn’s Beacon. With an endless breath, Aurrag took a knee, lifted himself up and began walking.
